# Evacuate by car

> **In short**: keep the tank at least half full and fill up as soon as an evacuation becomes possible. Leave early, one car per household, by the indicated routes, with no shortcuts and without ever crossing a flooded road. In some cases the car is a trap: a flood in progress, a tsunami, a toxic cloud, a fire close by.

## When to use it

- The authorities order or advise an evacuation.
- You decide to leave before the situation gets worse.
- Before a crisis, to prepare the car and the routes.

## What you need

- The go-bag and the car kit.
- A paper road map, with several routes marked.
- A radio, a charged phone and its charger.
- Water and food for the trip and the traffic jams.

## Steps

### Before

1. **Keep the tank at least half full** all year round. Fill up as soon as an evacuation seems possible: gas stations may close.
2. **Work out several routes** and a destination: a relative or friend, a reception center. Mark them on a paper map.
3. **Without a car**, make arrangements in advance with a relative, a friend or a neighbor, and find out what transport is planned.
4. **Maintain the car**: tires, fluid levels, battery.

### When you leave

1. **Leave early**, as soon as the instruction is given. Listen for the route, the destination and the assembly point that are announced.
2. **Take one car per household**: fewer cars, fewer traffic jams.
3. **Take** the go-bag, the radio, your medicines and your pets.
4. **Load heavy things low down**, nothing on the rear shelf.
5. **Close up the home**: doors and windows locked, appliances unplugged, gas, water and electricity shut off if the authorities ask for it.
6. **Tell a relative or friend** your destination and your route.
7. **Offer a seat** to neighbors who have no car.

### On the road

1. **Follow the indicated routes.** No shortcuts: they may be cut or dangerous.
2. **Never cross a flooded road** and do not drive around a barrier.
3. **Watch out** for washed-out roads, fallen trees and fallen power lines.
4. **Listen to the radio** to follow the instructions.
5. **Expect traffic jams**: keep water within reach and stay calm.
6. **Take a break** of at least a quarter of an hour every two hours. If you are falling asleep, stop and sleep for at least 20 minutes.

### When not to take the car

- **A flood in progress**: go upstairs, do not take to the road.
- **Tsunami**: leave on foot for high ground.
- **Toxic cloud or nuclear accident**: go into a building and wait for instructions.
- **Wildfire close by**: shelter in a solid building, do not flee at the last moment.
- **Red warning for wind or snow**: stay at home.

## Never do this

- Wait until the last moment to leave.
- Leave in several cars.
- Take a shortcut off the indicated routes.
- Cross a flooded road.
- Come back before the authorities allow it.

## Without equipment

- No car: tell the local authority or the emergency services that you need transport, and go to the assembly point that was announced.
- No GPS or no network: the paper map and the road signs are enough.

## Depending on where you are

- **With an elderly, sick or disabled person**: plan in advance who drives and how to take their medical equipment.
- **With pets**: in a carrier or on a leash, with their kit (see [Look after your pets](../at-home/look-after-your-pets.md)).
- **In winter**: blankets, warm clothes, snow chains or snow socks.
