# Thunderstorm, high winds, wildfire or earthquake while driving

> **In short**: slow down and stop in an open place, away from trees, power lines, bridges and streams. In a thunderstorm, in high winds or during an earthquake, stay in the car. Faced with a wildfire, a toxic cloud or a tornado, the car does not protect you: get to a solid building.

## When to use it

- You are driving and the weather turns violent: thunderstorm, hail, gusts, heavy rain.
- The ground shakes while you are driving.
- You see smoke or flames at the roadside.
- An alert reaches your phone or the radio during the trip.

## Steps

### In every case

1. **Slow down.**
2. **Do not read an alert while driving**: stop somewhere safe first, or listen to the radio.
3. **Choose where to stop**: an open place, away from trees, power lines, bridges and streams.
4. **If you can reach a solid building**, it is almost always the best shelter.

### Thunderstorm and hail

1. **Stay in the car**, windows closed: an enclosed vehicle with a metal roof protects you from lightning.
2. **Do not touch the metal parts.**
3. **Park away from trees and power lines** that could fall.
4. **Under a red warning**: drive at walking pace, do not go onto a flooded road, park somewhere safe and stay in the vehicle.
5. **Wait 30 minutes** after the last thunder before you get out.

### High winds

1. **Slow down**: gusts push the car sideways.
2. **Stop away from trees and power lines.**
3. **Stay away from wires that have fallen** on the road.
4. **Under a red warning**, you do not take the car: if you have already left, get to a nearby solid building and shelter inside.

### Wildfire

1. **Do not take to the road to flee at the last moment**, and do not drive through smoke.
2. **Get to a solid building** if you can: a car is not a shelter, it can burn.
3. **If the flames catch you in the car**: stop in an open area and do not get out.
4. **Call the emergency services**, give your position and turn on your lights so that you can be seen.

### Earthquake

1. **Stop** away from buildings, trees, bridges and power lines.
2. **Stay in the car** until the shaking stops.
3. **Set off again carefully**: avoid bridges and ramps, which may have been damaged.
4. **Near the sea**, leave the shore on foot and get to high ground as soon as the shaking stops.

### Tornado

1. **Do not try to outrun it.** If it is still far away, in open country, drive at right angles to its path, toward a solid building.
2. **If it is on you and there is no shelter**: either stay in the car, crouched down, head covered; or get out and lie flat in a ditch, protecting your head.
3. **Never shelter under a bridge.**

### Toxic cloud or nuclear accident

1. **Park and go into the nearest building**: a vehicle does not protect you.

## Never do this

- Stop under a tree, under a bridge or under a power line.
- Go onto a flooded road (see [Caught in a flood in your car](caught-in-a-flood-in-your-car.md)).
- Flee a fire by car through the smoke.
- Get out of the car during the thunderstorm or the shaking.
- Read or write a message while driving.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a forest in high winds**: minor roads there are the most affected by falling trees. Postpone the trip or change your route.
- **In a tunnel**: see [Vehicle fire or fire in a tunnel](vehicle-fire-or-fire-in-a-tunnel.md).
- **In France**: the red and orange warnings are the Météo-France "vigilance" levels.

This guide brings together the "in a car" instructions of the disaster guides. For wildfire, two instructions complete each other: the French Ministry for the Ecological Transition says not to take refuge in a car, the French civil protection service says to stop in an open area without getting out if the flames catch you. For tornadoes, the actions come from the US National Weather Service and the Government of Alberta.
