# Put together a car kit

> **In short**: a high-visibility vest within reach and a warning triangle in the trunk come first. Add water, something to eat, a blanket, a flashlight, a first aid kit, a phone charger and a paper road map. In winter: an ice scraper, warm clothes, snow chains or snow socks. And keep the tank at least half full.

## When to use it

- Now, then at each change of season and before a long trip.
- Before a warning for snow, extreme cold or a heat wave.
- The car is also an emergency shelter and a way to evacuate: its kit completes the go-bag.

## What you need

### All year round

- **High-visibility vest**, inside the car, within reach: you must be able to put it on before you get out.
- **Warning triangle.**
- **Flashlight** and spare batteries.
- **First aid kit** and a survival blanket.
- **Water** and food that keeps: bars, dried fruit, crackers.
- **Car phone charger** and its cable. A power bank if possible.
- **Paper road map** of the area.
- **Spare wheel** and the tools to change it, jumper cables.
- **Your medicines** if you are on a treatment.
- **A little cash.**

### In winter

- Ice scraper, antifreeze windshield washer fluid.
- Blankets or a sleeping bag, warm clothes, gloves, a hat, spare socks.
- Snow chains or snow socks, fitted once before you need them.
- A hot drink in a thermos for long trips.
- A small shovel and some sand, to free a wheel.

### In summer or for a remote area

- A lot more water: South Australia Police count 6 liters (1.6 gallons) per person per day in mild weather, 10 liters (2.6 gallons) in extreme heat.
- Hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, light clothes that cover you.
- Something to make shade: an umbrella or a tarp.
- Water for the radiator.

## Steps

1. **Gather the kit** in a bag or a crate, wedged in the trunk. The vest stays inside the car.
2. **Check the car** before a long trip or a cold spell: tires (condition, pressure, spare wheel), oil, coolant and washer fluid levels, brakes, lights, battery, wipers.
3. **Keep the tank at least half full**, and fill up before a forecast storm or if an evacuation is possible.
4. **Charge your phone** before you leave.
5. **Tell someone** where you are going, which way and when you will arrive, especially in winter or in a remote area.
6. **Check the kit twice a year**: dates of the water, the food and the first aid kit, batteries, pressure of the spare wheel.
7. **Replace right away** what you use.

## Never do this

- Keep the vest in the trunk: you have to put it on before you get out of the car.
- Leave heavy objects on the rear shelf: in a crash, they become projectiles.
- Keep a can of gasoline inside the car.
- Leave medicines in the car in midsummer or in extreme cold.
- Count on the phone alone to guide you and to call for help.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: the law requires a CE-marked high-visibility vest within reach and an approved warning triangle. Elsewhere, check the rules of the country you drive in.
- **In the French mountains**: from November 1 to March 31, winter tires, snow chains or snow socks are mandatory in some municipalities of the mountain ranges. Keep the chains or the socks in the trunk.
- **With a baby or a pet**: diapers, milk, water, leash and bowl.
- **If you drive little**: the kit ages all the same. Check it on the same dates as the go-bag.
