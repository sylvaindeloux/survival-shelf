# Car in the water

> **In short**: act in the first seconds, while the car is still afloat. Seatbelts off, open a window or break a side window, get out at once, children first. Do not wait for the car to fill with water and do not phone before you are out.

## When to use it

- The car leaves the road and ends up in a river, a canal, a lake or a harbor.
- It is swept away by a flood.
- You see a car go into the water.

## What you need

- A window-breaking tool, kept within the driver's reach: on the keychain, in the door or in the center console. Not in the glove box or the trunk.
- Failing that, a heavy object within reach, such as a large flashlight.

## Steps

### You are in the car

1. **Act at once, without panicking.** A car floats for a few seconds to a few minutes: that is the time to get out.
2. **Unfasten all the seatbelts.** Also unbuckle the children who cannot do it themselves.
3. **Open the windows.** Electric windows still work for a short while.
4. **The window will not open**: break a side window with the window-breaking tool, striking in a bottom corner. Without a tool, try with your foot, your shoulder or a heavy object.
5. **Get out through the window or the sunroof**, children and those who need help first. Push the youngest out, then follow them.
6. **Take nothing with you.**
7. **Once out of the water**, call the emergency services.

### No window opens or breaks

1. **Keep trying** the door handle and the windows.
2. **As the very last resort**: when the car is almost full, the pressure evens out and the door may open. Take a last breath, open it and get out.
3. **Do not count on it**: by then there is very little air left and the risk of drowning is extremely high. It is never a plan, only what is left when everything else has failed.

### You are a witness

1. **Call the emergency services** and say exactly where you are.
2. **Shout the steps** to the people inside: seatbelts, windows, out.
3. **Do not put yourself in danger**: do not go into the water (see [Drowning](../health-and-first-aid/drowning.md)).

### Afterward

1. **Get everyone dry and warm**: take off wet clothes, cover up (see [Hypothermia and frostbite](../health-and-first-aid/hypothermia-and-frostbite.md)).
2. **Have a doctor check** anyone who swallowed water, coughs or has trouble breathing.

## Never do this

- Wait for the car to fill with water before opening the door when a window can still be opened or broken.
- Phone before you are out.
- Strike the windshield: it is usually made of laminated glass, which window-breaking tools cannot break.
- Look for your belongings.
- Count on an air pocket at the bottom of the water.

## Without equipment

- No window-breaking tool: everything depends on speed. Seatbelts and windows in the first seconds.
- A heavy object within reach, such as a large flashlight or a steering wheel lock, can be used to strike the window.

## Depending on where you are

- **With children**: explain the plan in advance and practice it as a family, with the car parked. Children who are able to unbuckle themselves get out on their own through the open window.
- **Laminated side windows**: some cars have them, and a tool will not break them. Check in the car's manual or with the manufacturer.
- **Deep water**: a front-engine car sinks nose first and may turn over. One more reason to get out while it floats.
- **Flooded road**: see [Caught in a flood in your car](caught-in-a-flood-in-your-car.md).

This guide relies on police, fire and lifesaving services in Canada, Australia and the United States: no French official page on a car in the water was found at the time of writing. They differ on one point: the Phoenix and Palm Beach County fire departments describe opening the door once the pressure has evened out, whereas the Royal Life Saving Society calls this a myth, with an extremely high risk of drowning. This guide keeps the most cautious line: out through the window at once, and the door only as the very last resort. Common sense, with no official source: the "Afterward" section.
