# Stuck in snow or cold

> **In short**: stay in the car, it is your shelter. Leave it only when the authorities tell you to, or if help is visible close by. Run the engine for about 10 minutes every hour, with a window slightly open and the exhaust pipe clear of snow. Cover up, move, make the car visible.

## When to use it

- A road cut by snow or ice, a traffic jam that no longer moves in extreme cold.
- A car that has broken down or left the road in winter.
- Before you leave, when a warning for snow, ice or extreme cold has been issued.

## What you need

- Blankets or a sleeping bag, warm dry clothes, gloves, a hat.
- Water, food, a hot drink.
- A charged phone, its charger, a flashlight.
- An ice scraper, snow chains or snow socks.
- A brightly colored cloth.

## Steps

### Before you leave

1. **Check** the weather, the warnings and the state of the roads. If snow or extreme cold is forecast, postpone your trip if you can.
2. **Equip the car** and fill the tank (see [Put together a car kit](put-together-a-car-kit.md)).
3. **Tell someone** where you are going, which way and when you expect to arrive.

### You are stuck

1. **Stay in the slow lane** or park on a safe shoulder off the road. Do not stop on a motorway hard shoulder unless you are told to.
2. **Stay in the car.** Leave it only when the authorities tell you to, or if help is visible within about a hundred meters (a hundred yards).
3. **Listen to the radio** and follow the message signs and the instructions of the road crews.
4. **Call the emergency services** if someone is unwell or if you are isolated, and give your position first.

### Keep the heat in

1. **Run the engine and the heater for about 10 minutes every hour**, no more. Before each start, check that the exhaust pipe is not blocked by snow.
2. **Open a window slightly** on the side sheltered from the wind while the engine is running.
3. **Cover your whole body**, head included: clothes, blankets, and failing that newspapers, road maps or floor mats.
4. **Huddle together.**
5. **Move** your arms, legs and fingers, clap your hands, without tiring yourself.
6. **Drink.** Do not eat snow and avoid alcohol.
7. **Stay awake.** If the wait is long, take turns sleeping, with the engine off.

### Make yourself visible

1. **Tie a brightly colored cloth** to the antenna or to a window.
2. **Turn on the interior light** while the engine is running.
3. **Raise the hood** once the snow has stopped falling.
4. **Sound the horn or flash the headlights in series of three** if you hear or see someone.

## Never do this

- Set off on foot in the storm.
- Leave the engine running all the time, or fall asleep with the engine on: carbon monoxide builds up without you noticing.
- Run the engine when the exhaust pipe is buried in snow.
- Push the car or shovel with all your strength: effort in the cold can trigger a heart attack.
- Pass a salt spreader or a snowplow.

## Without equipment

- No blanket: newspapers, road maps and floor mats insulate from the cold.
- No bright cloth: hood raised, and interior light on while the engine is running.

## Depending on where you are

- **On a French motorway**: listen to 107.7 FM and follow the message signs.
- **Headache, nausea, dizziness, great tiredness in the car**: think of carbon monoxide. Switch off the engine, open the windows and the doors, breathe fresh air (see [Carbon monoxide poisoning](../health-and-first-aid/carbon-monoxide-poisoning.md)).
- **Shivering, slurred speech, confusion, drowsiness**: this may be hypothermia (see [Hypothermia and frostbite](../health-and-first-aid/hypothermia-and-frostbite.md)).
