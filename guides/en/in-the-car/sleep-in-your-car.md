# Sleep in your car

> **In short**: park somewhere safe, switch off the engine before you sleep and cover up instead of running the heater. An engine idling in a parked car can fill it with carbon monoxide, a deadly gas you cannot smell. In hot weather, do not sleep in a closed car.

## When to use it

- You are falling asleep at the wheel: a nap is better than driving on.
- A closed road, an endless traffic jam, a breakdown: the night will be spent in the car.
- An evacuation with nowhere to stay for the night.

## What you need

- Blankets or a sleeping bag, warm clothes, a hat.
- Water.
- A flashlight and a charged phone.

## Steps

### Choose where to stop

1. **Get out of the traffic**: a rest area, a service area, a parking lot. Not on the hard shoulder of a motorway, unless you are told to.
2. **In high winds**, not under trees or under a bridge.
3. **In heavy rain**, not along a stream or on a riverbank.
4. **In dry weather**, not on dry or tall grass.
5. **Never with the engine running in a garage**, even with the door open.
6. **Tell someone** where you have stopped.

### Before you sleep

1. **Switch off the engine.**
2. **Cover your whole body**, head included: clothes, blankets, sleeping bag.
3. **Huddle together** if there are several of you.
4. **Let fresh air in** from time to time by opening a window.

### In extreme cold

1. **Heat in short bursts, awake**: engine and heater for about 10 minutes every hour, a window slightly open, the exhaust pipe clear of snow.
2. **Take turns sleeping**: someone is always awake.
3. **Move** your arms, legs and fingers from time to time.
4. **Watch for signs of hypothermia**: shivering, slurred speech, confusion, drowsiness.

### In hot weather

1. **Do not sleep in a closed car**: its temperature can rise by about 11 °C (20 °F) in 10 minutes, and cracking a window open makes almost no difference.
2. **Park in the shade, all windows open.** If the car stays too hot, settle outside, in the shade, away from the traffic.
3. **Drink regularly.**
4. **Drive in the cool hours** rather than in the heat of the day.

### A nap against drowsy driving

1. **Spot the signs**: yawning, heavy eyelids, nodding off for a moment.
2. **Stop and sleep for at least 20 minutes.** You cannot fight sleep.
3. **Take a break** of at least a quarter of an hour every two hours.

## Never do this

- Sleep with the engine running, heater or air conditioning on.
- Run the engine in a garage.
- Heat the inside of the car with a camping stove or charcoal.
- Leave a child or a pet alone in the car, even asleep, even in the shade.
- Drink alcohol to warm up.

## Without equipment

- No blanket: put on all your clothes, and slip newspapers, road maps or the floor mats against your body.

## Depending on where you are

- **Headache, dizziness, nausea, great weakness**: think of carbon monoxide. Switch off the engine, open everything, get out into fresh air and call the emergency services.
- **If you sleep in your car because you have nowhere to live**: call the local helpline for homeless people. In France, it is 115.
- **On a motorway**: stop at a rest or service area, never on the hard shoulder to sleep.
