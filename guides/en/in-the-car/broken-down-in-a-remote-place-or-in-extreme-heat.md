# Broken down in a remote place or in extreme heat

> **In short**: stay near the car, it is far easier to find than a person on foot. Raise the hood, get into the shade and drink. If the inside of the car gets too hot, everyone gets out into the shade, away from traffic. Before you leave, tell someone where you are going and take a lot of water.

## When to use it

- A breakdown or a stranded car far from everything: a track, a small mountain road, an area with no phone signal.
- A breakdown or a traffic jam in extreme heat.
- Before a trip in a remote area or during a heat wave.

## What you need

- Water: 6 liters (1.6 gallons) per person per day in mild weather, 10 liters (2.6 gallons) in extreme heat, and a reserve for 3 to 4 days in a very remote area.
- Wide-brimmed hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, light loose clothes.
- An umbrella or a tarp to make shade.
- Charged phone, paper road map, flashlight, first aid kit, your medicines.
- Water for the radiator, tools, jumper cables, a spare wheel.

## Steps

### Before you leave

1. **Give your route** to someone you trust, with your destination, your arrival time and the times when you will check in.
2. **Check the car**: fluid levels, coolant, tire pressure, air conditioning, battery.
3. **Keep the tank at least three-quarters full** and plan where to refuel: in these conditions, the car uses more fuel.
4. **Do not count on mobile coverage.** In a truly remote area, a satellite phone or a distress beacon is the only sure link.

### You break down

1. **Get out of the traffic lanes** if you can, raise the hood and turn on the hazard lights.
2. **Call the emergency services right away** if you have a signal. Give your position.
3. **Stay near the car.** It can be seen from far away and that is where people will look for you.
4. **Signal** when you see or hear someone: horn or headlight flashes in series of three, a brightly colored cloth on the antenna or at a window.

### Hold out in extreme heat

1. **Park in the shade** if you can still drive, and keep the air conditioning on as long as it works.
2. **Without air conditioning, open all the windows.**
3. **If the inside of the car gets too hot**, get everyone out, pets included, and settle in the shade, as far from the traffic as possible.
4. **Have everyone drink**, regularly, without waiting to be thirsty.
5. **Cover up**: a hat, light loose clothes.
6. **Keep your shoes on** and carry pets: the road surface can burn the skin.

### Leave on foot only if

1. You know where you are and where you are going.
2. You are sure you can reach water, shelter or help.
3. Or you are convinced that nobody will come.

In that case, leave a note on the car: your destination, your route, your condition and what you are taking.

## Never do this

- Leave the car to walk at random.
- Open the radiator cap while the engine is hot.
- Leave a child, a vulnerable person or a pet alone in the car, even with a window cracked open, even in the shade.
- Park or drive on dry or tall grass: the car can set it on fire.
- Set off a distress beacon when there is no real distress.

## Without equipment

- No shade: open the doors and stretch a cloth or a survival blanket to make some next to the car.
- No bright cloth: hood raised, hazard lights, signals in series of three.

## Depending on where you are

- **A stopped car heats up fast**: its temperature can rise by about 11 °C (20 °F) in 10 minutes. Cracking a window open makes almost no difference.
- **Very remote area**: South Australia Police advise two spare wheels and a satellite phone or a beacon. Register your 406 MHz beacon before you leave.
- **In the mountains or in winter**: see [Stuck in snow or cold](stuck-in-snow-or-cold.md).
