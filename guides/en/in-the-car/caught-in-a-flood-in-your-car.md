# Caught in a flood in your car

> **In short**: never drive onto a flooded road, even if the water looks shallow: turn around. Less than 30 cm (12 in) of water is enough to carry a car away. If the water rises around the stopped car, leave it while you can still do so safely and get to higher ground. Never go down into an underground parking garage.

## When to use it

- Heavy rain, a violent thunderstorm or a river in flood: the road ahead of you is covered with water.
- A warning for rain, flooding or thunderstorms when you have to drive.
- Water is rising around your car.

## Steps

### Before you set off

1. **Postpone your trip**: during a flood, the instruction is not to take the car.
2. **Check** the warnings and which roads are closed.
3. **Do not go and fetch your children from school**: the staff shelter them there.
4. **Do not park** on a riverbank, on a bridge or along a stream when heavy rain is forecast.

### The road ahead is flooded

1. **Stop and turn around.** The water may be deeper than it looks and may hide a washed-out road or power lines.
2. **Never drive around a barrier** that closes a road.
3. **Do not drive** onto a ford or a low-water bridge, into an underpass or a tunnel, or onto a bridge over fast-moving water.
4. **Remember these figures**: 15 cm (6 in) of fast-moving water knocks an adult over, 30 cm (12 in) carries a car away, 45 cm (18 in) carries away a large vehicle.
5. **Get to higher ground** and wait for the water to go down.

### You are driving in a violent thunderstorm

1. **Drive at walking pace.**
2. **Park somewhere safe**, on high ground, away from trees and streams.
3. **Stay in the car** as long as the water is not rising around it.

### The water is rising around the car

1. **The car is stopped and the water is rising**: leave it and get to higher ground, without crossing moving water.
2. **You are trapped by fast-moving water**: climb to the highest point you can reach and call the emergency services.
3. **The car is swept away or falls into the water**: see [Car in the water](car-in-the-water.md).

### Afterward

1. **Do not go back into moving water**, even to recover the car.
2. **Drive carefully**: roads may be washed out and power lines down.

## Never do this

- Go onto a flooded road, on foot or by car.
- Drive around a barrier.
- Go down into an underground parking garage, for example to save your car.
- Park on a riverbank or on a bridge to watch the flood.
- Go and fetch your children from school during the alert.

## Depending on where you are

- **Underpass, sunken road, tunnel**: these are the first places to fill up. Take another route.
- **Ford or low-water bridge**: do not cross as soon as water covers the road.
- **In France**: follow the Météo-France and Vigicrues warnings, and the instructions of the town hall and the prefecture.

The French official instructions read say not to take the car and, in a thunderstorm under a red warning, to park safely and stay in the vehicle: they do not describe the case where water rises around the car. The advice to leave it then for higher ground comes from an AccuWeather article republished by CTIF, the International Association of Fire and Rescue Services, which is not an authority. Common sense, with no official source: the remark that underpasses fill up first.
