# Breakdown or accident on the road

> **In short**: turn on the hazard lights, stop as far from the traffic lanes as you can, put on the high-visibility vest before you get out, have everyone leave on the side away from traffic and wait behind the safety barrier. On a motorway: no triangle and no repairs, call from the roadside emergency phone. At an accident: protect, call for help, give first aid.

## When to use it

- Your car breaks down, has a flat tire or loses power while you are driving.
- You have an accident, or you come upon one.
- You are stopped on a motorway, an expressway or in a tunnel.

## What you need

- A high-visibility vest inside the car, within reach.
- A warning triangle.
- A charged phone.

## Steps

### The car is failing

1. **Turn on the hazard lights.**
2. **Leave the road if you can**: next exit, rest area or service area.
3. **If not, stop as far from the traffic as possible**, on the hard shoulder or at the side of the road, with the wheels turned away from the traffic.

### Get everyone to safety

1. **Put on the vest before you get out.**
2. **Get out on the side away from the traffic.**
3. **Go behind the safety barrier** and move away from the car and the lanes. Keep children close to you.
4. **Leave pets in the car.**
5. **Do not go back to the car**, even if it is raining, cold or dark.
6. **You cannot get out safely**: stay belted in, hazard lights on, and call the emergency services at once.

### Warn and call

1. **On an ordinary road**: put the triangle well behind the car, if you can do it without putting yourself in danger.
2. **On a motorway**: do not put out a triangle. Walk behind the barrier to the nearest roadside emergency phone and call: the operator sends the breakdown service.
3. **No emergency phone nearby**: call the emergency number.
4. **If you phone at the roadside**, face the traffic so that you see vehicles coming.

### At an accident

1. **Protect.** Turn on your hazard lights, put on your vest and warn of the accident with the triangle if you can do it safely. Go closer only if it is safe for you.
2. **Call for help.** Say where you are, what happened, how many people are hurt and whether there is still a danger, such as a fire.
3. **Give first aid**: talk to the injured, cover them, and move them only if an immediate danger threatens them (see [Protect, call for help, give first aid](../health-and-first-aid/protect-call-for-help-give-first-aid.md)).
4. **On a motorway**, if you are a witness and not involved, drive to the nearest roadside emergency phone and raise the alarm.

### In a tunnel

1. **Hazard lights on, stop as far to the side as possible**, in an emergency bay if you can, then switch off the ignition.
2. **Vest on before you get out**, triangle if the car sticks out into the roadway.
3. **Walk to the nearest emergency station**, staying on the sidewalk, and call from there. Follow the operator's instructions.
4. **Smoke or fire**: see [Vehicle fire or fire in a tunnel](vehicle-fire-or-fire-in-a-tunnel.md).

## Never do this

- Stay seated in a car stopped at the edge of the lanes when you can get out.
- Get out on the traffic side, or wait near the car.
- Repair the car or change a wheel on a motorway.
- Put out a triangle on a motorway, cross the lanes or try to stop other vehicles.
- Make a U-turn or reverse in a tunnel.

## Without equipment

- Triangle missing or impossible to place safely: keep the hazard lights on.
- Phone with no signal or no battery: on a motorway, the roadside emergency phone works without it.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: the triangle goes at least 30 m (100 ft) from the car, and the law does not require you to place it if that puts your life in danger. On motorways, the emergency phones are orange call boxes; in tunnels there is one every 200 m (650 ft).
- **French concession motorways** (usually toll roads): only an approved breakdown company is allowed to work there. Called from the orange call box, it must arrive within 30 minutes, at a regulated rate. It repairs a minor fault on the spot, otherwise it tows the car to its garage or, if you ask, to a place less than 5 km (3 miles) from the motorway exit.
- **At night**: leave the parking lights on as well.
- **In extreme cold or heat**: waiting behind the barrier becomes a risk. See [Stuck in snow or cold](stuck-in-snow-or-cold.md) and [Broken down in a remote place or in extreme heat](broken-down-in-a-remote-place-or-in-extreme-heat.md).
