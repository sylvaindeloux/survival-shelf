# Epidemic and pandemic

> **In short**: wash your hands often, air your home, wear a mask in enclosed places or as soon as you have symptoms, stay home when you are ill and keep your distance from vulnerable people. Follow the instructions of the health authorities and the vaccination recommendations. Trouble breathing or a condition that gets worse: call the emergency services.

## When to use it

- A seasonal epidemic (flu, COVID-19) or a new disease that is spreading.
- The health authorities announce measures: masks, fewer contacts, closures.
- A virus can spread from person to person, through objects, and from people who have no symptoms.

## What you need

- Soap and hand sanitizer (at least 60 % alcohol).
- Masks and single-use tissues.
- Your usual medicines and your prescriptions.
- Supplies of water, food and hygiene products for several days.

## Before

1. **Build your supplies little by little**, so that everyone can find what they need.
2. **Keep your vaccinations up to date** according to the current recommendations, especially if you are vulnerable or live with a vulnerable person.
3. **Plan for closures**: childcare, working from home, remote appointments with your doctor.
4. **Organize help** for elderly, sick or isolated relatives: who does their shopping, who checks on them.

## During

1. **Wash your hands often**, with soap and water, or with hand sanitizer. Avoid touching your eyes, nose and mouth.
2. **Air** the rooms several times a day, for a few minutes, even when it is cold.
3. **Wear a mask** as soon as you have symptoms, and during an epidemic in enclosed or very busy places.
4. **Cough and sneeze into your elbow** or into a single-use tissue, throw it away at once, then wash your hands.
5. **Keep your distance**: at least 2 m (6 ft) from people who may be contagious. Greet without contact.
6. **If you are ill, stay home** and limit your contacts, especially with older people, pregnant women, infants and people with a weakened immune system.
7. **Clean regularly** the surfaces everyone touches: handles, switches, phones.
8. **Protect the most vulnerable**: avoid taking an infant to very busy places, and keep in touch by phone with relatives you cannot visit.
9. **If you have been exposed or have symptoms**, contact your doctor and follow the isolation instructions.
10. **Get your information from the health authorities** and pass on only information that has been checked.

### When to call

- **The emergency services**: a life-threatening emergency.
- **Your doctor or pharmacist**: symptoms that last, a treatment in progress, a vulnerable person.

## After

1. **Keep the good habits** as long as the virus is still circulating.
2. **Restock** masks, soap and medicines.
3. **Look after the morale** of your household: isolation and worry weigh on everyone (see [Shock, stress and supporting others](../health-and-first-aid/shock-stress-and-supporting-others.md)).

## Never do this

- Visit a vulnerable person when you have a fever or a cough.
- Stop a usual treatment without medical advice.
- Rush to the stores or pharmacies to stockpile.
- Follow or pass on a "remedy" seen on social media without the advice of a health professional.

## Depending on where you are

- **In contact with animals** during a bird flu outbreak: wash your hands after every contact.
- **Without running water**: see [Wash your hands without running water](../hygiene/wash-your-hands-without-running-water.md).
- **In France**: call 15 for a life-threatening emergency, or to be directed when your doctor cannot be reached.
