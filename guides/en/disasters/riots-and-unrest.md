# Riots and unrest

> **In short**: keep away. Do not go closer, do not film, and leave the area as soon as you can do it safely. Go home and stay there, with the door locked and the shutters closed, following official information. Caught in tear gas: get out of the cloud, find fresh air and rinse your eyes with water.

## When to use it

- A demonstration that turns violent, riots, looting, clashes in your neighborhood or on your route.
- A curfew or a travel ban announced by the authorities.
- For armed violence that lasts, see [Civil war](civil-war.md).

## Before

1. **Follow local and international news**: unrest often breaks out around elections or the anniversary of major events.
2. **Find out which neighborhoods and places are most exposed** and avoid them.
3. **Keep a few days of supplies**: water, food, medicines. Stores and public transport may close.
4. **Protect your home**: a reliable lock, shutters, automatic lighting.

## During

### You are outside

1. **Do not get involved**, even if you agree with the demonstrators. A peaceful gathering can turn violent without warning.
2. **Do not take photos or videos**: being there, watching or filming may be enough for you to be taken for a participant.
3. **Leave the area** as soon as you can do it safely.
4. **Change your plans**: transport may be disrupted. Let your relatives know you are safe with a short message.

### You are at home

1. **Stay inside.** Lock the door and close the shutters.
2. **Keep informed** by radio and through the official accounts of the local authorities. Be wary of rumors.
3. **Follow the instructions of the authorities**, curfew included.
4. **Do not open the door** to someone you do not know.
5. **Call the police or the emergency number** if people or property are threatened, and the fire service if there is a fire.

### Tear gas

1. **Get out of the cloud** and into fresh air. Outdoors, move to higher ground: the gas settles near the ground. Indoors, leave the building.
2. **Rinse your eyes with plain water** for 10 to 15 minutes if they burn or if your vision is blurred. Take out contact lenses and do not put them back in.
3. **Wash your skin** with plenty of soap and water.
4. **Take off the affected clothes**, cutting them off rather than pulling them over your head, and seal them in a plastic bag.
5. The effects usually pass 15 to 30 minutes after you have left the cloud and washed. **If you have trouble breathing or the signs last**, get medical care.

### You are assaulted or robbed

- Do not resist: your life matters more than your belongings.
- Shout for help.
- Remember what the attacker looks like and tell the police as soon as you are safe.

## After

1. **Keep avoiding the affected places** until calm has returned.
2. **If your home or your car is damaged**: touch nothing, take photos, file a complaint and report the damage to your insurer.
3. **Check on** your neighbors.

## Never do this

- Go and see "what is going on".
- Film or photograph the clashes.
- Step in, or defend a store or a car against a group.
- Go out during a curfew.
- Pass on a rumor.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a car**: turn around before you reach a crowd.
- **Traveling abroad**: read your country's travel advice and follow the instructions of the local authorities.
- **In France**: call 17 or 112 for the police, 18 for the fire service, 15 for medical help.
