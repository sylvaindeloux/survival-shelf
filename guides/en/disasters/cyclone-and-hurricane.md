# Cyclone and hurricane

> **In short**: get ready from the first alert level, be home or in a shelter before the red alert, then stay in until the alert is lifted, even during the calm of the eye. After the storm, fallen wires, flooded river crossings and tap water are still dangerous.

## When to use it

- You live or travel in a part of the world hit by cyclones, hurricanes or typhoons.
- A cyclone alert is announced.
- Every territory has its own alert system: learn yours. This guide describes the one used on Réunion Island (France) as an example.

## What you need

- Supplies: water, canned food, medicines, batteries for the radio and the flashlights.
- A battery radio.
- Something to protect doors and windows: shutters, boards.
- The address of the nearest public shelter and useful phone numbers.

## Before

### At the start of the season

1. Find the nearest public shelter.
2. Check your supplies and your emergency kit.
3. Prune the trees, clean gutters and drains.

### Pre-alert: "I keep informed" (possible threat within 24 to 72 hours)

1. Follow the forecasts.
2. Avoid long mountain hikes and trips at sea of more than 24 hours. Keep off the shore in a heavy swell.
3. Check your supplies: canned food, water, batteries, medicines.
4. Kidney or respiratory failure: contact your usual care center without waiting.

### Orange alert: "I get ready" (danger within 24 hours)

1. Bring in anything the wind can carry away, and your animals.
2. Protect doors and windows: shutters, boards.
3. Prepare for a possible evacuation. Schools and day-care centers close.

## During

1. **Be indoors before the red alert.** On Réunion Island it is announced 3 hours ahead: use that time to reach your home or a shelter. After that, all traffic is banned.
2. **Stay confined** in a solid building, in an interior room, away from doors and glass windows.
3. **Do not go out during the eye of the cyclone**: the calm does not last and the winds come back suddenly.
4. **Phone only when necessary.**
5. **If the water rises**, go upstairs, but do not shut yourself in an attic with no opening.
6. **Under a purple alert** (winds above 200 km/h, about 125 mph), nobody goes out, not even the emergency services.
7. **Wait for the alert to be lifted** before going out.

## After

During the recovery phase the threat moves away but the dangers remain: floods, mudflows, wires on the ground, blocked roads.

1. **Do not cross** submerged fords or gullies in flood.
2. **Do not touch** fallen wires.
3. **Be wary of tap water**: prefer bottled water or treat the water before you drink it. On Réunion Island it may stay unfit to drink for at least 48 hours after the rain stops.
4. **Take the car** only if it is necessary and allowed.
5. **Keep the phone lines free** and do not get in the way of the emergency services.
6. **Throw away food** that got wet or was no longer kept cold.
7. **Pace yourself while cleaning up**: take breaks, wear gloves, goggles and boots.

## Never do this

- Go out during the red alert, even "just to have a look".
- Believe it is all over during the eye of the cyclone.
- Cross a ford or a gully in flood.
- Run a generator indoors.

## Depending on where you are

- **Light building, trailer, mobile home**: they do not withstand these winds. Get to a solid building or a public shelter before the red alert.
- **On the coast**: the sea can rise within minutes. If the authorities ask you to evacuate, leave at once.
- **Far from the coast**: a hurricane can still cause damage more than 160 km (100 miles) inland.
