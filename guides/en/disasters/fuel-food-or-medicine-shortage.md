# Fuel, food or medicine shortage

> **In short**: a shortage gets worse when everyone rushes to the shelves or the pumps. Build your reserves beforehand, little by little. During the shortage, buy only what you need and cut down on trips. Never stop a treatment on your own: your pharmacist can often suggest an equivalent medicine.

## When to use it

- A strike, a blockade, a breakdown or a crisis disrupts supplies to gas stations, stores or pharmacies.
- Empty shelves, lines, rationed sales.

## What you need

- A reserve of water and food for several days, which you rotate.
- Your usual treatment with a little in hand, and a copy of your prescriptions.
- A car tank that is at least half full.

## Before

1. **Build your reserve little by little**, buying a bit more on each shopping trip, so that you do not empty the shelves on the day everyone worries.
2. **Choose food that keeps** and can be eaten without cooking. Rotate it so that it does not expire.
3. **Keep the tank at least half full.**
4. **Talk to your doctor or pharmacist** about your reserve of medicines, and renew your prescription without waiting for the last box (see [Get through a crisis with a medical treatment](../health-and-first-aid/get-through-a-crisis-with-a-medical-treatment.md)).

## During

### Fuel

1. **Do not rush to the pump** if your tank is not empty: in October 2022, the prefecture of the Jura department (France) noted that stations ran dry because drivers flocked to them, while deliveries remained regular.
2. **Follow the orders of the authorities**: they may, for example, ban the sale of fuel in jerricans.
3. **Cut down on trips**: combine them, share a car, walk or cycle when you can.
4. **Keep what you have left** for essential trips: care, work, people to help.

### Food

1. **Buy only what you need** for a few days.
2. **Eat first** what spoils quickly, then draw on your reserve.
3. **Check on** elderly or isolated neighbors and share if you can.

### Medicines

1. **Do not stop or change your treatment** without medical advice: stopping suddenly can be dangerous.
2. **Ask your pharmacist**: when supplies are tight, they can often replace the missing medicine with a similar one, and they inform your doctor.
3. **Finish the box you have** before moving on to the replacement.
4. **Contact your doctor** if you are allergic to the replacement, or if you notice unusual symptoms.

## After

1. **Rebuild your reserves gradually**, without bulk buying.
2. **Check the dates** of what you have stored.

## Never do this

- Store gasoline in bottles or containers not made for it, or inside the home (see [Store fuel safely](../fire-and-power/store-fuel-safely.md)).
- Fill jerricans when the authorities ban it.
- Stop a treatment, space out the doses or split them on your own initiative.
- Pass on a rumor of a shortage without checking it.
