# Nuclear explosion

> **In short**: do not look at the flash. Drop to the ground behind cover, then quickly get inside the nearest brick or concrete building, in the basement or the center, away from the outer walls and the roof. Stay there for at least 24 hours, unless told otherwise, listening to a battery radio. If you were outside when the fallout arrived, take off your clothes and wash.

## When to use it

- The explosion of a nuclear weapon, with or without warning.
- Three dangers follow one another: the flash and the heat, the blast wave, then the fallout, radioactive dust that falls from the sky.
- For an accident at a power plant, the actions are not the same: see [Nuclear accident](nuclear-accident.md).

## What you need

- A battery or hand-crank radio: it still works when the phone, the television and the internet are down.
- Water and packaged food, stored indoors.
- Soap, wipes or clean cloths, plastic bags.
- Your medicines.

## Before

1. **Find possible shelters** near your home, your workplace and the school: basements, subways, tunnels, windowless middle floors of a high-rise.
2. **Remember what protects you**: distance, the thickness of the walls (concrete, brick, earth) and time.
3. **Keep supplies** to last several days without going out (see [Store water](../water/store-water.md)).
4. **Tell your relatives**: everyone shelters where they are, and you meet up afterwards.

## During

### An attack is announced

- Go into the nearest building and move away from the windows.

### The flash

1. **Do not look at the flash or the fireball**: they can blind you.
2. **Take cover behind anything you can find** and lie face down, with your head protected, to shield your skin from the heat and the debris.
3. **Stay down**: far from the explosion, the blast wave can take 30 seconds or more to arrive.
4. **In a car**: stop safely and duck down.

### Get inside

1. **Get to shelter quickly.** After the blast wave you have 10 minutes or more to reach the best shelter: outside the area closest to the explosion, fallout takes more than 15 minutes to reach the ground.
2. **Choose the nearest brick or concrete building.** A car or a mobile home does not protect you enough.
3. **Go down to the basement**, or to the center of the building, away from the outer walls and the roof.
4. **Outside, cover your nose and mouth** with a mask or a cloth.
5. **Take shelter even far from the explosion**: the wind carries fallout for hundreds of kilometers (hundreds of miles).

### Stay inside

1. **Turn off the ventilation, the air conditioning and forced-air heating.** Close the windows and the fireplace damper.
2. **Stay inside for at least 24 hours**, unless the authorities say otherwise. Most people will be able to leave within a few days. In the worst-hit areas it may last up to a month.
3. **Do not go out to fetch your relatives**: everyone stays where they are, and you will meet up afterwards.
4. **Keep pets inside.**
5. **Sick or injured**: wait to be told where to get care before leaving the shelter.

### Stay tuned

- Follow the instructions on the battery or hand-crank radio.
- Evacuate only when you are asked to.

### You were outside when the fallout arrived

1. **Take off your outer layer of clothing**: this removes up to 90 % of the radioactive material. Seal it in a plastic bag, away from people and animals.
2. **Shower** with plenty of soap and water, without scrubbing or scratching the skin.
3. **Wash your hair** with shampoo or soap, **without conditioner**: it binds radioactive material to the hair.
4. **Blow your nose gently** and wipe your eyelids, eyelashes and ears with a clean damp cloth.
5. **No shower**: wipe the skin and hair that were not covered with a wipe or a clean damp cloth.
6. **Pets**: brush them gently, then wash them.

### Eating and drinking

- Packaged food and anything that was stored indoors is safe.
- Do not eat or drink anything that was left uncovered outside, and do not drink water from an open source.

## After

1. **Go out only when told to.** Fallout is most dangerous during the first hours. After two weeks, about 1 % of the initial radioactivity is left.
2. **If an evacuation is decided**, follow the route you are given.
3. **Iodine tablets**: take them only when the authorities tell you to. They protect only the thyroid, against radioactive iodine, not against other radioactive materials or against radiation from outside the body.
4. **Look after the morale** of your household (see [Shock, stress and supporting others](../health-and-first-aid/shock-stress-and-supporting-others.md)).

## Never do this

- Look at the flash or the fireball.
- Stay outside or in a car believing you are sheltered.
- Go out to fetch your relatives during the first 24 hours.
- Use conditioner after being exposed to fallout.
- Eat or drink what was left uncovered outside.
- Take iodine without instructions.
