# Nuclear accident

> **In short**: at the alert, shelter in a solid building, close doors and windows, turn off the ventilation and listen to the radio. Take iodine tablets only when the authorities tell you to. Leave your children at school and be ready to evacuate if you are asked to.

## When to use it

- An accident at a nuclear power plant or another nuclear site, announced by a siren, an alert on your phone or the radio.
- You live or stay near a nuclear site.
- For a nuclear explosion, see [Nuclear explosion](nuclear-explosion.md).

## What you need

- A battery radio.
- Your iodine tablets, if you live in a distribution zone.
- Tape and cloths to block openings.
- Your emergency kit and your medicines, ready for an evacuation.

## Before

1. **Find out whether you live in the planning zone** of a nuclear site, and whether iodine tablets are handed out in advance there.
2. **Ask your local authority** for the leaflet about the warning signal and the instructions for the site.
3. **Prepare your emergency kit**: an evacuation may be decided.

## During

1. **Stay in the building where you are**, or go into the nearest one. In a car, get to a building.
2. **Close doors and windows**, block the air inlets, turn off the ventilation and the air conditioning.
3. **Listen to your local public radio** and follow the instructions.
4. **Do not touch objects left outside**, vehicles included.
5. **If it is raining and you come in from outside**, leave what is wet at the entrance: umbrella, shoes, clothes.
6. **Take iodine only when the authorities ask.** Stable iodine saturates the thyroid and stops radioactive iodine from settling there. Taken at the wrong time, it protects poorly; and it protects neither from other radioactive materials nor from radiation.
7. **Leave your children at school**: they are sheltered there.
8. **Get ready to evacuate.** If the order is given, take your kit and your medicines and go to the assembly point you are given.

### If you were outside during the alert

1. Take off your outer layer of clothing: this removes most of the contamination. Seal it in a plastic bag, away from people and animals.
2. Shower with soap and water, without scrubbing hard. Wash your hair with shampoo, without conditioner.
3. If you cannot shower, wipe uncovered skin with a wipe or a clean damp cloth.

## After

1. **Tap water usually remains safe to drink**, unless a restriction is announced. Follow the instructions.
2. **If some foods are banned**, eat the supplies stored in your home first.
3. **Do not eat** anything gathered, hunted or fished.
4. **Limit outings** in parks and forests, which may be more contaminated.
5. **Go back into an evacuated zone** only with the agreement of the authorities.

## Never do this

- Take iodine on your own initiative, or give it to a child without instructions.
- Fetch your children from school.
- Flee by car without instructions: outside, you are more exposed than in a closed building.
- Go out before the all-clear.

## Depending on where you are

- **Far from a plant**: the authorities organize the distribution of iodine if it becomes necessary, which takes several hours. Until then, sheltering remains the protection.
- **In France**: if you live within 20 km (12 miles) of a power plant, collect your iodine tablets from a pharmacy with the voucher sent in your name. The prefect decides when to take them.
- **In another country**: distances and instructions change from one country to the next. Follow the local authorities.
