# Long power outage

> **In short**: use a flashlight rather than candles, keep the refrigerator and the freezer closed, unplug appliances and save your phone battery. Never use a generator, a camping stove or a barbecue indoors: carbon monoxide kills without warning.

## When to use it

- An outage that lasts more than a few hours: storm, snow, heat wave, grid failure, planned load shedding.
- With the power, you may lose heating, hot water, the phone, the internet, cash machines, gas pumps and card payments.

## What you need

- Battery or hand-crank flashlights and spare batteries.
- A battery or hand-crank radio.
- A charged power bank.
- Water and food you can eat without cooking, for several days.
- Cash.
- A list of contacts on paper.

## Before

1. **Prepare lighting, a radio and a way to charge** your phone.
2. **List what depends on electricity** in your home: heating, cooking, a medical device, refrigerated medicines, the garage door, an electric lock, the elevator.
3. **Medical device or medicine kept in the refrigerator**: ask your doctor or your supplier what to do in an outage (see [Get through a crisis with a medical treatment](../health-and-first-aid/get-through-a-crisis-with-a-medical-treatment.md)).
4. **Put a thermometer** in the refrigerator and the freezer.
5. **Keep your car's tank** at least half full.
6. **Install a battery-powered carbon monoxide detector** if you have a fuel-burning appliance.

## During

1. **Look at your main breaker.** If it is still on, the outage comes from the grid: be patient. If it has tripped, the problem is in your home: unplug powerful appliances and switch it back on. If it trips again, call an electrician.
2. **Unplug electrical and electronic appliances** to protect them from surges when the power returns. Leave one lamp switched on so that you know when it is back.
3. **Use a flashlight.** If you only have candles, never leave them unattended.
4. **Keep the refrigerator and the freezer closed.** Closed, a refrigerator stays cold for about 4 hours; a full freezer for about 48 hours, 24 hours if it is half full. Eat fresh food first.
5. **Cook and keep warm safely**:
   - generator outdoors, at least 6 m (20 ft) from windows and doors;
   - camping stove, barbecue and brazier outdoors only;
   - a gas stove can be used to cook, never to heat the home.
6. **Save your phone**: power-saving mode, dim screen, text messages rather than calls.
7. **Keep informed by radio** and follow the instructions, including those about tap water.
8. **Check on** elderly, sick or isolated neighbors.
9. **Leave if the home gets too cold or too hot**, or if a medical device needs power: go to relatives or to a public shelter.

## After

1. **Plug things back in gradually**: the heating first, then the refrigerator and the freezer, and wait 10 to 15 minutes before the rest.
2. **Throw away refrigerated food** that stayed above 4 °C (40 °F) for more than 4 hours, and anything with an unusual smell, color or texture. Do not taste it to check.
3. **Medicines kept cold**: if the outage lasted a day or more, ask your pharmacist whether they must be replaced.
4. **Stay at least 10 m (35 ft) away from fallen power lines** and from anything they touch.
5. **Restock** batteries, water and food.

## Never do this

- Run a generator in the home, the garage or the basement, even with the door open.
- Plug a generator into a wall outlet.
- Heat your home with the oven or the gas stove.
- Leave a car running in the garage to charge a phone or to warm up.
- Touch the electrical panel with wet hands or while standing in water.
- Store food outdoors or in the snow.
