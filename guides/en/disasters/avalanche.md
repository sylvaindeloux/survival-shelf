# Avalanche

> **In short**: off the marked runs, never alone and never without a transceiver, a shovel and a probe, and turn back at the slightest doubt. If the avalanche takes you: escape to the side, drop anything heavy, protect your face and swim to stay on the surface. If you are a witness: keep your eyes on the victim, call the emergency services and search at once, if the place is safe.

## When to use it

- Off-piste skiing, ski touring, snowshoeing, mountaineering.
- Orange or red avalanche warning, heavy snowfall, a sudden thaw, strong wind, rain on snow.
- More than 9 accidents out of 10 come from a slab of snow, often triggered by the victim or their group.

## What you need

- For every member of the group: an avalanche transceiver (beacon), a shovel and a probe, and the training to use them.
- A charged phone, warm clothes, a flashlight.
- The avalanche bulletin of the day.

## Before

1. **Read the avalanche bulletin** for your area: the danger level, and the altitudes and slope aspects that are most dangerous.
2. **Look at the avalanche flag and signs** at the bottom of the ski lifts, and ask the ski patrol.
3. **Never go alone.** Tell someone your route and when you expect to be back.
4. **Choose a route that matches your level.** Slopes of 30° and more are where avalanches start: avoid them, and the areas just below them.
5. **Wear your transceiver under your outer layer**, in transmit mode, and test it before every outing. Shovel and probe in the pack.
6. **Practice searching regularly.** Going with a professional is recommended.
7. **Give up** if conditions are uncertain. An existing track guarantees nothing.

## During

### On a doubtful slope

- Spread out or cross one at a time, watching one another. Wait for each other on a high or sheltered spot.
- A recent avalanche nearby, a crack shooting through the snow: turn back.

### If the avalanche takes you

1. **Escape to the side**, toward safe ground.
2. **Drop heavy equipment.** Trigger your airbag pack if you have one.
3. **Fight to stay on the surface**, with swimming movements.
4. **Protect your airway**: when the slide slows down, bring your hands in front of your face.
5. **Stay calm.**

### If you are a witness

1. **Keep your eyes on the victim** and note the place where you last saw them.
2. **Call the emergency services.**
3. **Check that the place is safe**, then **search at once**: transceiver, probe, shovel.
4. **Dig the victim out**, give first aid and protect them from the cold.

## After

1. **More avalanches can start**: do not stay in the area longer than necessary.
2. **Warm the victim**: hypothermia is a threat. A body temperature below 35 °C (95 °F) is an emergency.
3. **Go back to the site** only with the permission of the rescuers or the authorities.

## Never do this

- Go out in the mountains under an orange or red avalanche warning.
- Leave without a transceiver, a shovel and a probe, or without knowing how to use them.
- Ignore a closed run or a closed road.
- Believe a slope is safe because others have crossed it.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a ski resort**: follow the safety instructions, the markings and the closures.
- **In an exposed valley or village**: avoid the threatened area and move away from valley bottoms. Under a red warning, stay home.
- **In France**: Météo-France publishes the avalanche risk bulletin every day at 4 p.m., for the next 24 hours.
