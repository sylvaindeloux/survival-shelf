# Extreme cold, snow and ice

> **In short**: limit your trips, dress in several layers, head and hands included, and heat your home without risking poisoning: never a generator, a camping stove or a brazier indoors. Stuck in your car, stay inside it. Check on isolated people, and call for help for anyone sleeping rough who is in trouble.

## When to use it

- Orange or red weather warning for extreme cold, snow or ice.
- Snow-covered or icy roads, power outages caused by snow.
- The danger is greater for older people, babies, people with a heart or lung condition, isolated or homeless people and people who work outdoors.

## What you need

- Warm clothes, blankets, shoes that do not slip.
- Water, food and your medicines for several days.
- A flashlight and a battery radio.
- In the car: snow equipment, blankets, dry clothes, water, food, a hot drink, medicines, a charged phone, a full tank.

## Before

1. **Have the heating and the flues serviced** by a professional before winter.
2. **Stock up**: snow and ice can cut roads, power and phone lines for several days.
3. **Equip your car** and keep the tank full.
4. **Identify vulnerable people** around you.
5. **Know where to go** if your home gets too cold: a relative's home, a heated public place.

## During

### At home

1. **Stay home** as much as possible. Under a red warning, do not go out.
2. **Keep at least 19 °C (66 °F)** in the rooms you live in, bedroom included. Close the rooms you do not use.
3. **Heat without poisoning yourself**: do not run a fuel-burning space heater continuously, never heat with a stove, a brazier or a barbecue, leave the generator outdoors.
4. **Do not block the air vents** and air the home for a few minutes every day, even in winter.
5. **Eat enough** and have hot drinks, without alcohol.
6. **Check on** elderly or isolated people.

### Outdoors

1. **Avoid long exposure** to cold and wind, especially in the evening, at night and early in the morning.
2. **Dress in several layers**, with an outer layer that stops wind and rain. Cover your head, your hands, your nose and your mouth. Do not keep damp clothes on.
3. **Avoid sudden effort**: shoveling snow or pushing a car can trigger a heart attack. Take breaks.
4. **Walk carefully** on snow and ice.

### In a car

1. **Check the state of the roads** and leave only if you really have to. Tell someone your route.
2. **If you are stuck, stay in the car**: it is your shelter. Leave it only when the authorities tell you to, or if help is visible close by.
3. **Run the engine for about 10 minutes every hour**, with a window slightly open, and check that the exhaust pipe is not blocked by snow.
4. **Move** your arms, legs and fingers. Huddle together and cover yourselves with everything you have.
5. **Make yourself visible**: a brightly colored cloth tied to the antenna, the hood raised once the snow has stopped.

### Warning signs

- **Shivering, numbness, slurred speech, confusion, drowsiness**: this may be hypothermia. Get the person into the warm, remove wet clothes, call the emergency services.
- **White or grayish, hard, numb skin** on the nose, ears, fingers or toes: this is frostbite. See [Hypothermia and frostbite](../health-and-first-aid/hypothermia-and-frostbite.md).
- **Headache, nausea, dizziness in a heated home**: think of carbon monoxide, air the home and get out.

## After

1. **Beware of ice**, of branches and of lumps of snow or ice that fall.
2. **Stay away from fallen wires.**
3. **Shovel snow in short sessions**, with breaks.

## Never do this

- Run a generator, a camping stove or a barbecue in the home, the garage or the basement.
- Heat your home with the oven or the stovetop.
- Block the air vents to keep the heat in.
- Set off on foot in the storm from a stuck car.
- Drink alcohol to warm up.

## Depending on where you are

- **In the street**: a homeless person seems to be in trouble? Call the emergency services or the local helpline for homeless people. In France, call 115, and 15 for a life-threatening emergency.
- **With a baby**: several thin layers rather than one thick one, and a hat. Do not let the baby sleep in a cold room.
- **In the mountains**: see [Avalanche](avalanche.md).
