# Attack by an artificial intelligence

> **In short**: an artificial intelligence turning against the population remains a hypothetical scenario, and no authority publishes instructions for it. What protects you is what protects you from any large outage and from any manipulation: being able to live for several days without power, without a network and without a bank card, checking an order or an alert through another channel, and keeping control of your connected devices.

## When to use it

- You want to prepare for a scenario in which artificial intelligence (AI) systems escape all control, or are used to cause harm on a large scale.
- **What the experts say**: the International AI Safety Report of January 2025, written by 96 experts, describes loss of control as a hypothetical scenario. It notes a broad consensus that current AI lacks the capabilities to pose this risk. For the coming years, opinions vary greatly: some experts consider the scenario implausible, others likely, others again unlikely but serious enough to deserve attention.
- **What already exists**: people use AI to make cyberattacks easier or faster, to imitate voices and faces, and to manipulate public opinion at lower cost.
- This guide gives no "anti-AI technique": none is validated by the official sources read. It starts from a simple idea: whoever is behind a cyberattack, what you can do at home stays the same.

## What you need

- A kit for 72 hours: 6 liters (about 1.5 gallons) of water per person, food, a battery radio, a flashlight, cash.
- On paper: the numbers of your relatives and your bank.
- A backup of your data on a drive that you unplug.

## Before

### Be able to live without the network

1. **Prepare enough to last several days** without electricity, phone, internet or bank card (see [Long power outage](long-power-outage.md) and [Network and payment outage](network-and-payment-outage.md)).
2. **Keep a battery radio** to follow official instructions.
3. **List what depends on power or on the network in your home**: locks, the garage door, the elevator, medical devices, cooking. Check that you can do without them or operate them by hand.

### Do not let yourself be manipulated

1. **Agree with your relatives**: any urgent request for money, a code or information is checked by calling the person back on their usual number.
2. **Keep a low profile online**: limit public photos, videos and recordings. A short recording of your voice is enough to copy it.
3. **Know the official channels** where you live: sirens, alerts on your phone, public radio, the accounts of the local authorities.

### Keep control of your connected devices

1. **Change the default passwords and codes** of every device and of your home router.
2. **Protect the online account linked to the device** with a strong password, different from the others, and with extra authentication if it is offered.
3. **Check that not just anyone can connect to it.**
4. **Turn the device off when you are not using it.**
5. **Be wary of low-cost devices.**

### Protect your accounts and your data

- Long, different passwords, two-factor authentication, automatic updates, unplugged backups: see [Cyberattack, scams and disinformation](cyberattack-scams-and-disinformation.md).

## During

### Services go down

1. **Apply the matching guides**: [Long power outage](long-power-outage.md), [Water outage or unsafe tap water](water-outage-or-unsafe-tap-water.md), [Network and payment outage](network-and-payment-outage.md).
2. **Listen to the radio** and to official channels.
3. **Do not phone unless you have to**, and send short messages: the networks are congested.

### An alert or an order arrives

1. **An official warning signal or a visible danger**: protect yourself first, you will check afterwards.
2. **A single message asks you for something unusual** (money, a code, personal data, an urgent instruction): check it by another means before acting, even if it seems to come from a relative or a colleague.
3. **Ask yourself four questions**: who is sending it to me? Is the source official? Has a major national news outlet published it? What is its purpose?
4. **Is it the voice or the face of a relative?** They can be imitated. Hang up and call the person back on their usual number, or go through another family member.
5. **Do not pass on** an alert or a chain message that you have not checked.

### A connected device behaves strangely

- Turn it off, then change the password of the account linked to it.
- Hacked account or lost money: see [Cyberattack, scams and disinformation](cyberattack-scams-and-disinformation.md).

## After

1. **Change your passwords** if an account may have been affected, and watch your statements.
2. **Report it** to the police and to your country's cybercrime reporting service. Without the internet, go to the police station or the town hall.
3. **Rebuild your supplies** and your backup.

## Never do this

- Trust a voice or a video alone to send money or give a code.
- Pass on an alert that you have not checked.
- Cut yourself off from official instructions out of distrust: in a danger, public radio and the local authorities remain your reference points.
- Rely on an "anti-AI protection" seen online.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: the official channels are the siren, FR-Alert notifications, public radio and the accounts of the prefecture and the town hall. Get help on cybermalveillance.gouv.fr or from the 17Cyber service, and report illegal content on PHAROS.
