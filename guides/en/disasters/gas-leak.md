# Gas leak

> **In short**: you smell gas? Touch no switch, electrical appliance or phone, and light no flame. Open the windows, close the gas valve if you can, get out, then call your gas company's emergency number or the fire service from outside. If the smell is strong, get out first.

## When to use it

- A smell of gas in your home, the stairwell, the basement or the street. Natural gas has no smell of its own: a strong smell, close to rotten eggs, is added so that you notice it.
- A hissing sound near a pipe, a meter or a gas appliance.
- After an earthquake or damage to the building.

## Before

1. **Find the gas valve** of your home and learn how to close it (see [Shut off gas, water and electricity](../fire-and-power/shut-off-gas-water-and-electricity.md)).
2. **Write down your gas company's emergency number.**
3. **Store gas cylinders** properly (see [Store fuel safely](../fire-and-power/store-fuel-safely.md)).

## During

1. **No flame, no spark**: no lighter, match or cigarette. Do not touch switches, either on or off, or the doorbell, electrical appliances or the phone.
2. **Open the windows** to air the place.
3. **Close the gas supply**, at the valve on the meter or on the appliance.
4. **Get everyone out.** Warn the neighbors by knocking on doors, without ringing. Take the stairs, not the elevator.
5. **Call once you are outside, away from the smell**: your gas company's emergency number, or the fire service.
6. **Wait for the technician outside.**

### The smell is strong, or you hear gas escaping

- Get out at once, without doing anything else, get the others out and call from a neighbor's home or from the street.

### You smell gas outdoors

- Move away, report the leak and do not look for where it comes from.
- Keep vehicles and engines away from the area.

## After

1. **Do not go back in** until the technician or the firefighters say it is safe.
2. **Do not turn the gas back on yourself** after a safety shut-off: a professional must do it.
3. **Have the installation repaired** by a professional before you use it again.

## Never do this

- Switch a light on or off.
- Make a phone call from inside the home, even with a mobile.
- Look for the leak with a flame.
- Take the elevator.
- Go back in "just to air the place" before the emergency services agree.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment building**: warn the neighbors and the caretaker, without using doorbells or the intercom.
- **Headache, nausea, dizziness without any smell**: this is not a gas leak, it may be carbon monoxide, which has no smell at all. See [Carbon monoxide poisoning](../health-and-first-aid/carbon-monoxide-poisoning.md).
- **In France**: the gas emergency number (Urgence sécurité gaz) is 0 800 47 33 33, free of charge. You can also call 18 or 112.
