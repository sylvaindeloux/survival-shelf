# Tsunami

> **In short**: on the coast, if the ground shakes hard or for a long time, if the sea draws back or rises suddenly, or if you hear a roar from the ocean, leave at once on foot for high ground, without waiting for a warning. Stay there until the official all-clear: the first wave is not always the biggest.

## When to use it

- You are on a coast: a beach, a harbor, a bay, a river mouth or the bank of a river close to the sea.
- One of the natural signs appears:
  - an earthquake, with ground that shakes violently or for a long time;
  - an abnormal rise of the sea, or a fast retreat that uncovers the sea floor;
  - a roar from the ocean, sometimes like a train.
- The authorities issue a warning.

## Before

1. **Find the signposted refuge sites** and the routes to reach them on foot, at home and on vacation.
2. **Where nothing is signposted, pick a high point** you can reach in a few minutes.
3. **Teach the natural signs** to the whole household: when the source of the tsunami is close, the wave can arrive within minutes, before any official warning.

## During

1. **Leave immediately**, at the natural sign or the warning. Do not wait to see the wave.
2. **Evacuate the shore and low-lying areas on foot.** Do not take the car.
3. **Go up to a refuge site**: in the French West Indies they are signposted above 15 m (50 ft). Where nothing is signposted, go as high and as far as you can: the American Red Cross aims for 30 m (100 ft) above sea level or 1.6 km (1 mile) inland.
4. **Move away from rivers**: the waves travel up them.
5. **If you cannot get away**: go up to the top floor or the roof of a sturdy building with several floors. As a last resort, climb a strong tree and hold on.
6. **If you are swept away**: hold on to something that floats.
7. **Do not call**, send text messages: leave the lines to the emergency services.

## After

1. **Stay on high ground** until the official all-clear. Waves can follow one another for hours, and currents stay dangerous for much longer.
2. **Do not go down to look** between two waves.
3. **On the way back, keep out of the floodwater** and treat every power line as live.
4. **Look at the building from outside** before going in.
5. **Do not drink tap water** until you have checked that it is safe. Throw away food that got wet.

## Never do this

- Go and look at the sea drawing back.
- Wait for an official warning after a strong earthquake on the coast.
- Take the car.
- Go back after the first wave.
- Believe a tsunami can be "surfed": the whole mass of water moves forward, at 30 to 50 km/h (20 to 30 mph) near the shore.

## Depending on where you are

- **On a boat at sea**: stay at sea, face the waves and move away from the coast. **In a harbor**: leave the boat and go to high ground.
- **On a beach on vacation**: find the way to high ground when you arrive.
- **Distant earthquake**: the wave can take several hours to arrive. The warning is then given by radio, television and the authorities.
