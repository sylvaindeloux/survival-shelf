# Earthquake

> **In short**: during the shaking, drop, take cover under sturdy furniture or against a load-bearing wall, away from windows, protect your head and neck, and hold on until it stops. Then leave by the stairs, move away from anything that could collapse and expect aftershocks. On the coast, go to high ground at once.

## When to use it

- The ground shakes, objects fall.
- You live or travel in an earthquake zone: ask your local authority about the risk.

## What you need

- Next to the bed: sturdy shoes and a flashlight.
- An emergency kit with water and food for several days, a whistle, a battery radio.
- Fastenings for tall furniture.

## Before

1. **Secure what can fall**: tall furniture, shelves, the television, mirrors, the water heater. Keep heavy or breakable objects low down.
2. **Learn how to shut off** the gas, the water and the electricity.
3. **In every room, pick the place** where you will take cover: a sturdy table, a load-bearing wall, away from glass.
4. **Practice** "drop, cover, hold on" with the whole household.
5. **Be ready to live for several days** without electricity, gas and water.

## During

### Indoors

1. **Drop** onto your hands and knees, **take cover** under a sturdy table or against a load-bearing wall, away from windows, and **hold on** until the shaking stops.
2. **Protect your head and neck** with your arms.
3. **Do not rush outside**: during the shaking, falling debris is the first danger. Only if you are on the ground floor, right next to an exit, go out and move away from the building.
4. **In bed**, stay there and protect your head with the pillow.
5. **In a wheelchair**, lock the wheels and protect your head and neck.
6. **Do not take the elevator.**

### Outdoors

- Move away from buildings, power lines and trees. Drop to the ground in an open space.

### In a car

- Stop away from buildings, trees, bridges and power lines, and stay inside until the shaking stops.

## After

1. **Wait a minute** before getting up, then put on sturdy shoes: there is glass everywhere.
2. **Leave the building by the stairs** and move away from anything that could collapse. Do not go back into a damaged building.
3. **Expect aftershocks**: at each one, drop, cover, hold on.
4. **On the coast, leave at once** for high ground or go inland, without waiting for a warning (see [Tsunami](tsunami.md)).
5. **Beware of gas**: no flame, lighter or switch until you are sure there is no leak. Smell of gas: shut it off if you can, get out and call the emergency services from outside.
6. **Shut off the water and the electricity** if the installations are damaged.
7. **Phone only for an emergency**: text instead. Listen to the radio.
8. **If you are trapped**: cover your mouth and nose with a cloth, and whistle or knock three times on a pipe or a wall every few minutes rather than shout.

## Never do this

- Run outside during the shaking.
- Stand in a doorway: it is not a shelter.
- Take the elevator.
- Go back into a damaged building to fetch belongings.
- Light a flame before checking for gas.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a high-rise**: expect alarms and sprinklers to go off. Stay under cover, then go down by the stairs.
- **In the mountains or near a cliff**: watch out for falling rocks and landslides.
- **On the coast**: a strong or long earthquake is the first sign of a tsunami.
- **In France**: the Géorisques website and the town hall tell you the earthquake risk at your address.

The sources differ on one point: the French Government's guide advises moving away from the building if you are on the ground floor or near an exit, while FEMA says not to run outside during the shaking. This guide keeps both: take cover where you are, and go out only if the exit is a step away.
