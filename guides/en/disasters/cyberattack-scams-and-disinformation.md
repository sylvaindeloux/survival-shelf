# Cyberattack, scams and disinformation

> **In short**: a long, different password for each account, two-factor authentication, automatic updates and backups that you unplug. A message, a call or even the voice of a relative demands money or a code urgently: hang up and check by another means. Before sharing a piece of news, ask yourself who published it, when and for what purpose.

## When to use it

- An unexpected message or call from a "bank", a "government office", a "delivery driver" or a "relative in distress".
- A hacked account, a locked computer, payments you do not recognize.
- A shocking video, photo or message that circulates during a crisis.
- A cyberattack against a service you depend on: hospital, bank, phone company, local authority.

## Before

1. **Use a long password** (at least 12 characters) **and a different one for each account**. A password manager remembers them for you.
2. **Turn on two-factor authentication** wherever it is offered.
3. **Turn on automatic updates** on your devices, and install software and apps only from official sites and stores.
4. **Back up your data regularly** on an external drive, which you then unplug: a backup left connected can be destroyed by the same attack.
5. **Change the default user name and password** of your home router.
6. **Keep a low profile online**: limit public photos, videos and personal information. A short recording of your voice is enough to copy it.
7. **Keep on paper** the numbers of your bank and your relatives.

## During

### A suspicious message or call

1. **Do not click, do not reply, give nothing.** A serious organization never asks for a password or bank details by message or by phone.
2. **Spot the signs**: urgency, an offer too good to be true, an unexpected attachment, an odd sender address, a request for a wire transfer, cryptocurrency or gift cards.
3. **Check by another route**: contact the organization yourself, with the number or the address you already know.
4. **Is it the voice or the face of a relative?** Voices and videos can be imitated by artificial intelligence. Hang up and call the person back on their usual number, or go through another family member.
5. **Report it** to your country's reporting service.

### You have been tricked

1. **Call your bank** at once to block your cards and payments.
2. **Change the password** concerned, everywhere you used it.
3. **Keep the evidence**: messages, screenshots, links.
4. **Have the affected device scanned or cleaned**, or take it to a professional.
5. **File a complaint** with the police.

### Doubtful news is going around

1. **Do not share it** until you have checked the source, the author and the date.
2. **Ask yourself four questions**: who is sending it to me? Is the source official? Has a major national news outlet published it? What is its purpose?
3. **Be wary of spectacular images.** Possible signs of a deepfake: a blurred or pixelated image, an unnatural gaze, a badly synchronized mouth, abnormal light and shadows on the face.
4. **In a crisis**, rely on official channels: local authorities, public radio.
5. **Report** illegal content on the platform concerned.

## After

1. **Watch your accounts** and your statements.
2. **Warn your contacts** if your account may have sent them booby-trapped messages.
3. **If your image or your voice has been misused**: keep the evidence, report it to the platform and file a complaint.

## Never do this

- Give a code received by text message, a password or a card PIN to someone who contacts you.
- Send money under pressure.
- Use the number or the link given in the suspicious message to "check".
- Pass on an alert or a chain message that you have not checked.

## Depending on where you are

- **Without the internet**: to report something or file a complaint, go to the police station or the town hall.
- **If the outage affects payments or the phone**: see [Network and payment outage](network-and-payment-outage.md).
- **In France**: forward a suspicious text message to 33700 and report an email on Signal Spam. Get help on cybermalveillance.gouv.fr or from the 17Cyber service. Info Escroqueries answers on 0 805 805 817 (free). Report illegal content on PHAROS, and contact the CNIL if your personal data is used without your consent.
- **In the United States**: report fraud to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
