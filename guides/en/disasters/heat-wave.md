# Heat wave

> **In short**: drink regularly without waiting to be thirsty, wet your skin several times a day, keep your home closed by day and open at night, and spend 2 to 3 hours a day in a cool place. Check on elderly or isolated people. A fever above 40 °C (104 °F), hot, red and dry skin, confused speech: this is heatstroke, call the emergency services.

## When to use it

- Orange or red weather warning for heat, hot weather that lasts, warm nights.
- Everyone is concerned, even in good health. The danger is greater for older people, babies and young children, pregnant women, people with a chronic illness, isolated people, athletes and people who work outdoors.

## What you need

- Plenty of water.
- A spray bottle, washcloths or cloths to wet.
- Shutters, blinds or curtains.
- A fan, knowing that it is no longer enough when it gets very hot.

## Before

1. **Find cool places** near your home: a library, a movie theater, an air-conditioned store.
2. **Think of the isolated people** around you: you will need to check on them.
3. **Ask your doctor or pharmacist** whether your treatment calls for precautions: some medicines make dehydration worse. Never stop or change a treatment on your own.

## During

1. **Drink regularly**, without waiting to be thirsty. Avoid alcohol, very sweet drinks and drinks with caffeine.
2. **Keep eating normally**: fruit, raw vegetables, cold meals.
3. **Cool yourself several times a day**: a water spray, a wet cloth on your face and forearms, a lukewarm shower or bath.
4. **Stay in during the hottest hours** and put off any effort: no intense activity between noon and 6 p.m. Outside, wear a hat and light, loose clothing.
5. **Spend 2 to 3 hours a day in a cool** or air-conditioned place.
6. **Close shutters, curtains and windows by day**, on the sunny side first. Open wide as soon as the air outside is cooler than inside, especially at night.
7. **Do not rely on a fan alone**: above about 35 °C (95 °F) it no longer protects you. Wet your skin, take a shower or go to a cool place.
8. **Never leave** a child, an adult or an animal alone in a car.
9. **Check on** vulnerable relatives and neighbors, and help them drink and cool down.

### Warning signs

- **Cramps, great tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea**: stop any effort, get into the cool, drink, wet your skin. Get medical advice if it lasts more than an hour or gets worse.
- **Fever above 40 °C (104 °F), hot, red and dry skin, confused speech, seizures, loss of consciousness**: call the emergency services and cool the person down (see [Heatstroke](../health-and-first-aid/heatstroke.md)).

## After

1. **Air the home wide open** as soon as the air outside is cooler than inside again.
2. **See a doctor** if you still feel unwell.
3. **Ask your pharmacist** what to do with medicines that stayed for a long time in a very hot home.

## Never do this

- Wait until you are thirsty to drink.
- Leave someone in a car, even for a few minutes.
- Do sports or outdoor work during the hot hours.
- Stop a medicine on your own initiative.
- Under a red warning: use a tool that makes sparks, smoke or have a barbecue near vegetation.

## Depending on where you are

- **Home under the roof or without shutters**: spend the hot hours elsewhere, with relatives or in a cool public place.
- **Working outdoors**: drink a glass of water every 15 to 20 minutes and move the hardest tasks to the cool hours.
- **In France**: register isolated people you know with the town hall, which can also help you. The Canicule info service line answers at 0 800 06 66 66, free of charge, from 9 a.m. to 7 p.m.
