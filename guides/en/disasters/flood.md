# Flood

> **In short**: do not take the car, do not go down to a basement or an underground garage, go upstairs and stay sheltered. Less than 30 cm (1 ft) of water is enough to carry a car away. Evacuate only when the authorities order it, and leave your children at school: they are sheltered there.

## When to use it

- Orange or red weather warning for rain, flooding or rising rivers, or a violent thunderstorm.
- Water is rising in your street or near your home.
- You live in a flood zone and want to get ready.

## What you need

- Your emergency kit for 3 days, stored high up, out of reach of the water.
- A battery radio, a flashlight and a charged phone.
- Your documents in a waterproof pouch.
- For cleaning up: boots, work gloves, goggles, a mask.

## Before

1. **Find out about the risk** where you live: ask your local authority.
2. **Follow the warnings** of your weather service and of the service that watches river levels.
3. **Work out where to go up**: an upper floor, or high ground you can reach quickly. A flash flood leaves little time: in the storms that hit the French Mediterranean coast, some rivers rise by more than 1 m (3 ft) in a few minutes.
4. **Put what matters out of reach**: documents, valuables and polluting products high up.
5. **When a warning is issued**: locate your kit, lift your belongings above the water, clear gutters and drains.

## During

1. **Stay sheltered in a building** and put off every trip, on foot or by car.
2. **Go upstairs.** As a last resort, go onto the roof. Do not shut yourself in an attic with no opening: you could be trapped there.
3. **Do not go down** to a basement or an underground garage.
4. **Shut off the gas, the electricity and the heating**, only if you can do it without putting yourself in danger.
5. **Keep away from rivers**, banks and bridges.
6. **Leave your children at school**: they are sheltered there.
7. **Follow the instructions** on the radio and on official accounts. Evacuate only when the authorities order it, with your kit.
8. **Check on your neighbors** and on vulnerable people, by text message rather than by phone, to leave the networks to the emergency services.

### If you are caught outside or in a car

- Never drive or walk onto a flooded road, even a partly flooded one, a ford, a low bridge or an underpass.
- 15 cm (6 in) of fast-moving water can knock an adult down, 30 cm (1 ft) carries a car away.
- Get to high ground and stay there.

## After

1. **Wait for the go-ahead from the authorities** before going home.
2. **Beware of electricity**: stay away from fallen wires, do not touch any wet appliance, and touch nothing electrical while you are standing in water. Turn the power back on only when the wiring is dry.
3. **Keep out of the floodwater**: it may contain sewage, chemicals and sharp objects.
4. **Do not drink tap water** until your local authority has confirmed that it is safe.
5. **Throw away food** that got wet or was no longer kept cold.
6. **Protect yourself to clean up**: boots, gloves, goggles, a mask. Air, disinfect and dry the home within 24 to 48 hours, or mold sets in. Throw away what cannot dry.
7. **Photograph the damage** and tell your insurer.

## Never do this

- Take the car or cross a flooded road.
- Go down to a basement or an underground garage, for example to save your car.
- Fetch your children from school during the alert.
- Stand on a bridge or a bank to watch the flood.
- Run a generator, a camping stove or a barbecue indoors to dry or heat the home: they cause carbon monoxide poisoning.

## Depending on where you are

- **On the coast**: when a warning for waves and coastal flooding is issued, keep away from the shore and estuaries, do not go out to sea and get to high ground.
- **Behind a levee**: in a major flood, a levee can be overtopped or give way. Be ready to go upstairs, or to evacuate when ordered.
- **Below a dam**: see [Dam failure](dam-failure.md).
- **In France**: the Géorisques website and the town hall tell you the risk at your address. Vigicrues watches the rivers and gives the flood risk for the next 24 hours.
