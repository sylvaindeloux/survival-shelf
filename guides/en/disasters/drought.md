# Drought

> **In short**: find out which water restrictions apply where you live and follow them. Save water every day: short showers, full machines, reused water, repaired leaks. In dry weather, the smallest fire can set vegetation ablaze.

## When to use it

- Your area is under a drought watch, alert or emergency.
- Groundwater and rivers are low, and your local authority asks everyone to save water.
- If the tap runs dry, see [Water outage or unsafe tap water](water-outage-or-unsafe-tap-water.md).

## Before

1. **Look for leaks**: read your water meter, use no water for 30 minutes, read it again. If it has moved, there is a leak. A tap that drips once a second wastes about 10,000 liters (2,700 gallons) a year.
2. **Test the toilet**: put a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If the color shows in the bowl within 30 minutes without flushing, it leaks.
3. **Fit water-saving equipment** and a rain barrel for the garden.
4. **In the garden**: choose plants that tolerate dry weather, mulch the soil, prefer drip irrigation.

## During

1. **Check the restrictions in force** at your address, on your local authority's or water supplier's website.
2. **Follow the restriction level.** Systems differ from one country to the next. In France the prefect sets one of four levels:
   - **vigilance**: no restriction, everyone is asked to save water;
   - **alert**: restrictions on watering, on filling and emptying pools, on washing vehicles;
   - **reinforced alert**: the same, stricter;
   - **crisis**: many household uses are banned.
3. **Save water at home**:
   - take short showers, turn the water off while you soap up, brush your teeth or shave;
   - run the washing machine and the dishwasher only when they are full;
   - when washing dishes by hand, use two basins, one to wash and one to rinse;
   - defrost food in the refrigerator, not under the tap.
4. **Reuse water** instead of throwing it away: the cold water that runs while you wait for hot water, or the water used to rinse vegetables, can water plants.
5. **Outdoors**, if watering is still allowed: water the plants and not the paths, in short sessions, and cover the pool.
6. **Beware of fire**: no cigarette ends, barbecues or tools that make sparks near dry vegetation.

## After

1. **Keep checking the restrictions**: they stay in force until the order is lifted.
2. **Keep the good habits** and repair the leaks you found.

## Never do this

- Water the garden, wash your car or fill your pool despite a ban.
- Pour down the sink water that could still be used for plants.
- Drink or cook with collected rainwater without treating it.
- Light a fire or throw away a cigarette end outdoors.

## Depending on where you are

- **With a private well or borehole**: the restrictions may apply to it too. Check the local order.
- **In a wooded area**: find out the fire risk of the day and any access bans (see [Wildfire](wildfire.md)).
- **In France**: the VigiEau website (vigieau.gouv.fr), updated every day, gives the restrictions in force at your address. The exact rules are in the order issued for your department.
