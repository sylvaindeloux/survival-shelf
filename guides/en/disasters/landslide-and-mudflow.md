# Landslide and mudflow

> **In short**: leaving beforehand is the only real protection. If you see signs (cracks in the ground, leaning trees or poles, doors that suddenly stick), or if it rains very hard on a slope, move away and raise the alarm. Never cross a road where water or mud is flowing.

## When to use it

- You live or travel at the foot or the top of a steep slope or a cliff, or at the bottom of a valley.
- Intense or long rain, waterlogged ground, an earthquake, land burned by a fire.
- A landslide has already happened in the same place: this is the first risk factor.

### Warning signs

- New cracks or bulges in the ground, the road or the sidewalks.
- Leaning trees, poles, walls or fences.
- Cracks in walls and floors, doors or windows that start to stick.
- Soil moving away from the foundations, a tilting patio.
- Leaking or broken pipes, sagging wires.
- A spring that appears in a new place, a stream that suddenly stops flowing.

## Before

1. **Find out about the risk** from your local authority.
2. **Do not cut into a steep slope** and do not fell the trees that hold it. Plant the banks.
3. **Lead rainwater away** from fragile slopes: gutters, runoff.
4. **Work out which way to leave** and where to take refuge, away from slopes and valley bottoms.

## During

1. **If you see signs or feel in danger, leave at once**, without waiting for an official announcement. A landslide can happen with almost no warning and come down very fast.
2. **Move away from steep slopes**, valley bottoms and streams.
3. **Once you are safe**, call the emergency services and warn your neighbors.
4. **On the road**, never cross a roadway where water or mud is flowing.
5. **If signs appear in the evening**, do not wait for the night: leave before.

## After

1. **Stay away from the area**: more slides are likely.
2. **People may be injured or trapped**: do not go into the area, guide the rescuers.
3. **Beware of fallen wires** and broken pipes.
4. **Watch the water**: a flood can follow a landslide.
5. **Have your home and your land checked** before you move back in.

## Never do this

- Stay to watch a slope that is moving.
- Cross a mudflow, on foot or by car.
- Go back for belongings just after the slide.

## Depending on where you are

- **In the mountains after a thunderstorm**: a mudflow can rush down a mountain stream at more than 50 km/h (30 mph). Do not settle in the bed of a mountain stream, even a dry one.
- **After a wildfire**: bare ground slides more easily with the first heavy rain.
- **In France**: the town hall and the Géorisques website tell you the risk at your address.
