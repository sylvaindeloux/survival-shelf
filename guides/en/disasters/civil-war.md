# Civil war

> **In short**: stay discreet, keep away from armed groups and crowds, and keep enough to last several days without going out. Strengthen the entrances of your home, equip it so that you are warned of an intrusion, and prepare a room where you can lock yourself in. If someone breaks in, do not fight for your belongings: your life matters more.

## When to use it

- Lasting armed violence between groups inside the country, neighborhoods or roads held by armed groups, looting, emergency services that can no longer come everywhere.
- This is an extreme scenario: no official guidance written for it was found. This guide brings together official advice given for neighboring situations: armed conflict, home protection, attacks, unrest.
- For air strikes and artillery fire, see [Armed conflict and shelling](armed-conflict-and-shelling.md). For unrest that does not last, see [Riots and unrest](riots-and-unrest.md).

## What you need

- Supplies for 3 to 7 days: water (about 3 liters, or 3 quarts, per adult per day), food that can be eaten without cooking, medicines.
- A battery radio, flashlights, a power bank, a whistle.
- Your documents in a waterproof pouch, cash in small bills, a paper road map.
- A first aid kit.

## Before

### Stay discreet

1. **Leave nothing valuable visible** from the windows. Keep money, keys and valuables away from the entrances.
2. **Never say** where you keep your money and your valuables. Be just as discreet about your supplies.
3. **Do not announce your absences**, on social media or on your voicemail.
4. **Put only your last name** on the mailbox.

### Strengthen the entrances

1. **Front door**: a reliable lock, a door viewer and a door chain. Double-lock it, even when you are at home.
2. **Windows**: shutters and grilles. Do not leave the key in the lock of a glazed door.
3. **Keys**: hide none outside, not under the doormat, in the mailbox or in a flower pot.
4. **Tools**: lock away ladders, hammers, screwdrivers and garden tools. They are used to force a way in.
5. **Garden**: walls, fences and gates at least 1.8 m (6 ft) high at the back, sturdy side gates kept locked, a locked shed.

### Be warned of an intrusion

All these measures are meant to warn you and to deter, without hurting anyone.

1. **Outdoor lighting** that comes on with movement or at dusk.
2. **An alarm** or a presence detector.
3. **Gravel**: it makes noise when someone walks on it, which warns you and puts off an intruder.
4. **A trellis** on top of a wall or a fence: it is noisy to climb.
5. **A dense, prickly hedge** along the boundary: holly, hawthorn, blackthorn, berberis, firethorn, roses.
6. **Trimmed vegetation**: it leaves no hiding place, and you and your neighbors can see what is going on.
7. **Neighbors**: agree to watch over one another and to warn each other. Faced with suspicious behavior, note the vehicle (type, color, license plate) and the description of the people.

**No traps that injure.** The Irish police advise against measures of this kind, such as glass shards on top of walls: they make you liable. A trap also makes no difference between an intruder, a child, a neighbor and a rescuer.

### Prepare a safe room

1. **Choose a room** with a solid door that locks, and few or no windows.
2. **Plan how to block the door**: heavy furniture.
3. **Keep within reach**, in that room or close to it: a charged phone, water, the first aid kit, the radio, a flashlight and the whistle.
4. **Against gunfire**, the best place is the basement. Failing that, a room separated from the outside by at least two walls, away from windows.

### Prepare to leave

1. **Prepare your bag and your documents**: identity papers, family record, birth certificates, insurance, property deeds, and digital copies of them (see [Pack a go-bag](../basics/pack-a-go-bag.md)).
2. **Prepare the children**: have them learn their full name, their parents' names and a phone number, and give them this information on a piece of paper to keep on them.
3. **Agree with your relatives** on how you will find each other: the phone may stop working (see [Make a household plan](../basics/make-a-household-plan.md)).
4. **Learn how to stop bleeding** (see [Stop severe bleeding](../health-and-first-aid/stop-severe-bleeding.md)).

## During

### Day to day

1. **Keep away** from armed groups, crowds and demonstrations: even a peaceful gathering can turn violent without warning.
2. **Do not take photos or videos**: being there, watching or filming may be enough for you to be taken for a participant.
3. **Avoid exposed neighborhoods and places**, and be ready to change your plans: transport may be disrupted.
4. **Keep informed** by radio and official channels. Before you share a piece of news, check who the author is, when it was published, where it comes from and what its purpose is.
5. **Follow the instructions of the authorities.**

### Gunfire or explosions

1. **Lie down**, away from windows. Go down to the basement, or put at least two walls between you and the outside.
2. **Wrap your arms around your head**, cover your ears and keep your mouth open.
3. **Outside**: lie down and protect your head. Then reach a brick or concrete building by crawling or in short dashes, using solid obstacles for cover.
4. **Wait until the firing stops** before moving to a better shelter.

### Someone knocks at the door

1. **Do not open right away**: look through the door viewer and keep the door chain on.
2. **Check who it is.** Even in uniform, an official must be able to show a professional card. If in doubt, do not open.

### Someone breaks in

1. **Take no risks.** Do not fight for your belongings: your life matters more.
2. **Get away** if you can do it without risk.
3. **Otherwise, lock yourself in the safe room**: door locked and blocked, light off, phone on silent with no vibration. Stay silent, lying down away from the door.
4. **Raise the alarm** as soon as you are sheltered: call the police or the emergency number, or text if your country has a text service, if the networks still work.
5. **If you find yourself face to face with them**: do not provoke them, obey, do not resist. As soon as you can, shout or blow the whistle to call for help.
6. **Remember what you can**: number of people, apparent age, height, clothes, distinguishing marks, vehicle.

### People are injured

- Press directly on a bleeding wound with a clean cloth (see [Stop severe bleeding](../health-and-first-aid/stop-severe-bleeding.md)).

### Leaving

1. **If there are evacuation instructions**, follow them: route, destination, assembly point.
2. **With no instructions**, leave if your home itself becomes dangerous, waiting until the firing stops before you move (see [Decide whether to stay or go](../basics/decide-whether-to-stay-or-go.md)).
3. **Take the recommended roads**, not ones you do not know. Tell your relatives.
4. **Take** your bag, your documents, your medicines. Before leaving, turn off the electrical appliances you do not need, close the windows and lock the doors.

## After

1. **Touch no unknown object**, even one that looks harmless: unexploded shells, grenades and ammunition stay dangerous long after the fighting. Move away and report the object to the authorities.
2. **You have lost contact with a relative**: the Red Cross and Red Crescent can start a search, free of charge, through their Restoring Family Links service.
3. **Your home was broken into or looted**: touch nothing, take photos and file a complaint as soon as that is possible.
4. **Take care of yourself and of the children**: see [Shock, stress and supporting others](../health-and-first-aid/shock-stress-and-supporting-others.md).

## Never do this

- Set a trap meant to injure.
- Fight to defend belongings.
- Open the door to a stranger without checking who it is.
- Go near armed groups, or film or photograph them.
- Pick up an unknown object or ammunition.
- Pass on unverified information.

## Without equipment

- **Without electricity**, the alarm and the automatic lighting stop working. An object that rings or falls when a door or a gate is pushed, such as a small bell or cans tied to a string, is enough to warn you.

No official guidance written for a civil war was found when this guide was written. It brings together advice given for neighboring situations: armed conflict (ICRC, Latvian Ministry of Defence), protecting a home against burglary (French Ministry of the Interior, French Gendarmerie, Irish police), attacks (Vigipirate, FEMA), unrest (Australian government). For a break-in, it applies the official reflexes meant for an attack: get away, hide, raise the alarm. The following points are common sense, with no official source: discretion about supplies, what to keep near the safe room, the improvised warning in "Without equipment", and leaving a home that has become dangerous. On purpose, the guide describes no trap that injures, no fighting technique and no use of weapons.
