# Read a map and use a compass

> **In short**: orient the map first, which means lining up its north with north on the ground. On a 1:25,000 map, 4 cm is 1 km. To follow a bearing: lay the compass on the map between your start and your destination, line up the dial with north on the map, then turn yourself until the red needle sits inside the arrow of the dial.

## When to use it

- A hike, a journey or an evacuation on foot, when the phone and the GPS no longer work.
- To prepare a route and its fallback options.
- To practice: this is learned calmly, on ground you know.

## What you need

- A paper map at the right scale. For walking, 1:25,000 is ideal.
- A baseplate compass, with a clear plate and a dial that turns.
- A waterproof case for the map, a pencil.

## Steps

### Read the map

1. **Look at the scale.** At 1:25,000, 4 cm on the map is 1 km on the ground (about 2.5 inches to the mile), and 1 mm is 25 m. At 1:50,000, 2 cm is 1 km.
2. **Read the legend**: it explains each symbol (roads, paths, buildings, forests, streams).
3. **Read the relief** with the contour lines: each line joins points at the same height. The gap between two lines is often 10 meters: check it in the legend. Lines close together mean a steep slope, lines far apart a gentle one.
4. **Find north**: it is at the top of the map.

### Orient the map

1. **Without a compass**: stand near a landmark you can see clearly (a church tower, a crossroads, a summit), find it on the map, then turn the map until the other landmarks line up with what you see.
2. **With a compass**: lay it on the map and turn the two together until the red needle points to the top of the map.
3. **Keep the map oriented** as you walk: what is on the left of the map is on your left on the ground.

### Follow a bearing

1. **Lay the edge of the compass** on the map, between the point where you are and the point you are going to, with the direction-of-travel arrow toward your destination. Hold the map flat.
2. **Turn the dial** until its lines are parallel to the north-south lines of the map, with the arrow of the dial toward the top of the map. Ignore the needle.
3. **Lift the compass**, hold it flat in front of you, and turn yourself around until the red needle sits inside the arrow of the dial.
4. **Look along the direction-of-travel arrow**, pick a fixed landmark far ahead (a tree, a rock), walk to it, then start again.

### Estimate the time

1. **On the flat**, the usual rule counts 5 km/h (3 mph). At 4 km/h (2.5 mph), 100 m takes a minute and a half.
2. **Uphill**, add half an hour for every 300 m (1,000 ft) of climb, which is 1 minute for each 10-meter contour line.
3. **Plan fallback routes** before you leave.

## Never do this

- Touch the dial once the bearing is set.
- Hold the compass near a phone, a metal object or a magnetic button: the needle is pulled off.
- Leave without telling someone where you are going and when you will be back.
- Count on the phone alone: there is not a signal everywhere.

## Without equipment

- No compass: orient the map with the landmarks on the ground, and follow lines that are easy to recognize (a path, a ridge, a stream, the edge of a forest).
- No map: see [Find your way without GPS](find-your-way-without-gps.md).

## Depending on where you are

- **Magnetic declination**: the needle points to magnetic north, slightly different from north on the map. The difference is given in the legend. It matters most in regions where it is large and on ground without landmarks.
- **In a town**: a street map is oriented the same way, with the streets and the monuments.
- **Which map**: in France, the 1:25,000 maps come from the national mapping agency IGN; in Great Britain, from Ordnance Survey.
