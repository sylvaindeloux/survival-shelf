# Spot rumors and false information

> **In short**: before you believe or share, ask yourself four questions: who is sending this to me? Is the source official? Has a major news outlet published it? What is its purpose? The more shocking or urgent a message is, the more it deserves to be checked against at least two other sources. When in doubt, do not share.

## When to use it

- During a crisis: an alarming message, a shocking photo or video, "information" heard in the street or received as a chain message.
- An instruction is going around and you do not know where it comes from: evacuation, contaminated water, shortage, curfew.
- When information is missing, rumors take its place.

## What you need

- A battery radio, to hear the official instructions.
- The names of the official channels where you live: national and local government, emergency services.
- A step back: there is no hurry to share.

## Steps

1. **Stay calm.** A message that frightens, outrages or demands that you act right now is made to be shared without thinking.
2. **Ask yourself the four questions**:
   - who is sending this to me, and who wrote it in the first place?
   - is the source official?
   - has a major national news outlet published it?
   - what is its purpose: to inform, to sell, to frighten, to set people against each other?
3. **Look for the date and the place.** A real photo that is old or was taken somewhere else can illustrate a false story.
4. **Cross-check** with at least two other reliable sources: public radio and television, major newspapers, official accounts and websites.
5. **Look at who is publishing.** An official account is usually verified and carries the name of the organization. A serious website shows a full address and the name of the person responsible for it.
6. **Be wary of spectacular images.** A video or a voice can be made by artificial intelligence. Possible signs: a blurry image, an unnatural gaze, a mouth out of sync, abnormal light and shadows on the face.
7. **For an instruction, go back to the authority**: national or local government, fire service, police. Without the internet, listen to the radio or go to the town hall.
8. **When in doubt, do not share.** If you have passed on false information, correct it with the people you sent it to.

## Never do this

- Share "just in case".
- Act on an unconfirmed instruction: evacuate, rush to a store, stop a treatment.
- Believe a piece of information because many people repeat it.
- Use the link or the number given in a suspicious message to "check".
- Give a code, a password or money after an alarming message.

## Without equipment

- Without the internet or a phone, rumor travels by word of mouth. Ask every time: "Who saw it? Who told you?" If nobody can trace it back to an official source, treat the information as unverified.
- Go to the town hall, or listen to the radio at a neighbor's.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a neighborhood or messaging group**: appoint one person who checks information before it circulates.
- **With children or teenagers**: explain these questions to them, and look with them at what they receive.
- **A call or message from a relative who urgently asks for money**: hang up and call them back on their usual number (see [Cyberattack, scams and disinformation](../disasters/cyberattack-scams-and-disinformation.md)).
- **In France**: the official channels are the prefecture, the town hall and the emergency services. Report illegal content on the official Pharos platform.
