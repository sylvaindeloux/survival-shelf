# Find your way without GPS

> **In short**: if you are lost, stop before you stray any farther: sit down, think, observe, then decide. The sun rises in the east and sets in the west. Move from landmark to landmark instead of guessing, and follow the lines of the land: a road, a path, a stream. At night, in fog, injured or exhausted, stay where you are.

## When to use it

- Your phone is dead, broken or has no signal, or the GPS has failed.
- You no longer recognize anything, in a town or out in nature.
- Before a trip, so that you do not depend on a screen.

## What you need

- A paper map of the area and a compass (see [Read a map and use a compass](read-a-map-and-use-a-compass.md)).
- A watch.
- A paper with your route, left with someone you trust.

## Steps

### If you are lost

1. **Stop.** Panic is your worst enemy: sit down, drink, breathe.
2. **Think**: which way did you come? What is the last place you recognized? Do not set off again without a specific reason.
3. **Observe**: visible landmarks, signs, trail markers, the shape of the land, the sound of a road or of water.
4. **Decide.** If you are sure of the way back, retrace your steps to the last known point. If not, stay where you are and signal (see [Call for help without a phone signal](call-for-help-without-a-phone-signal.md)).
5. **Leave a broken-down vehicle** only if you know where you are and where you are going, if you are sure to reach water, shelter or help, or if you are certain nobody will come. Then leave a note: your destination, your route, the time, your condition.

### Find a direction

1. **The sun** rises in the east and sets in the west.
2. **A watch with hands**, in the northern hemisphere: point the hour hand at the sun. South is halfway between the hour hand and 12. On daylight saving time, take off one hour first. In the southern hemisphere, point 12 at the sun: north is halfway between 12 and the hour hand.
3. **At night**, in the northern hemisphere, the North Star shows north, if you can recognize it. In the southern hemisphere, the Southern Cross shows south.
4. **These methods are not accurate**: they give a general direction, not a bearing.

### Move without going in circles

1. **Walk from landmark to landmark**: pick a fixed point far ahead in your direction (a tree, a rock, a building), reach it, then pick the next one.
2. **To go around an obstacle**, first aim at a landmark on the far side, in line with your direction, and reach it.
3. **Aim off on purpose** when you are looking for a road or a river: by aiming a little to the left or to the right, you know which way to turn when you reach it.
4. **Estimate the distance**: at 4 km/h (2.5 mph), you cover 100 m (110 yd) in a minute and a half.
5. **As a last resort**, follow a small valley or a stream downhill: the going is often hard, but it may lead to a path or a road.

## Never do this

- Keep going "to see" when you no longer know where you are.
- Split from the group.
- Walk at night, in fog or when exhausted.
- Cut across ground you do not know to save time.
- Rely on a single rough method for a long journey.

## Without equipment

- No map and no compass: use the map in your head. Which landmarks do you know around here (a church tower, a pylon, a summit, a river), and on which side were they on the way out?
- No watch: in Europe and North America, the sun at its highest is roughly to the south.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a town**: follow a main street to a crossroads or a square, read the street signs, ask your way in a shop or a public office.
- **In the forest or the mountains**: see [Read a map and use a compass](read-a-map-and-use-a-compass.md).
- **In a car**: keep a paper road map and write down the route before you leave.
