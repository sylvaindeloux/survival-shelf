# Get information without the internet

> **In short**: keep a battery or hand-crank radio, with spare batteries, and listen to public radio: it relays the instructions of the authorities. Without a radio, you still have the car radio, the sirens, vehicles with loudspeakers and the town hall. Do not pass on information you have not checked.

## When to use it

- The internet, the mobile network or the television no longer works, or the power is out.
- A warning has just been issued and you want to know what to do.
- Before a crisis, to prepare a way of getting information without a screen.

## What you need

- A battery or hand-crank FM radio, and spare batteries.
- The frequencies of the public radio stations, written on a paper stuck to the set.
- A paper list of useful numbers: family, neighbors, doctor, town hall.
- A paper map of your area.

## Steps

1. **Turn on the radio** as soon as a warning sounds or the networks go down. Public radio and television relay the official instructions.
2. **Listen at set times**, for example at the top of each hour, then switch off: you save the batteries.
3. **Without a battery radio, use the car radio**, with the car parked outdoors.
4. **Listen for the signals**: the siren, and the vehicles with loudspeakers that drive through the streets (see [Recognize warning signals](../basics/recognize-warning-signals.md)).
5. **Read the emergency alerts sent to your phone** if the mobile network still works.
6. **Go and get the information in person** if you can go out safely: town hall, police station.
7. **Call on your neighbors**: the one with a radio informs the others, and you check on people who live alone.
8. **Sort what you hear.** An instruction comes from the authorities: national or local government, emergency services. Everything else is checked before it is repeated (see [Spot rumors and false information](spot-rumors-and-false-information.md)).
9. **Write down the instructions** and the time: reception centers, closed roads, whether the water is safe to drink.

## Never do this

- Go out during a warning to find out what is happening: take shelter first, the radio is enough.
- Call an emergency number to ask for information: those lines are for emergencies.
- Repeat a rumor.
- Run the engine in a garage to listen to the car radio.

## Without equipment

- No radio and no car: the siren tells you to take shelter. Then ask a neighbor, and then the town hall, what the instructions are.
- A corded landline phone, plugged into a traditional line, may work without power. A cordless phone or one plugged into an internet box stops with the outage.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: the Radio France stations (France Info and the local public station) and public television relay the instructions. FR-Alert notifications reach every phone in the danger zone, with no sign-up and no app. On motorways, 107.7 FM gives the traffic conditions.
- **Near an industrial site, a dam or a nuclear plant**: ask your town hall, now, for the instructions and signals specific to the site.
- **Abroad**: before you leave, write down the details of your consulate and register with your country's service for travelers, if there is one. For French citizens, it is the Ariane service of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
- **Before the outage**: download or print the maps you may need.
