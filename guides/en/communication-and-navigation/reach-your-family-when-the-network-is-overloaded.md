# Reach your family when the network is overloaded

> **In short**: write instead of calling: a short text often gets through when calls no longer do. If you have to call, keep it brief, and wait 10 seconds before redialing. Everyone goes through the same out-of-area contact, who gathers the news, and you meet at the agreed meeting place if nothing gets through.

## When to use it

- Right after a disaster, an attack or a major outage: everyone calls at the same time and the network is overloaded.
- You are separated from your family and want to give or get news.
- Before a crisis, to prepare how you will find each other.

## What you need

- A charged phone and a power bank.
- Your important contacts written on paper, in your wallet and in the go-bag.
- An out-of-area contact and two meeting places, chosen in advance (see [Make a household plan](../basics/make-a-household-plan.md)).

## Steps

1. **Get to safety first.** The news can wait a few minutes.
2. **Send a short text**: "I am OK, I am at this place, I am going to that place." Written messages need less network than voice.
3. **Try the other routes**: email, messaging apps, social media, Wi-Fi calling if Wi-Fi still works, a landline.
4. **If you have to call, be brief** and give only the essentials.
5. **The call does not go through: wait 10 seconds** before redialing. Calling again and again overloads the network even more.
6. **Write to the agreed out-of-area contact**: they live outside the affected area and pass on everyone's news.
7. **Move a few meters** if the signal is weak, without putting yourself in danger.
8. **Nothing gets through: go to the agreed meeting place.** If a relative lives alone, go and see them at home when it is safe to do so.
9. **Leave a note** in plain sight at home if you leave: the time you left and where you are going.
10. **Save the battery** between two tries (see [Make your phone battery last](make-your-phone-battery-last.md)).

## Never do this

- Call the same number over and over.
- Phone without need during a warning: the lines must stay free for the emergency services.
- Call an emergency number to get news of a relative.
- Go and fetch your children from school during a warning: the staff shelter them there.
- Send videos or large attachments.

## Without equipment

- Phone dead or lost: borrow a neighbor's and dial the numbers written on your paper.
- No means of communication at all: go to the meeting place at the agreed times, and leave a message at the town hall or the reception center.

## Depending on where you are

- **With children**: have them learn their full name, yours and your number, and slip these on a paper into their bag.
- **Separated by a disaster, a conflict or exile**: the Red Cross and Red Crescent search, free of charge, for relatives people have lost track of, through the Restoring Family Links service. Contact the Red Cross or Red Crescent of the country you are in. The search is confidential and does not always succeed.
