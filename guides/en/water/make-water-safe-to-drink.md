# Make water safe to drink

> **In short**: if the water is cloudy, clear it first, then bring it to a rolling boil for 1 minute. That is the safest method against germs. With no fire, filter and then disinfect. No home method removes chemicals or fuel: in that case, drink bottled water only.

## When to use it

- The authorities issue a boil-water notice, or you doubt your tap water after a flood, a water cut or a broken main.
- You have to drink backup water: river, spring, rain, well, a reserve that is too old.
- Not when water is polluted by chemicals, fuel or radioactive material ("do not drink" notice): boiling or disinfecting does nothing, only bottled water will do.

## What you need

- A pot with a lid and a heat source: kitchen stove, camping stove, fire.
- A clean cloth, a paper towel or a coffee filter if the water is cloudy.
- Clean containers that close.

## Steps

1. **Take the best water you can find**: clear, flowing, with no smell or color.
2. **Clear it if it is cloudy**: let it settle, then gently pour the clear part through a clean cloth, a paper towel or a coffee filter.
3. **Bring it to a rolling boil for 1 minute.** Above 1,500 m (5,000 ft), boil for 3 minutes.
4. **Let it cool with the lid on**, without adding untreated water.
5. **Keep it** in a clean, closed container: 24 hours at room temperature, 3 days in the refrigerator.
6. If it tastes flat, pour it back and forth between two clean containers a few times, or add a pinch of salt per liter (quart).

If a local official instruction gives another boiling time, follow it: some health agencies ask for 5 minutes after a hurricane, for example.

### If you cannot boil

Choose from what you have, safest first:

- **Portable filter, then disinfection** (bleach or tablets): the best option without fire. The filter holds back bacteria and parasites, the disinfectant kills viruses.
- **Chlorine dioxide tablets**: they also work on parasites, with a longer waiting time (up to 4 hours).
- **Bleach or chlorine tablets alone**: they kill bacteria and viruses, but are weak against some parasites.
- **Sunlight**: 6 hours of full sun in a clear plastic bottle. A last resort.

Details are in [Filter water](filter-water.md) and [Disinfect water](disinfect-water.md).

### If the water is salty

Distilling is the only home method that removes salt. It also removes germs, heavy metals and most chemicals.

1. Fill a pot halfway.
2. Tie a cup to the handle of the lid so that it hangs right side up, without touching the water, when the lid is placed upside down on the pot.
3. Boil for 20 minutes: steam condenses under the lid and drips into the cup.

It is slow and burns a lot of fuel. Do not rely on it for water polluted by fuel.

### Which water for which use

- **Treated or bottled water**: drinking, cooking, washing fruit and vegetables, making ice, brushing teeth, preparing baby formula.
- **Untreated water**: flushing, cleaning, laundry.
- **Hands and dishes**: the most cautious choice is treated water here too. During a simple boil-water notice, Quebec accepts tap water with soap, as long as you dry well.

## Never do this

- Trust the look of water: clear water can still make you sick.
- Boil water polluted by chemicals: it removes nothing.
- Rely on a filter pitcher or on the freezer: neither kills germs.
- Pour treated water back into a dirty container, or dip your hands in it.

## Without equipment

- No pot: a single-wall metal bottle or a clean tin can will go on the fire.
- No fire and no product: use sunlight (see [Disinfect water](disinfect-water.md)).
- Nothing at all: put off drinking doubtful water for as long as you can, but do not let yourself get dehydrated. Then pick the clearest flowing water.

## Depending on where you are

- **At home**: keep a camping stove and fuel so you can boil water without mains electricity or gas.
- **In the car**: keep bottled water and a few tablets rather than counting on a complicated treatment.
- **Outdoors**: carry a filter and tablets, and boil water at camp.
- **At altitude or in deep cold**: boil for 3 minutes above 1,500 m (5,000 ft). Disinfectants work more slowly in very cold water: double the waiting time.
- **When traveling**: where tap water is not safe, drink sealed bottled water opened in front of you.
