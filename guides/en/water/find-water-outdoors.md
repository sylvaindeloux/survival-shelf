# Find water outdoors

> **In short**: look for clear, flowing water, taken upstream, away from houses and livestock. Collect rain, dew and snow too. Always treat water before drinking it, even when it looks clean. Never drink seawater or urine.

## When to use it

- A hike goes wrong, you break down far from everything, you evacuate on foot.
- The mains are down, your reserve is empty and you have to fetch water outside.

## What you need

- A container: water bottle, pot, dry bag.
- A clean cloth (bandana, T-shirt) and, if you can, a tarp or a plastic bag.
- A way to treat water: stove and pot, filter, tablets (see [Make water safe to drink](make-water-safe-to-drink.md)).

## Steps

1. **Prepare your water before you are thirsty.** Save your sweat rather than your water: stay in the shade and avoid effort during the hot hours.
2. **Go downhill**: water gathers at the bottom of valleys, at the foot of cliffs and wherever plants are greener and thicker.
3. **Choose the best water**, in this order:
   1. A spring or a flowing stream, taken as high as possible, upstream of trails, campsites, pastures and houses.
   2. A river or a lake: take water from the first few centimeters (inches) below the surface, away from the muddy edge.
   3. Standing water (pond, puddle), only when there is nothing else.
4. **Collect rain** in clean containers, or with a tarp stretched on a slope toward a container (see [Collect rainwater](collect-rainwater.md)).
5. **Harvest dew** at dawn: tie a clean cloth around your ankles, walk through tall grass, then wring the cloth out into a container.
6. **Melt snow or ice** before drinking. Eating snow cools your body and dehydrates you even more.
7. **Make a plant sweat**: close a clear plastic bag around a leafy branch of a non-poisonous plant, in the sun. Water condenses inside the bag. The yield is low: treat it as a bonus.
8. **Treat the water**: strain it through a cloth, then boil it for 1 minute or disinfect it.

If you truly have no way to treat water, put off drinking doubtful water for as long as you can, but do not let yourself get dehydrated. Then pick the clearest flowing water.

## Never do this

- Drink seawater: it dehydrates you and can kill.
- Drink urine.
- Drink untreated water because it looks clean.
- Take water that has a chemical smell, a strange color or an oily sheen, or that flows downstream of a factory, a mine or sprayed fields: no field treatment removes chemicals.
- Drink floodwater.
- Eat snow or ice without melting it.

## Without equipment

- No container: a plastic bag, a well-rinsed bottle you found, or a waterproof jacket can hold water.
- No filter: let the water settle, then pour it through a clean cloth. That makes it clearer, not safe.

## Depending on where you are

- **In the mountains**: springs and streams above the pastures are the best water points. Melt snow from snow patches. At altitude, boil water for 3 minutes.
- **In forests and farmland**: be wary of streams that run along pastures or crops.
- **By the sea**: seawater is only drinkable once distilled. Collect rain instead.
- **In dry country**: in a dry riverbed, dig at the foot of the bank on the outside of a bend. Walk during the cool hours.
- **In winter**: keep your bottle against your body so it does not freeze, and melt snow or ice before you drink it.
- **In town**: public fountains and taps can help. If the water is not marked as drinkable, treat it.

Go to the toilet at least 60 meters (200 feet) away from any water, and bury your waste 20 cm (8 inches) deep.
