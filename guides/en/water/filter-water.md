# Filter water

> **In short**: filtering does two different jobs. A cloth or a coffee filter clears cloudy water but does not make it safe. A fine portable filter (0.1 to 0.3 micron) holds back bacteria and parasites, not viruses. Either way, finish by boiling or disinfecting whenever you can.

## When to use it

- The water is cloudy: clear it before boiling or disinfecting, otherwise the treatment works poorly.
- You have a portable filter and must drink from a river, a lake or a spring.

## What you need

- To clear water: a clean cloth, a paper towel or a coffee filter, and two containers.
- To filter out germs: a portable filter (squeeze, pump, gravity or straw type) whose instructions state 0.1 to 0.3 micron.

## Steps

### Clear cloudy water

1. Let the water stand until the particles sink to the bottom.
2. Gently pour the clear part into a second container, through a clean cloth folded several times, a paper towel or a coffee filter.
3. Repeat if the water is still cloudy.
4. Then treat the water: boil it or disinfect it.

### Use a portable filter

1. **Check how fine it is** in the instructions: 0.3 micron or less for bacteria, 1 micron or less for parasites. Common hiking filters are 0.1 to 0.2 micron.
2. **Clear cloudy water first**: the filter will clog more slowly.
3. **Keep dirty and clean apart**: one container for raw water, another for filtered water. Do not swap the caps, and keep your hands clean.
4. **Filter** as the instructions say.
5. **Then disinfect** with a tablet or bleach whenever viruses are possible: water soiled by human waste, downstream of houses, after a flood or when traveling.
6. **Look after the filter**: backflush it when the flow drops, and dry it before storing it.

### Build a makeshift filter

It clears the water and improves its taste, nothing more. You must treat the water afterwards.

1. Cut the bottom off a plastic bottle and turn it upside down, neck at the bottom.
2. Plug the neck with a clean cloth.
3. Stack, from bottom to top: crushed charcoal (from a wood fire that has gone out), fine sand, coarse sand, then gravel.
4. Pour water in at the top and collect it under the neck. Run the first liters through again, as they come out cloudy.

## Never do this

- Drink water that only went through a cloth or a makeshift filter.
- Believe a filter removes everything: viruses, dissolved chemicals, metals and salt go through most portable filters.
- Rely on a filter pitcher: it does not hold back germs.
- Let a used filter freeze. Frost cracks it and you cannot see the damage: replace it.
- Filter seawater or water polluted by chemicals.

## Without equipment

- A clean T-shirt, a bandana or a clean sock can replace the cloth.
- With nothing at all, simply let the water settle and take the clear part from the top.

## Depending on where you are

- **At home**: clear the water, then boil it. That is safer than a filter alone.
- **Outdoors**: a squeeze filter and a few tablets weigh little and cover almost every case.
- **In winter**: keep the filter against your body during the day and in your sleeping bag at night.
- **When traveling**: where sanitation is poor, viruses are common in water. Filter, then disinfect.
