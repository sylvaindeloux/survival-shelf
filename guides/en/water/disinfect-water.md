# Disinfect water

> **In short**: with no fire to boil water, add 2 drops of unscented household bleach (5 to 9 %) per liter of clear water, twice as much if the water is cloudy or very cold, and wait 30 minutes. Or use purification tablets as their instructions say. This kills bacteria and viruses, is weak against some parasites and removes no chemicals.

## When to use it

- You cannot boil the water: no fire, no fuel, too much water.
- After a portable filter, to kill the viruses it lets through.
- For stored water you have doubts about.

## What you need

- Liquid household bleach, **unscented, with no added cleaner**, less than a year old. Read its strength on the label: it changes the dose.
- A dropper or a syringe: 1 drop is about 0.05 ml, so 20 drops make 1 ml.
- Or water purification tablets that have not expired.
- A clean container that closes, and a watch.

## Steps

### With bleach

1. **Clear the water** if it is cloudy (see [Filter water](filter-water.md)).
2. **Read the label** to find the strength of your bleach (sodium hypochlorite or active chlorine), and check that it is unscented and has no additives.
3. **Add the dose for that strength** to clear water:
   - 5 to 9 % (common in North America): 2 drops per liter or quart, 8 drops per US gallon.
   - About 2.6 % (common in France): 4 drops per liter.
   - 1 %: 10 drops per liter.
4. **Double the dose** if the water is still cloudy, colored or very cold.
5. **Mix**, then loosen the cap a little and tip the container so that treated water rinses the neck too.
6. **Wait 30 minutes**, and 1 to 2 hours if the water is very cold or cloudy.
7. **Smell the water**: a slight chlorine smell should remain. If not, add the same dose again and wait 15 more minutes. If there is still no smell, throw that water away: your bleach is too old or the water too dirty.
8. To soften the taste, let the water stand for a few hours in a clean container, or pour it back and forth a few times.

### With tablets

1. **Read the instructions**: how many liters one tablet treats (often 1 liter) and how long to wait.
2. Drop the tablet into clear water, close, shake, and rinse the neck with treated water.
3. **Wait** for the stated time: usually 30 minutes for chlorine tablets (NaDCC), up to 2 hours in very cold water, and up to 4 hours for chlorine dioxide if you also want to deal with parasites.

Tablets that contain only silver salts keep clean water clean: they do not disinfect.

### With sunlight, as a last resort

1. Take a clear plastic (PET) bottle of 2 liters (half a gallon) at most, clean and not scratched.
2. Fill it with clear water and close it.
3. Lay it on its side in full sun, on a dark surface or a metal sheet if you can, for 6 hours. Leave it for 2 days if the sky is more than half covered. The method does not work in continuous rain.
4. Drink the water straight from that bottle.

### The limits

- Chlorine and iodine are weak against some parasites, such as Cryptosporidium. Chlorine dioxide does better, and boiling kills them all.
- The best combination without fire: filter (1 micron or less), then disinfect.
- No disinfectant removes chemicals, metals or salt.

## Never do this

- Use scented bleach, gel bleach, bleach with added cleaners, or bleach tablets made for cleaning.
- Use swimming pool chlorine tablets.
- Mix bleach with another product: it gives off toxic gases.
- Add more "to be sure", or drink before the waiting time is over.
- Use iodine if you are pregnant or have a thyroid problem, for a child, or for more than a few weeks.

## Without equipment

- No dropper: for a large amount, 1 level teaspoon (5 ml) of 5 to 9 % bleach treats about 40 liters (10 US gallons) of clear water.
- No bleach and no tablets: boil the water, or use sunlight.

## Depending on where you are

- **At home**: write the purchase date on the bleach bottle and keep a dropper with your reserve.
- **In the car and in your go-bag**: tablets travel better than a bottle of bleach, which can leak.
- **Outdoors**: mountain water is cold, so double the waiting time.
- **When traveling**: tablets back up a filter against viruses.

Where the doses come from: the CDC gives 2 drops per liter for 5 to 9 % bleach and 10 drops per liter for 1 % bleach, about 5 mg of chlorine per liter of water. The dose for 2.6 % bleach is worked out from the same target. A French health agency describes a weaker dose: this guide keeps the CDC one, which is more cautious. For iodine, the EPA gives 5 drops of 2 % tincture of iodine per liter (10 if the water is cloudy) and a 30-minute wait. The EPA also explains how to use granular calcium hypochlorite, a product that is dangerous to handle: follow its page to the letter if you have nothing else.
