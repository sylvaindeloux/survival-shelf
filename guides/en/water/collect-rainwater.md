# Collect rainwater

> **In short**: rainwater is first of all for everything except drinking: flushing, cleaning, watering. It is not necessarily safe to drink, and some countries forbid drinking it. In a real shortage, rain that falls straight into a clean container can be drunk after treatment. Be wary of water that ran off a roof.

## When to use it

- A water cut drags on and you want to keep your safe water for drinking.
- You are outdoors with no water point and it is raining.

## What you need

- Clean, wide containers: buckets, basins, pots, bowls.
- A clean tarp, poncho or large plastic bag.
- For a lasting setup: a closed rain barrel connected to a downspout, with a screen and a mosquito net.

## Steps

### In an emergency, with no setup

1. **Put your containers out** as soon as it rains, in the open, away from trees and roof edges.
2. **Stretch a tarp** on a slope, or as a funnel, toward a bucket. Let the rain rinse it for a minute or two before collecting.
3. **Estimate what you get**: 1 mm of rain on 1 m² gives 1 liter. A 10 mm shower on a tarp of 2 m by 3 m gives about 60 liters (16 US gallons).
4. **Cover the containers** as soon as the rain stops.
5. **To drink it**, as a last resort: pour the water through a clean cloth, then boil it for 1 minute or disinfect it (see [Make water safe to drink](make-water-safe-to-drink.md)).

### With a roof and a gutter

1. **Let the first minutes of rain run off**: they wash dust and bird droppings off the roof.
2. **Screen the water** where it enters the barrel, and close the barrel with a lid and a mosquito net. Without protection, empty it at least every 10 days so you do not breed mosquitoes.
3. **Use this water** for flushing, cleaning floors, watering and washing the car.
4. **Do not drink it** if the roof contains asbestos or lead: boiling removes neither fibers nor metals.
5. **Maintain the setup**: clean the screens, and empty and disinfect the barrel once a year. Never connect it to your drinking-water pipes, and label its taps "Not drinking water".

## Never do this

- Drink rainwater without treating it.
- Drink water from a roof that contains asbestos or lead, even boiled.
- Collect rain for drinking during or just after a fire, an industrial accident or smoke fallout: it brings down the pollutants in the air.
- Use untreated rainwater for cooking, washing dishes or washing yourself.
- Connect a rainwater tank to drinking-water pipes.

## Without equipment

- A poncho, a tent fly, a split trash bag or a waterproof jacket stretched between four points already collects several liters.
- A clean cloth left in the rain and wrung out gives a few mouthfuls to drink.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment**: put basins out and stretch a tarp on the balcony. Never balance anything on a window ledge.
- **In a house**: a rain barrel under a downspout supplies flushing and cleaning water during a cut.
- **Outdoors**: rain collected in a clean container is the cleanest natural water you will find. Treat it anyway if you can.
- **At sea or on the coast**: rinse the salt off the tarp with the first drops.
