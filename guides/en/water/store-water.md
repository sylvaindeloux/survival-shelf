# Store water

> **In short**: sealed store-bought bottled water, kept cool and away from light, is the simplest and safest reserve. If you store tap water, use clean, sanitized food-grade containers, write the date on them and replace the water every 6 months.

## When to use it

- Now, before anything happens: water is the first reserve to build.
- When a water cut, a storm or a flood is announced: fill everything you can while the tap still runs.

## What you need

- Packs of bottled water, or food-grade containers: jerry cans and drums sold for drinking water (HDPE plastic, recycling code 2, or the glass-and-fork symbol in Europe), well-rinsed plastic soda or water bottles.
- Unscented household bleach and dish soap to prepare the containers.
- A marker or labels.

## Steps

### With bottled water

1. Buy the amount worked out for your household (see [Work out how much water you need](work-out-how-much-water-you-need.md)).
2. Keep the packs sealed, in a cool, dry and dark place, away from fuel, paint and pesticides: smells seep through plastic.
3. Rotate your reserve: drink the oldest bottles and buy new ones as you go, keeping to the date printed on the bottle.

### With tap water

1. **Choose good containers**: food-grade plastic, sturdy, with a cap that closes tightly. Several containers of 5 to 20 liters (1 to 5 gallons) are better than one huge one: 1 liter weighs 1 kilo, and a gallon weighs over 8 lb.
2. **Wash them** with water and dish soap, cap included, then rinse.
3. **Sanitize them**: mix 1 teaspoon (5 ml) of unscented bleach at 5 to 9 % in 1 liter (1 quart) of water. With weaker bleach (about 2.6 %), use 2 teaspoons. Pour the mix into the container, close it and shake so it touches every surface and the cap. Wait at least 30 seconds, pour it out and let the container air-dry.
4. **Fill from the cold tap**, to the top, without touching the inside of the neck or the cap. Mains water is already treated: add nothing.
5. **Close, then write** "Drinking water" and today's date.
6. **Store** somewhere cool (10 to 21 °C, 50 to 70 °F, if you can), out of the light, away from fuel and chemicals. Do not put containers straight on concrete: stand them on boards.
7. **Replace every 6 months**: empty (water the garden with it), wash, sanitize and refill.

If your water comes from a well or a private spring, disinfect it before storing it (see [Disinfect water](disinfect-water.md)).

### When a water cut is announced

1. Fill every clean container: bottles, pots, bowls, buckets.
2. Fill the bathtub and the sinks. That water is for flushing and cleaning: do not drink it without treating it.
3. If contamination of the mains is announced, close your main water valve to keep the water already in your home clean.

### When you use it

Pour the water, or take it out with a clean ladle. Never dip your hands in it and never drink straight from a big container.

## Never do this

- Reuse a container that held chemicals, bleach, fuel or pesticides, even after washing.
- Use milk jugs: they are hard to clean and their plastic turns brittle.
- Choose glass for a reserve you may have to move: it is heavy and it breaks.
- Leave your reserve in the sun, near a heat source or next to fuel cans.
- Add bleach "to preserve" tap water that is already treated.

## Without equipment

- No jerry can: soda or water bottles of 1.5 to 2 liters, washed and sanitized, do the job very well.
- No room: slide packs under the beds and into the bottom of cupboards.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment**: spread the packs under beds and at the bottom of cupboards rather than on a high shelf. Keep a few small bottles that are easy to grab if you have to evacuate.
- **In a house**: a basement or a cool pantry works well. Avoid the garage if you keep fuel or chemicals there.
- **In the car**: keep a few small bottles and replace them often. A car in the sun is too hot for a real reserve.
- **Where it freezes**: leave about 10 % of empty space in the container, because water expands when it freezes.
