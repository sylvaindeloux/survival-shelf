# Work out how much water you need

> **In short**: plan 3 liters of safe water per person per day for drinking and preparing food, or 4 liters (about 1 US gallon) to cover basic hygiene too. Keep at least 3 days' worth and aim for 2 weeks. Double it in very hot weather.

## When to use it

- To build your water reserve before anything happens.
- To see how many days your current stock would last.
- To get ready to leave: evacuation, long drive, hike.

## What you need

- The number of people and pets in your household.
- How long you want to hold out: 3 days at the very least, 2 weeks if you have room.
- Something to write on, or the Survival Shelf app, which does the math for you.

## Steps

1. **Pick a daily figure per person.**
   - 2 liters (about half a gallon): the bare minimum for drinking. The French emergency kit is built on it (6 liters for 3 days).
   - 3 liters (about three quarters of a gallon): drinking and preparing food. This is the WHO survival figure (2.5 to 3 liters) and the Swiss emergency plan figure (9 liters per person). It is also the app's default.
   - 4 liters (about 1 US gallon): drinking, cooking and basic hygiene. This is the figure used in the United States (1 gallon) and in Quebec (12 liters for 3 days).
   - 7.5 to 15 liters (2 to 4 gallons): drinking, cooking and washing when a crisis lasts (WHO, Sphere standards).
2. **Multiply**: people × days × liters per day.
3. **Add your pets**: about 50 ml per kilo of body weight per day, so 0.25 liter for a cat and 1.5 liters for a dog of 25 to 30 kg (55 to 65 lb).
4. **Add a margin** for those who need more: children, pregnant or breastfeeding women, sick people (fever, diarrhea, vomiting), anyone doing physical work. In very hot weather, needs can double.
5. **Count the water in your meals**: pasta, rice, dehydrated meals and powdered milk need extra water. Ready-to-eat canned food does not.
6. **Convert into bottles or containers**, so you know what to buy and where it will fit.

Example: 4 people for 3 days at 3 liters a day need 4 × 3 × 3 = 36 liters (about 9.5 US gallons). For 2 weeks: 4 × 14 × 3 = 168 liters (about 44 US gallons).

## Never do this

- Ration drinking water. Drink what you need today and look for more for tomorrow. To use less, slow down and stay cool.
- Count soda, coffee or alcohol instead of water.
- Forget hygiene: keep water for washing your hands, or illness will follow quickly.

## Depending on where you are

- **At home**: keep at least 3 days of water and aim for 2 weeks if you have room. The water heater and the pipes can add to it (see [Find water at home](find-water-at-home.md)).
- **In the car**: take water for every passenger before a long drive, and more in summer or when you might get stuck (snow, traffic jam).
- **Outdoors**: 1 liter weighs 1 kilo (a gallon weighs over 8 lb), so you cannot carry several days of water. Count at least 2 liters a day even in cold weather, spot the water points on your route and carry something to treat water.
- **In hostile environments** (desert, extreme heat): needs double, sometimes more. Move during the cool hours and stay in the shade the rest of the time.
