# Recognize warning signals

> **In short**: an alert on your phone or a siren means: stop, get the instruction, apply it at once. Read the whole message, go indoors unless you are told to leave, and turn on the radio. Learn your local signals now, because they differ from one country to the next.

## When to use it

- You hear a siren or your phone sounds with an alert message.
- Your weather service issues an orange (amber) or red warning.
- To teach the signals to the whole household before you need them.

## What you need

- A battery or hand-crank radio, with spare batteries.
- A charged phone. Government alerts on mobile phones need no sign-up and no app.

## Steps

### An alert on your phone

1. **Stop what you are doing and read the whole message.** It gives the danger, the area and what to do.
2. **Apply the instruction** without waiting for confirmation.
3. **Driving? Do not read it at the wheel.** Stop somewhere safe and legal first, or listen to live radio.
4. Updates may follow, then an end-of-alert message.

How it looks depends on the country:

- **United Kingdom, Emergency Alerts**: a loud siren-like sound and a vibration for up to 10 seconds on 4G and 5G phones, with a message that stays on screen until you acknowledge it. Only the government and the emergency services can send them.
- **United States, Wireless Emergency Alerts**: short text-like messages sent to compatible phones in the threatened area, for dangerous weather and other critical situations.
- **France, FR-Alert**: a notification on every phone in the danger zone, with the danger, the authority, the place and the instruction.

### A siren

1. **Go indoors at once**, unless local instructions say otherwise (near a dam, for example, the signal means go to high ground).
2. **Close doors and windows** and stay away from the glass.
3. **Turn on the radio** or the public television channel for instructions.
4. **Leave children at school**: the staff protect them there.
5. **Phone only for a life-threatening emergency**, so that the network stays available.
6. **Wait for the all-clear** before going out.

Example, the French national signal: a rising and falling sound in three sequences of 1 minute 41 seconds each. The all-clear is a continuous 30-second sound. The monthly test (first Wednesday) is a single sequence.

### Weather warnings

Many weather services use colors. In the French system:

- **Green**: no particular warning.
- **Yellow**: be attentive if you are doing something exposed to the weather.
- **Orange**: be very careful, dangerous weather is expected.
- **Red**: absolute vigilance, weather of exceptional intensity.

Check which scale your own weather service uses and where it is published.

## Never do this

- Go and fetch your children from school during the alert.
- Stay in your car, near windows or outside to watch.
- Light a flame or take the elevator.
- Leave your shelter without instructions, or turn back during a flood.
- Pass on a rumor: rely on official sources.
- Switch off government alerts on your phone.

## Without equipment

- No phone and no radio: a siren is enough to know that you must take shelter. Then ask a neighbor with a radio for the instruction.
- Phone battery flat: once the alert is over, a car radio picks up the same stations.

## Depending on where you are

- **Near an industrial site, a dam or a power plant**: ask your local authority for the instructions specific to the site.
- **Abroad**: find out on arrival how alerts are given, and register with your embassy or consulate if it offers a travelers' service.
