# Make a household plan

> **In short**: in one hour, agree at home on four things: the risks near you, two meeting places, one out-of-area contact everybody calls, and who does what (children, vulnerable people, pets, shutting off water, gas and electricity). Write it down, post it, and review it twice a year.

## When to use it

- Now, while things are calm: a plan is made before the crisis.
- After moving, a birth, a change of school or job.
- When someone in the household becomes more vulnerable (age, illness, disability).

## What you need

- One hour with everyone in the household, children included.
- Paper and a pen, or a family plan template from your civil protection agency or the Red Cross.
- Useful numbers: relatives, school, doctor, insurer, local authority.

## Steps

1. **List the risks around your home**: flood, storm, wildfire, earthquake, industrial site, dam. Your local authority or civil protection agency publishes them.
2. **Choose two meeting places**: one right outside your home, for a sudden emergency such as a fire; another outside your neighborhood, in case you cannot get home. Write down the route and an alternate route.
3. **Name an out-of-area contact**, who lives far enough away not to be affected. Everyone calls or texts that person with their news, and learns the number by heart. A text often gets through when calls do not.
4. **Share out the roles**: who takes the go-bag, who looks after the pets, who helps the elderly or sick person, who follows the news.
5. **Talk about the children**: find out where the school or daycare evacuates to and how it will contact you. Decide who picks them up, and tell the school.
6. **Show everyone, adults and children, how to shut off water, gas and electricity**, and where the fire extinguisher is (see [Shut off gas, water and electricity](../fire-and-power/shut-off-gas-water-and-electricity.md)).
7. **Spot the safe places**: the room where you would shelter in place, the exits from your home, the routes out of your neighborhood.
8. **Prepare the kit and the documents** (see [Pack a go-bag](pack-a-go-bag.md) and [Keep your documents and contacts safe](keep-your-documents-and-contacts-safe.md)).
9. **Write the plan** on one page. Post emergency numbers near the phone and give everyone a contact card to carry at all times.
10. **Practice and update**: review the plan with the whole household every six months, and drive your evacuation route twice a year.

## Never do this

- Keep the plan in your head: if it is not written down and known to all, it is useless on the day you are away.
- Rely on the phone alone: the network can fail or be overloaded.
- Forget people who do not live with you but count on you: an elderly parent, an isolated neighbor.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment**: locate the stairs and emergency exits. Elevators must not be used during a fire or an alert.
- **In a house**: also locate the outside gas valve, the water meter and the electrical panel.
- **If you live alone**: agree with a neighbor or a relative that they will check on you in a crisis.
- **With pets**: plan where they can stay, because many emergency shelters accept only service animals. Keep a list of pet-friendly places.
- **Abroad**: write down the contact details of your consulate.
