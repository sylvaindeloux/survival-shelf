# Decide whether to stay or go

> **In short**: follow the authorities first: if they say evacuate, leave at once; if they say shelter in place, stay indoors. With no instruction, leave when the danger reaches your home (fire, rising water, damaged building, gas leak) and stay sheltered when the danger is outside (toxic cloud, storm). Either way, decide early.

## When to use it

- An alert has just been issued and you hesitate.
- A danger is coming: flood, wildfire, storm, industrial accident.
- To prepare both scenarios in advance with your household.

## What you need

- A battery radio and a charged phone to follow instructions.
- Your go-bag (see [Pack a go-bag](pack-a-go-bag.md)).
- To shelter in place: wide tape, plastic sheeting or cloths.
- A vehicle with at least half a tank of fuel, if you have one.

## Steps

### Choose

1. **Listen for the instruction** on the radio, on your phone or from responders. Authorities order an evacuation only when they think you are in danger: do not wait.
2. **With no instruction, ask yourself three questions**:
   - Is the danger in your home, or about to reach it? If so, leave.
   - Is the outside air dangerous (smoke, toxic cloud), or is the road riskier than your home? If so, stay.
   - Can you hold out where you are with your water, food, warmth and medicines? If not, prepare to leave while roads are still open.
3. **Decide early.** Leaving in time is safer than leaving at the last moment.

### If you stay (shelter in place)

1. Bring everyone inside, pets included. Close and lock doors and windows.
2. **Turn off fans, heating and air conditioning**, which pull outside air in. Close vents and fireplace dampers.
3. Settle in an interior room with as few windows as possible. For a chemical cloud, French guidance prefers an upper floor.
4. **Seal the openings** (under the door, vents) with tape and plastic sheeting or cloths.
5. Listen to the radio until the all-clear or an order to evacuate.
6. A sealed room cannot be used for long: US guidance counts about 1 m² (10 sq ft) of floor per person for up to 5 hours of air, and officials are unlikely to recommend more than 2 to 3 hours. Air the room as soon as the alert is over.

### If you go (evacuation)

1. **Take the essentials**: ID for everyone, copies of important documents, medicines and prescriptions, phone, go-bag. Put on sturdy shoes, long pants and long sleeves.
2. **Shut off water, gas and electricity** if you are told to or if the home is damaged (see [Shut off gas, water and electricity](../fire-and-power/shut-off-gas-water-and-electricity.md)).
3. Lock doors and windows. Leave a note saying when you left and where you are going.
4. **Tell a contact** who lives outside the area.
5. **Follow the recommended routes**, with no shortcuts. Never drive into a flooded area and stay away from downed power lines.
6. Take one car per household if you can. Offer a seat to a neighbor without a vehicle.
7. Take your pets, knowing that some public shelters accept only service animals.

## Never do this

- "Wait and see" once the order to evacuate is given.
- Go and fetch your children from school during the alert: the staff shelter them there.
- Drive through a flooded road: a few centimeters of water are enough to carry a car away.
- Take the elevator or turn back.
- Stay for hours in a sealed room without airing it after the alert.

## Depending on where you are

- **Flood**: go upstairs with your bag. Do not go down to a basement or an underground garage.
- **In an apartment**: if smoke fills the stairwell, stay in, close the door and show yourself at the window (see [Put out a small fire](../fire-and-power/put-out-a-small-fire.md)).
- **With a car**: always keep at least half a tank of fuel and know several ways out.
- **With a vulnerable person**: arrange in advance who comes to help and how the person will travel.
