# Call the emergency services

> **In short**: get to safety, then call your emergency number (112 in Europe, 911 in North America). Say where you are, what is happening, how many people are hurt and how they are. Answer the questions and never hang up first. If you cannot speak, use the text service of your country if there is one.

## When to use it

- A life is in danger: collapse, serious injury, fire, drowning, accident, assault.
- You witness a danger to other people.
- When in doubt, call: the person on the line decides what help to send.

## What you need

- A phone, landline or mobile. Emergency calls are free.
- Your exact address, or landmarks to describe where you are.
- The emergency numbers of your country, written down before you need them.

## Steps

1. **Get to safety**, you and the others, before calling: away from the fire, the smoke, the road.
2. **Dial the emergency number**:
   - **112**: everywhere in the European Union, from landlines and mobiles, next to the national numbers. It also works in some other countries.
   - **911**: United States and Canada.
   - Elsewhere: find out the local number now and write it in your contact list.
3. **Say where you are**: town, street, number, floor, room or apartment. Outside a town, give landmarks: road, marker, trail, visible building.
4. **Say what is happening**: collapse, fall, fire, road accident.
5. **Say how many people are hurt and how they look**: conscious or not, breathing or not, bleeding.
6. **Give the number you are calling from**, so that they can call you back.
7. **Answer the questions and follow the instructions**: the operator can guide you through first actions.
8. **Never hang up first.** Wait until you are told you can.
9. **Keep your phone free and switched on.** The emergency services may call you back: pick up.

Called by mistake? Do not hang up: simply explain that everything is fine.

## Never do this

- Hang up before you are told to.
- Call an emergency number for simple information: you block a line.
- Assume that someone else has already called.
- Put yourself back in danger to make the call.

## Without equipment

- **No signal**: move to a higher or more open place if you can do it safely, or send someone to get help.
- **Unable to speak** (danger, injury, disability): many countries have a text or app service. In the United States, text 911 where the service exists (you get an automatic reply if it does not): call if you can, text if you can't. In France, text 114.
- **No phone**: ask a neighbor, a passer-by, a shop. A whistle or repeated shouts help people find you.

## Depending on where you are

- **In the European Union**: operators often also answer in English or French, and they can locate the caller. When you arrive in another EU country, a text message reminds you of the 112.
- **In the United States**: a mobile phone without a plan can still call 911, but the center cannot locate it or call it back: give your position first.
- **At sea**: use channel 16 on the VHF radio.
- **In the mountains or in a forest**: before you leave, tell someone where you are going and when you will be back. With no signal, that person will raise the alarm.
