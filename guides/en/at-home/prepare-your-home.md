# Prepare your home

> **In short**: find out where to shut off the water, the gas and the electricity, install smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, secure what can fall and keep the exits clear. Choose the room where you will shelter and the place for your supplies and your go-bag. Do it now: on the day of the crisis, it will be too late.

## When to use it

- Now, calmly, then once a year.
- When you move in, or after building work.
- When a weather warning announces a storm, a flood or extreme cold.

## What you need

- An hour or two, with the people of the household.
- Labels and a marker, to mark the valves and the main breaker.
- Smoke alarms, a battery carbon monoxide alarm, a fire extinguisher.
- Fixings for tall furniture, wide adhesive tape.

## Steps

### Know the risks

1. **Ask your local authority** which risks are known where you live: flood, wildfire, earthquake, industrial site, dam.
2. **Look at your home with these risks in mind**: a ground floor or basement that can flood, trees close by, dry vegetation around the house.

### Utilities

1. **Find the gas valve, the main water valve and the main breaker.** Label them.
2. **Show everyone, adults and children, how to shut them off** (see [Shut off gas, water and electricity](../fire-and-power/shut-off-gas-water-and-electricity.md)).
3. **List what depends on power**: heating, cooking, medical device, garage door, gate, roller shutters, electric lock. Learn how to open the doors and gates by hand.

### Fire and carbon monoxide

1. **Install smoke alarms** and test them every month (see [Home fire](../disasters/home-fire.md)).
2. **Add a battery carbon monoxide alarm** if you have a fuel-burning appliance: boiler, stove, fireplace, gas water heater.
3. **Have the heating and the flues checked** by a professional every year, and never block the air vents.
4. **Keep a fire extinguisher** in a place the whole household knows.

### What can fall, blow away or be flooded

1. **Secure tall furniture**, shelves, the television, mirrors and the water heater. Store heavy or fragile objects low down.
2. **Before a storm**, bring in or tie down what the wind can carry off, cut back dangerous branches and clean the gutters.
3. **In a flood-prone area**, move documents, valuables, medicines and supplies upstairs or up high, in waterproof boxes.
4. **In a wildfire area**, clear the brush around the house (see [Wildfire](../disasters/wildfire.md)).

### Shelter, supplies and exits

1. **Choose the room to shelter in**: on an upper floor, with as few windows as possible. Keep adhesive tape and cloths there (see [Shelter in place at home](shelter-in-place-at-home.md)).
2. **Store your supplies** of water and food in a cool, dry place that is easy to reach (see [Store water](../water/store-water.md)).
3. **Put the go-bag near the exit**, in a place you can find in the dark.
4. **Next to each bed**: a flashlight, sturdy shoes, glasses.
5. **Find two ways out** of the home, and keep them clear.
6. **Give a spare set of keys** to someone you trust.
7. **Photograph your valuables**, write down their serial numbers and keep a copy of your insurance policies with your important documents.

## Never do this

- Wait for the warning to look for the gas valve or the breaker.
- Block the air vents to keep the heat in.
- Store gasoline in the home or the basement, or a propane cylinder indoors (see [Store fuel safely](../fire-and-power/store-fuel-safely.md)).
- Clutter corridors, stairs and exits.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment**: see [Hold out in an apartment](hold-out-in-an-apartment.md).
- **In a house**: see [Hold out in a house](hold-out-in-a-house.md).
- **Tenant or co-owner**: ask the landlord, the caretaker or the building manager where the building's valves and shutoffs are.
- **In France**: the town hall and the prefecture publish the known risks of each municipality. The official minimum supply is 6 liters (about 1.6 gallons) of water per person for 3 days.
