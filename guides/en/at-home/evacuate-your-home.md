# Evacuate your home

> **In short**: when the authorities tell you to evacuate, leave at once. Take everyone's identity documents, medicines and prescriptions, your phone and the go-bag. Lock the home, leave a note with the time and your destination, tell a relative and follow the indicated routes, with no shortcuts. Do not come back before you are allowed to.

## When to use it

- An evacuation order or instruction: flood, wildfire, industrial accident, risk of collapse, explosive device.
- The danger is in your home or is about to reach it: fire, rising water, damaged building, gas leak.
- The home is no longer livable: too cold, too hot, no water, a medical device without power.

## What you need

- Each person's go-bag (see [Pack a go-bag](../basics/pack-a-go-bag.md)).
- Identity documents, copies of important documents, medicines and prescriptions, phone and charger.
- Sturdy shoes and clothes that cover you.
- A destination decided in advance: a relative or friend outside the area, or the reception center named by the authorities.

## Steps

### If you have a few minutes

1. **Gather everyone**, pets included, and count heads.
2. **Take the essentials**: documents, medicines, phone, go-bag, keys.
3. **Dress for outdoors**: closed shoes, long pants, long sleeves, protection against rain or cold.
4. **Shut off water, gas and electricity** if the authorities ask you to or if the home is damaged (see [Shut off gas, water and electricity](../fire-and-power/shut-off-gas-water-and-electricity.md)).
5. **Unplug electrical appliances.** Leave the fridge and the freezer plugged in, unless there is a risk of flooding.
6. **Close the windows and lock the doors.**
7. **Leave a note** in plain sight: the time you left and your destination.
8. **Text your out-of-area contact.**
9. **Check on the neighbors** who may need help or a seat in a car.

### If you have no time

1. **Get out at once** with the people and the pets. Grab the go-bag if it is on your way.
2. **Do not go back** for belongings.
3. **Tell the emergency services and your family** once you are safe.

### On the way

1. **Follow the routes indicated** by the authorities, with no shortcuts: other roads may be cut.
2. **Never enter a flooded area** and stay away from fallen power lines.
3. **Go to the assembly point or the reception center** that was announced, and report in when you arrive.
4. **Listen to the radio** to follow the instructions.

### Coming back

1. **Wait for permission** from the authorities.
2. **Do not enter a damaged building**, and do not turn the gas back on yourself: that is a professional's job.
3. **Throw out food** that was left without cold or touched by the water (see [Tell whether food is still safe to eat](../food/tell-whether-food-is-still-safe-to-eat.md)).

## Never do this

- Wait "to see" when the order to evacuate has been given.
- Take the elevator.
- Go and fetch your children from school during the alert: they are evacuated or sheltered with their class.
- Go back for a forgotten object.
- Announce on social media that your home is empty.
- Leave a pet tied up or shut in outdoors.

## Without equipment

- No bag ready: documents, medicines, phone, water, a warm garment. The rest can be replaced.
- No car: walk to the assembly point, ask a neighbor for a seat, or call the local authority if you cannot move on your own.

## Depending on where you are

- **In winter, if the heating is off**: before you leave, shut off the water supply and drain the pipes so that they do not freeze.
- **In an apartment building**: go down by the stairs. If smoke fills the stairwell, stay in your home with the door closed (see [Home fire](../disasters/home-fire.md)).
- **With a vulnerable person**: arrange in advance who will come to help and how the person will be transported (see [Protect the most vulnerable](protect-the-most-vulnerable.md)).
- **By car**: see [Evacuate by car](../in-the-car/evacuate-by-car.md).
