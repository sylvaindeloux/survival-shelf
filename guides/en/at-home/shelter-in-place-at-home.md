# Shelter in place at home

> **In short**: bring everyone inside, close doors and windows, turn off ventilation, heating and air conditioning, and settle in an upstairs room with as few windows as possible. Seal the openings with adhesive tape and cloths, listen to the radio, and do not go out before the all-clear. Do not go and fetch your children from school: the staff shelter them there.

## When to use it

- A siren sounds, or a phone alert or the authorities tell you to take shelter.
- A toxic cloud, smoke, an industrial or nuclear accident: the danger is in the air, outdoors.
- A storm, unrest in the street: outdoors is more dangerous than indoors.

## What you need

- A battery radio and a flashlight.
- Wide adhesive tape, cloths or towels.
- Water, food that needs no cooking, your medicines.
- A bucket with a lid and trash bags, if the toilet is outside the room.
- Something to keep the children busy.

## Steps

### Take shelter

1. **Go into the nearest building at once.** Bring everyone inside, pets included.
2. **Close doors, windows and shutters.**
3. **Turn off ventilation, heating and air conditioning**, which bring outdoor air in.
4. **Put out any flame** and do not smoke.
5. **Turn on the radio** and keep your phone for emergencies: do not overload the network.

### Choose the room according to the danger

1. **Toxic cloud, industrial or nuclear accident**: an upstairs room, without windows if possible.
2. **Storm, tornado**: the lowest level, in a small interior room, away from glass.
3. **Flood**: upstairs, never in the basement.
4. **Gunfire or explosions**: the basement, or a room with at least two walls between you and the outside (see [Armed conflict and shelling](../disasters/armed-conflict-and-shelling.md)).

### Seal the room against dangerous air

1. **Seal the openings** of the room: the bottom of the door, around the windows, the air vents, with adhesive tape and cloths.
2. **If breathing is hard**, cover your nose and mouth with a damp cloth.
3. **Count the air.** The US authorities count about 1 m² (10 square feet) of floor per person for up to 5 hours of air in a sealed room, and generally do not advise staying in it for more than 2 to 3 hours.
4. **Stay away from the windows.**

### Hold on, then come out

1. **Listen to the radio** until the all-clear: the end-of-alert signal of the siren, or a message from the authorities.
2. **If the order to evacuate comes**, leave at once (see [Evacuate your home](evacuate-your-home.md)).
3. **After the all-clear**, air the whole home and turn the ventilation back on.

## Never do this

- Go and fetch your children from school: you expose yourself and get in the way of the emergency services.
- Go out "to see", or stand at the window.
- Stay in your car: get into a building.
- Light a flame, a candle or a camping stove in a sealed room.
- Stay for hours in a sealed room after the all-clear.
- Phone without need.

## Without equipment

- No adhesive tape: wet towels and clothes, rolled at the bottom of the doors and over the air vents, already limit the air coming in.
- No radio: wait for the end-of-alert signal of the siren, or the instruction given by loudspeaker.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment**: the stairwell and the common areas are not a shelter against a toxic cloud. Stay in your home.
- **In a public place, at work, at school**: follow the instructions of the person in charge of the premises.
- **Sheltering for several days** (epidemic, unrest): there is nothing to seal. See [Epidemic and pandemic](../disasters/epidemic-and-pandemic.md) and [Live several days without water or electricity](live-several-days-without-water-or-electricity.md).
- **In France**: the warning can come from the siren or from an FR-Alert notification. The all-clear from the siren is a continuous 30-second sound.
