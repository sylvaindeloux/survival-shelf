# Hold out in an apartment

> **In short**: an apartment building can shelter you for several days if you have water and food. Without power, the elevator stops and, on the upper floors, the water may no longer come up: keep a supply of water, a flashlight for the stairs and what you need for an emergency toilet. No generator and no barbecue on the balcony. Find out which neighbors cannot get down the stairs on their own.

## When to use it

- A power or water outage, a storm, snow, sheltering in place: you stay at home, on an upper floor.
- Before a crisis, to adapt your supplies to life in an apartment building.

## What you need

- Bottled water, and containers to fill as soon as an outage is announced.
- One flashlight per person, for the stairs and the unlit common areas.
- A bucket, strong trash bags and cat litter for the emergency toilet.
- Food that can be eaten without cooking.
- A whistle, to signal to your neighbors.
- The numbers of the caretaker, the building manager and the neighbors on your floor, on paper.

## Steps

### Before

1. **Find the stairs, the emergency exits** and the way to the street. Walk it once in the dark, with a flashlight.
2. **Learn how to open by hand** the lobby door, the garage door and the roller shutters: entry keypads and electric doors stop with the power.
3. **Get to know your neighbors** and find out who will need help: elderly or disabled people, families with a baby.
4. **If you cannot get down the stairs on your own**, arrange in advance who will come to help you, and ask the caretaker or the building manager to warn you before the elevator is taken out of service.

### Without power

1. **Do not take the elevator** as soon as the power flickers: it can stop between two floors.
2. **Fill containers right away**: if the building's pumps stop, the water no longer comes up to the upper floors.
3. **Use a flashlight for light**, not a candle.
4. **Cook safely**: your gas stove if it works, otherwise eat cold. Never a camping stove, a barbecue or a generator indoors or on the balcony.
5. **Limit trips** up and down the stairs: group your outings and carry less each time.
6. **Take your trash down** in closed bags, without letting it pile up on the landing.

### Without water, or after an earthquake

1. **If the water is off but the sewers work**, flush with a bucket of non-drinking water.
2. **After an earthquake, do not use the toilet** until the building's drain pipes have been checked: sewage can back up into the floors below. Switch to the emergency toilet (see [Set up an emergency toilet](../hygiene/set-up-an-emergency-toilet.md)).

### With the neighbors

1. **Check on** those who live alone or cannot get down the stairs.
2. **Share information**: the one with a radio tells the floor.
3. **When you call for help**: give the address, the floor, the apartment number, the door code and the best entrance, and send someone to wait for the rescuers downstairs.

## Never do this

- Take the elevator during a fire, a power outage or after an earthquake.
- Use a barbecue, a camping stove or a generator on the balcony or in the apartment.
- Prop the stairwell doors open: they keep the smoke out.
- Go down to the underground parking garage during a flood.
- Go out into a smoke-filled stairwell: stay in your home with the door closed (see [Home fire](../disasters/home-fire.md)).

## Depending on where you are

- **On a high floor**: during a storm or a cyclone, stay away from the windows.
- **On the ground floor or in a basement apartment**: in a flood, move to the upper floors, to a neighbor's if you have to.
- **Smell of gas**: no switches and no phone indoors, get out and call from outside (see [Gas leak](../disasters/gas-leak.md)).

Walking the stairs in the dark, limiting trips and taking the trash down are common sense, with no official source.
