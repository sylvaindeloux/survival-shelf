# Hold out in a house

> **In short**: a house gives you space, an outdoor area and sometimes a wood stove or a fireplace, but it is more exposed and more isolated. Gather daily life in one room, protect the pipes from frost, set up the generator outdoors, away from windows, and never go down into a flooded basement. Tell your neighbors: if you need help, they are the closest.

## When to use it

- A power or water outage, a storm, snow, a flood, a heat wave: you stay at home, in a house.
- Before a crisis, to prepare the house and the land around it.

## What you need

- Supplies of water and food, a backup way of cooking.
- Dry wood if you have a wood stove or a fireplace, serviced and swept.
- A battery carbon monoxide alarm.
- If you have a generator: outdoor extension cords and fuel stored outside the house.
- Simple tools: a shovel, buckets, tarps, a headlamp.

## Steps

### Before

1. **Maintain the wood or gas heating**: a check and a chimney sweep once a year.
2. **Prepare the outside before a storm**: bring in or tie down what can blow away, cut back dangerous branches, clean the gutters and drains.
3. **Choose the spot for the generator**: outdoors, sheltered from the rain, away from windows and doors, yours and the neighbors'.
4. **Find the main water valve** and how to drain the pipes.
5. **Clear the brush** around the house if you live near a forest (see [Wildfire](../disasters/wildfire.md)).

### Without power

1. **Gather daily life in one room**, the easiest to heat, and close the others.
2. **Heat and cook without poisoning yourself**: a wood stove or fireplace in good condition; camping stove, barbecue and brazier outdoors only.
3. **Run the generator outdoors**, at least 6 m (20 ft) from any opening. Plug appliances directly into it, and stop it and let it cool before refueling.
4. **Protect the pipes from frost**: let a thin trickle of water run. Thaw a pipe with a hair dryer, never with a flame.
5. **If you have a private well**, avoid using its water while its equipment is without power.
6. **Open the electric gate and garage door by hand**, so that you are not locked in and your car is not stuck.

### According to the danger

1. **Storm**: close the shutters, stay away from glass, do not go up on the roof.
2. **Flood**: go upstairs with your supplies. Do not go down to the basement.
3. **Extreme cold, when you have to leave the house**: shut off the water supply, drain the pipes and pour antifreeze into the toilet bowl and tank and into the drain traps.
4. **Heat wave**: shutters and windows closed by day, wide open at night.

### Do not stay isolated

1. **Tell a relative or a neighbor** that you are staying, and agree on a time to check in.
2. **Check on** elderly neighbors or those who live alone.
3. **If the road is cut**, report it to the local authority and keep enough to hold out longer.

## Never do this

- Run a generator in the house, the garage, the basement or a conservatory, even with the door open.
- Plug a generator into a wall outlet.
- Go down into a flooded cellar or basement, or touch the electrical panel there.
- Go up on the roof during or just after a storm.
- Store gasoline in the house or the basement.
- Thaw a pipe with a flame.

## Depending on where you are

- **Isolated house**: help and repairs arrive later. Plan supplies for longer and a way of getting information without a network.
- **Septic tank out of service or sewers cut**: see [Set up an emergency toilet](../hygiene/set-up-an-emergency-toilet.md).
- **Yard**: rainwater can be collected for the toilet and for cleaning (see [Collect rainwater](../water/collect-rainwater.md)).
