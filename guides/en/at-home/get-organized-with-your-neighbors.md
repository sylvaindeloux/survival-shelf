# Get organized with your neighbors

> **In short**: in a crisis, the first people who can help you are your neighbors, and you are the first who can help them. Get to know each other now, swap numbers on paper, find out who will need help and who can do what. During the crisis, call on each other, share information and leave nobody alone.

## When to use it

- Now: neighbors who know each other look out for each other better.
- During an outage, a storm, a flood, a heat wave or a period of sheltering at home.
- When the emergency services are overwhelmed and help takes time to arrive.

## What you need

- A paper list of the neighbors: name, floor or house, phone number.
- A place to meet: lobby, courtyard, common room, in front of a house.
- A way to signal without a phone: a whistle, a note on the door.

## Steps

### Before

1. **Introduce yourself** to the neighbors on your floor, in your stairwell or in your street. A neighborhood party or a residents' meeting is a good occasion.
2. **Swap numbers**, on paper, and tell each other whom to warn if there is a problem.
3. **Find out who will need help**: elderly, disabled, sick or isolated people, families with a baby. Ask them what would help.
4. **Find out who can do what**: first aid, nursing, repairs, languages spoken. And who has what: a battery radio, a wood stove, tools, a vehicle.
5. **Talk about your plans**: where each household would go in an evacuation, who can give whom a ride.
6. **Build on what already exists**: residents' association, neighborhood group, club, school.

### During

1. **Go round** the neighbors as soon as it can be done safely, starting with those who live alone.
2. **Share information**: the one with a radio passes on the instructions, without distorting them.
3. **Agree on a daily meeting**: same time, same place, to count everyone and share out what is needed.
4. **Pool** what can be pooled: a hot meal, a way to charge phones, a heated room.
5. **Offer a seat in your car** to those without a vehicle if you evacuate.
6. **Keep an eye on empty homes** and report any suspicious behavior to the police.
7. **Call the emergency services** for a person who does not answer or who is unwell.

### After

1. **Ask after** those who were evacuated or taken to hospital.
2. **Help with the cleanup and the paperwork**, without putting yourselves in danger.
3. **Update the list** of numbers and talk about what was missing.

## Never do this

- Assume that someone else has been to see the elderly neighbor.
- Repeat a rumor to the whole building (see [Spot rumors and false information](../communication-and-navigation/spot-rumors-and-false-information.md)).
- Enter someone's home without their agreement, except in a life-threatening emergency.
- Take the law into your own hands: faced with a theft or an assault, call the police.
- Put yourselves in danger to help: rescuers are the ones who go into a damaged building, rising water or a fire.

## Without equipment

- No phone: a note slipped under the door, a round of the landings at a set time.
- A sign agreed in advance, such as a colored cloth at the window or on the door handle, can mean "I need help".

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment building**: the lobby or a common area can serve as the meeting point and the notice board.
- **In a house or in the countryside**: agree on a regular round, and on who has a vehicle that can get through.
- **You have just moved in**: simply introducing yourself is enough to start.
- **In France**: ask the town hall whether there is a "participation citoyenne" scheme, run with the police or the gendarmerie.
