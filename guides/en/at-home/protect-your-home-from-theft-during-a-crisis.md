# Protect your home from theft during a crisis

> **In short**: double-lock the door, even when you are in, leave nothing valuable in sight and do not say that your home is empty. Agree with your neighbors to look out for each other. Do not open to a stranger without checking who they are. Faced with a burglar, take no risks: your life matters more than your belongings.

## When to use it

- A power outage that switches off lighting and alarms, an evacuated neighborhood, empty homes after a flood or a storm.
- Fake officials, fake tradespeople or fake volunteers who turn up after a disaster.
- Before a crisis, to strengthen the entrances.

## What you need

- A reliable lock, a peephole and a door chain on the front door.
- Shutters or bars on the windows that can be reached.
- A flashlight, a whistle, a charged phone.
- Photos and serial numbers of your valuables.

## Steps

### Before

1. **Strengthen the entrances**: reliable lock, peephole, door chain, shutters or bars, motion-activated lighting, alarm.
2. **Hide no key outside**: not under the doormat, not in the mailbox, not in a flowerpot.
3. **Lock away** ladders and tools, which can be used to force a way in.
4. **Put away** cash, jewelry, documents and car keys, far from the entrances and out of sight from the windows. Tell nobody where they are.
5. **Photograph your valuables** and write down their serial numbers.
6. **Cut back the vegetation** that could hide someone near the doors and windows.

### During the crisis

1. **Double-lock the door**, even when you are at home. Do not leave the key in the lock of a glazed door.
2. **Close the shutters** in the evening and the windows whenever you go out.
3. **Without power**, the alarm and the automatic lighting no longer work: an object that rings or falls when a door or a gate is pushed, such as a small bell, is enough to warn you.
4. **Stay discreet**: nothing on social media about your absence, your supplies or your equipment.
5. **Look out for each other**: tell your neighbors about unusual comings and goings. Faced with suspicious behavior, note the vehicle (type, color, license plate) and the description of the people, then call the police.

### Someone knocks at the door

1. **Look through the peephole** and keep the door chain on.
2. **Ask for a professional ID card** or a work order, even from a person in uniform.
3. **When in doubt, do not open.** Suggest another appointment and check by calling the organization on the number printed on your own bills, never on the one the person gives you.
4. **If you let someone in**, ask a neighbor to be present and do not leave the person alone in the home.

### If you have to leave

1. **Close windows and shutters and lock the doors.**
2. **Take** documents, means of payment and small valuables with you.
3. **Tell a neighbor who is staying**, or the caretaker.
4. **Do not announce your departure** on social media or on your voicemail greeting.

### Faced with a burglary

1. **The burglars are still there**: take no risks. Do not go in, move away and call the police.
2. **You are at home when they come in**: do not resist, do not fight for your belongings. As soon as you can, shout or blow a whistle to call for help.
3. **Remember what you can**: number of people, height, clothes, distinguishing features, vehicle.
4. **Afterward**: touch nothing, call the police, take photos, file a report and have the stolen cards and checkbooks blocked.

## Never do this

- Set a trap meant to injure, such as broken glass on top of a wall. It makes you liable and cannot tell a thief from a child, a neighbor or a rescuer.
- Fight to defend belongings.
- Open to a stranger without checking who they are.
- Say where you keep your money, or how much you have in store.
- Stand guard alone outdoors at night.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment**: do not let a person you do not know into the building, and close the lobby door behind you.
- **In a house**: also lock the garage, the garden shed and the side gates. An electric gate does not always lock without power: check it.
- **Flooded or damaged home**: do not stay to guard it if the authorities ask you to evacuate.
- **In France**: the police and gendarmerie number is 17, or 112.
