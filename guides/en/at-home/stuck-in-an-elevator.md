# Stuck in an elevator

> **In short**: stay calm: the car is ventilated, you will not run out of air. Press the alarm or call button, and wait for help away from the doors. Never try to open the doors or to get out by yourself.

## When to use it

- The car stops between two floors or the doors no longer open.
- A power outage, a breakdown, an earthquake.
- Someone else is stuck and is calling you for help.

## Steps

### You are in the car

1. **Breathe calmly.** An elevator is ventilated: you will not run out of air.
2. **Press the button of another floor**: this is sometimes enough to get the car moving again.
3. **Press the alarm or call button.** It puts you through to a response service: give the address, say how many of you there are and whether anyone is unwell.
4. **If nobody answers**, call the emergency services with your phone if it has a signal.
5. **If nothing works**, shout or knock on the walls of the car, pausing to listen.
6. **Move away from the doors** and sit down if the wait is long.
7. **Keep yourself and the others busy**: breathe slowly, talk, reassure the children.
8. **Agree to get out** only with the help of a trained person: an elevator technician or a firefighter.

### Someone else is stuck

1. **Tell** the caretaker or the building's security service, or call the emergency services.
2. **Talk to them** through the door: say that help is coming and that they must not try to get out.
3. **Do not try to get them out yourself.**
4. **Stay there** until help arrives.

## Never do this

- Force or pry open the doors of the car.
- Get out by your own means from a car stopped between two floors.
- Go through the hatch in the ceiling.
- Hold the doors of an elevator open with an arm or a leg.
- Take the elevator during a fire, a power outage or after an earthquake.

## Without equipment

- Alarm silent and phone without a signal: knock on the walls at regular intervals and call out, then listen.
- Save your phone battery: it will also be your light if the lighting goes out.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: since 2013, every elevator must have a remote alarm linking the car to a response service, along with emergency lighting in the car, and the maintenance contract provides for freeing trapped people 7 days a week, 24 hours a day.
- **If you depend on the elevator** to leave your home: ask the caretaker or the building manager to warn you before a planned shutdown, and arrange who will help you on the stairs.
- **When an outage or a storm is forecast**: take the stairs.
