# Look after your pets

> **In short**: for each animal, prepare a kit that is easy to carry: a carrier or a leash, the health record, a photo, medicines, water and food for 7 days. If you evacuate, take your pet with you, knowing that not all shelters accept animals. As a last resort, leave it loose indoors with water and food, never tied up outside.

## When to use it

- An evacuation, sheltering in place, a power or water outage, a heat wave, extreme cold.
- An unexpected absence: a hospital stay, no way of getting home.
- Before a crisis, to prepare the kit and a place that will take your pet.

## What you need

- A carrier, or a leash and a harness.
- The health record with the vaccinations, a photo of the animal, the list of its medicines.
- Water and food for 7 days, bowls, a can opener.
- For a cat: a litter box and litter.
- A familiar object: a blanket, a toy.
- The name and number of a boarding kennel or of a relative or friend who can take it in.

## Steps

### Before

1. **Prepare the kit** and keep it somewhere visible, near the go-bag. With several animals, label what belongs to each.
2. **Stick a note on the kit** for the medicines: name, dose, time.
3. **Find out in advance where it can go**: a relative or friend, a boarding kennel, accommodation that accepts animals.
4. **Name a trusted person** who has your keys and knows where the kit is, in case you cannot get home.
5. **Get it used to its carrier** and to car trips.

### If you stay

1. **Bring it inside** as soon as the warning is given and keep it indoors.
2. **Count its water** in your supplies.
3. **In hot weather**, keep it cool and give it water. Hot ground can burn its paws: carry it or go out in the cool hours.
4. **After a nuclear accident or fallout**, keep it indoors. If it was outside, brush it gently then wash it, following the instructions of the authorities.

### If you leave

1. **Follow the instructions** and never put yourself in danger for an animal.
2. **Take it with its kit**, in a carrier or on a leash.
3. **Find out about the shelter**: some do not accept animals, or only assistance dogs. In that case, leave it with the relative, friend or kennel you planned.
4. **If you really cannot take it**, as a last resort:
   - leave it indoors, loose in a room that is easy to clean and well ventilated, such as a bathroom;
   - leave water and food for at least 3 days;
   - put a note on the front door saying it is there, for the rescuers.

## Never do this

- Leave an animal tied up or shut in outdoors.
- Leave it alone in a car in warm weather, even with a window cracked open.
- Go back into a burning or flooded home to look for it. Teach children, too, to get out without it.
- Release it into the wild thinking it will manage.

## Without equipment

- No carrier: a leash, or for a small animal a strong cardboard box with air holes, well closed.
- No pet food ready: ask a neighbor or the shelter.

## Depending on where you are

- **Wildfire**: if you have to leave it, partly fill the bathtub or a large container with water.
- **In a shelter**: keep it on a leash or in its carrier, with its photo and its record at hand.
- **Farm animals and horses**: ask your local authority or a veterinarian what is planned where you live.
