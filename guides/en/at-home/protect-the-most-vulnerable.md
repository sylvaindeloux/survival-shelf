# Protect the most vulnerable

> **In short**: before a crisis, find out who around you will need help: elderly people, babies, pregnant women, people who are sick, disabled or isolated. Each of them should have a small network of people who know what to do, a supply of medicines and a solution if the power fails. During the crisis, see them every day, and have them leave early if the home becomes unlivable.

## When to use it

- A heat wave, extreme cold, a power or water outage, an evacuation, an epidemic.
- You live with a vulnerable person, or you know one in your building or your street.
- You are concerned yourself: prepare your support network.

## What you need

- The list of people to help, with their address and number, on paper.
- For each of them: the list of their treatments, a copy of their prescriptions, their doctor's number.
- A spare set of their keys, with their agreement.
- Enough to hold out: medicines in advance, batteries and chargers for devices, water, suitable meals, diapers or incontinence pads.

## Steps

### Before

1. **Make the list** of vulnerable people in your household and around you, and talk with them about what they would need.
2. **Set up a support network** of a few relatives, friends and neighbors. At least one of them has a spare key, knows where the supplies and the medicines are, and knows how to work the medical equipment.
3. **Prepare the treatments**: a supply of medicines, prescriptions, a cooler for those that must be kept cold (see [Get through a crisis with a medical treatment](../health-and-first-aid/get-through-a-crisis-with-a-medical-treatment.md)).
4. **Electric medical device**: plan a backup power supply, and agree with relatives or friends on a place to go if the outage lasts.
5. **Plan the evacuation**: who comes to fetch the person, with which vehicle, how to take the wheelchair, the walker, the devices and the chargers.
6. **Talk with the doctor's office, the dialysis unit or the equipment provider** about what is planned in a crisis.

### During

1. **Check on them every day**, with a visit or a call. A person living alone who does not answer is a reason to go and see.
2. **Make sure they drink and eat.** Never cut the rations of a child or a pregnant woman.
3. **Watch the temperature of the home.** In the cold: one heated room, several layers, hot drinks. In the heat: a cool room, wet skin, water within reach.
4. **Keep the treatments going**, without changing anything on your own.
5. **Reassure**: explain what is happening in simple words, keep to schedules and habits.
6. **Have them leave early** for a relative's home or a reception center if the home gets too cold or too hot, or if a device needs power.
7. **To move a person**, ask them how they want to be carried or guided, and listen to them.

### Signs that mean you must call the emergency services

- **In the heat**: fever, hot, red, dry skin, a violent headache, confused speech, loss of consciousness.
- **In the cold**: shivering, then numbness, slurred speech, confusion, drowsiness. A person who has stopped shivering is not out of danger.
- **A baby in the cold**: bright red, cold skin, a baby with no energy.

## Never do this

- Assume that someone else has been to see them.
- Let a baby sleep in a cold room.
- Leave a child, or an elderly or dependent person, alone in a car in warm weather, even for a few minutes.
- Stop or change a treatment without medical advice.
- Carry a person without asking them how to do it.

## Depending on where you are

- **Elderly person**: have them drink very often in the heat; in the cold, check the temperature of their home often.
- **Baby**: ready-to-feed formula or bottled water for the bottles, diapers in advance, several thin layers and a hat in the cold.
- **Person who depends on the elevator**: plan who will help them on the stairs.
- **Person living alone**: agree with a neighbor that they will call in.
- **In France**: have an isolated person registered with the town hall, so that they are contacted during a heat wave. A person on a ventilator can ask the regional health agency (ARS) to be registered as a high-risk patient, to be warned of power cuts.
