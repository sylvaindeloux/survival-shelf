# Keep warm without heating

> **In short**: gather everyone in one room, close the others, block drafts, pile on clothes and blankets, keep moving and eat warm. Never heat with an oven, a barbecue, a charcoal brazier or a camping stove: carbon monoxide kills without any smell. If the home stays too cold for a baby or an older person, go and sleep somewhere else.

## When to use it

- Power or gas outage in winter, broken boiler or furnace.
- Cold wave in a poorly insulated home.

## What you need

- Warm clothes, hats, blankets, sleeping bags.
- Towels or rags for the bottom of doors, blankets or thick curtains for the windows.
- An indoor thermometer.
- A battery-powered carbon monoxide detector if you use any fuel-burning appliance.
- Dry wood if you have a fireplace or wood stove in good condition, with a swept chimney.

## Steps

1. **Choose one room to live in**, the easiest to keep warm, and all settle there. Close the doors of unused rooms.
2. **Block drafts**: rolled towels or rags under the doors. Avoid opening doors and windows without need.
3. **Cover the windows at night**: shutters, drapes, blankets.
4. **Dress as if you were outside**: several layers, hat, socks, slippers, and a blanket over your legs and shoulders.
5. **Move a little every hour**: sitting still for a long time cools you down.
6. **Eat and drink warm** if you can heat food safely. Avoid alcohol.
7. **Use the fireplace or wood stove** if they are in good condition and up to code, with dry wood, and never push them beyond what the manufacturer allows (see [Light a fire](../fire-and-power/light-a-fire.md)).
8. **If you use a space heater**:
   - electric: at least 1 m (3 ft) from drapes, furniture and bedding, never covered, plugged straight into a wall outlet, with no extension cord, with an automatic shut-off if possible;
   - fuel-burning (kerosene, gas): only a model vented to the outside, according to the CDC. French health authorities advise against using them for heating in a cold wave, and never for more than 2 hours in a row. Never while you sleep.
9. **Still air the home for 10 minutes a day** and never block the air vents.
10. **Protect the pipes from freezing**: let the taps drip a little. Thaw a pipe with a hair dryer, never with a flame.
11. **Watch the temperature and vulnerable people.** If you cannot keep the home warm enough for an infant, an older or a sick person, take them to relatives or to a heated shelter.

## Never do this

- Heat with the kitchen oven or stovetop, a barbecue, a charcoal brazier, a camping stove or a camping heater.
- Run a generator inside the home, the basement, the garage or a porch.
- Leave a fuel-burning space heater on all the time or while you sleep.
- Block the vents to keep the heat in.
- Thaw a pipe with a torch.

## Without equipment

- Not enough blankets: put on several layers of clothing. Failing anything better, wrap yourself in newspapers too: this is what the CDC advises drivers stranded in snow.
- Huddle together: several people in one room warm each other up.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a house with a fireplace**: have the chimney swept every year, before winter.
- **In a car**: see [Protect yourself from the cold](protect-yourself-from-the-cold.md) to hold out in a stranded vehicle.
