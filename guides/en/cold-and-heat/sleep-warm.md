# Sleep warm

> **In short**: get your bed ready before night, without waiting to feel cold. Sleep in the room that is easiest to keep warm, insulate yourself from the floor, put on dry clothes, a hat and socks, add blankets and eat a little before lying down. Put out candles and flame heaters. A baby must never sleep in a cold room.

## When to use it

- Heating off or power outage in winter.
- A night in a car, a shelter or outdoors.
- A home that is hard to heat during a cold wave.

## What you need

- Mattress, sleeping pad or any thickness that insulates you from the floor.
- Blankets, comforters, sleeping bags, emergency blanket.
- Dry nightclothes, hat, socks.
- Something to cover the windows and the bottom of the doors.

## Steps

1. **Choose the room**: the one that stays warm best. Close the doors of the other rooms and keep bedroom windows closed at night.
2. **Block drafts**: a rolled cloth under the door, shutters closed, drapes drawn or a blanket over the window.
3. **Insulate yourself from the ground.** Never sleep directly on a cold or damp floor: mattress, rugs, folded blankets. Outdoors, a bed of boughs, leaves or dry moss does the same job.
4. **Sleep several to a room**: you warm each other up.
5. **Change.** Take off the damp clothes of the day and put on dry ones: long underwear, socks, hat.
6. **Add blankets**, or get into a sleeping bag with a blanket on top.
7. **Eat something and have a warm drink before bed**, with no alcohol.
8. **Put out everything that burns**: candles, flame lamps, fuel-burning space heaters. An electric space heater stays at least 1 m (3 ft) from the bedding.
9. **Aim for at least 18 °C (64 °F) in the bedroom** if you can still heat. Older adults need more, 20 °C (68 °F) or above: they can become dangerously cold in a home at 16 to 18 °C (60 to 65 °F).
10. **Check on the most vulnerable** during the night: children, older or sick people.

### For a baby

- Never let an infant sleep in a cold room. If you cannot warm it, go and sleep somewhere else with the baby.
- Dress the baby warmly for the night: footed pajamas, a sleep sack, several thin layers.
- No pillow, comforter or soft blanket in the crib: they can smother a baby.

## Never do this

- Sleep with a barbecue, a charcoal brazier, a camping stove or a fuel-burning heater lit in the home.
- Leave a candle burning while you sleep.
- Drink alcohol "to warm up" before sleeping.
- Go to bed in wet clothes.
- Leave a car engine running to sleep warm: 10 minutes an hour at most, awake, with a window slightly open and the exhaust pipe clear.

## Without equipment

- No mattress: pile up rugs, cushions, clothes, newspapers, anything that puts thickness between the floor and you.
- Not enough blankets: keep your layers of clothing on, wrap yourself in newspapers and add coats and towels on top.

## Depending on where you are

- **At home**: if your bedroom is hard to keep warm, all sleep in the living room.
- **In a car**: wrap your whole body, head included, in clothes and blankets. Huddle together.
- **Outdoors**: first set up a shelter against wind and rain (see [Build an emergency shelter](build-an-emergency-shelter.md)).
