# Dress for cold and rain

> **In short**: three layers beat one big coat: a layer next to the skin that moves sweat away (wool, silk or synthetic, not cotton), a layer that insulates (wool, fleece, down), a layer that stops wind and rain. Cover your head, neck and hands, stay dry, and take a layer off before you sweat.

## When to use it

- You must go out, walk or work outside in cold, wet or windy weather.
- The heating is off and you need to dress warmly indoors.
- To choose the clothes for your go-bag.

## What you need

- Warm base layer: wool, silk or polypropylene.
- Wool sweater, fleece or down jacket.
- Waterproof and windproof jacket and pants, tightly woven.
- Hat, scarf or knit mask, mittens, warm socks, waterproof boots.
- A dry change of clothes, in a waterproof bag.

## Steps

1. **Put on a first layer that keeps the skin dry**: wool, silk or polypropylene. Avoid cotton next to the skin: it soaks up moisture and holds less body heat.
2. **Add an insulating layer**: wool, fleece or down. Several thin layers keep you warmer than one thick layer.
3. **Finish with an outer layer** that stops wind and rain, tightly woven and preferably water-repellent.
4. **Keep clothing loose**, with nothing tight.
5. **Cover your head and neck**: hat, scarf or knit mask pulled up over your mouth and nose.
6. **Protect hands and feet**: mittens are warmer than gloves, sleeves should be snug at the wrist, boots and coat should resist water.
7. **Adjust the layers all the time.** Open or remove a layer as soon as you feel warm, before you sweat, and put it back on when you stop.
8. **Change as soon as you are wet**, from rain or from sweat. A wet body cools down very fast.
9. **Look after your clothes**: keep them clean and dry, mend tears. Dirty or matted clothing insulates less.

## Never do this

- Keep wet clothing on when you have something to change into.
- Leave without a hat or gloves "because it is not that cold": wind and rain change everything.
- Lace boots or fasten gloves too tight.
- Dry clothes right next to a flame or a space heater.

## Without equipment

- No technical clothing: pile on what you have: T-shirts, sweaters, two pairs of pants.
- Newspaper slipped under your jacket adds a layer of insulation.
- An emergency blanket, over your clothes, stops wind and rain.

## Depending on where you are

- **At home without heating**: dress as if you were outside, with a hat, socks and slippers.
- **In the car**: in winter, keep a hat, gloves and a blanket in the trunk.
- **With a baby**: several thin layers rather than one thick one, and a hat.
- **In rain and mild weather**: you can get dangerously cold well above freezing when you are wet and exposed to wind.
