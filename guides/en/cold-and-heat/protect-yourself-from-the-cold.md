# Protect yourself from the cold

> **In short**: stay dry and out of the wind, cover your head, neck and hands, wear several layers, keep moving without sweating, eat and drink warm, with no alcohol. Shivering that does not stop is the signal to get indoors and warm up. Someone who is confused, drowsy or slurring words has hypothermia: call the emergency services.

## When to use it

- Cold wave, heating failure, winter power outage.
- You must go out or stay out in cold, wet or windy weather.
- You are stranded in your car in the snow.

## What you need

- Several layers of clothing, a hat, a scarf, mittens or gloves, waterproof footwear.
- Blankets, an emergency blanket.
- Warm drinks and something to eat.
- A thermometer to watch the temperature of your home.

## Steps

1. **Limit outings** and strenuous effort outdoors. Postpone long car trips.
2. **Cover what loses the most heat**: head, neck, hands, feet. Put a scarf over your mouth and nose so that you do not breathe freezing air.
3. **Wear several layers** rather than one thick garment (see [Dress for cold and rain](dress-for-cold-and-rain.md)).
4. **Stay dry.** Change wet clothing at once. Take a layer off before you sweat: sweat chills you afterward.
5. **Move regularly**, without wearing yourself out: arms, legs, fingers.
6. **Eat and drink warm**: balanced meals, warm sweet drinks, broth. Avoid alcohol and caffeine, which make you lose heat faster.
7. **At home, keep the rooms you live in warm**, bedroom included: at least 18 to 19 °C (64 to 66 °F) according to British and French health authorities, and 20 °C (68 °F) or more for older adults according to the US National Institute on Aging. If the heating is off, see [Keep warm without heating](keep-warm-without-heating.md).
8. **Check on vulnerable people**: older or isolated people, infants, people with heart or lung disease.
9. **Watch for warning signs**, in yourself and in others (see below).

### Recognize and react

- **Hypothermia** (body temperature below 35 °C or 95 °F): intense shivering, exhaustion, fumbling hands, slurred speech, confusion, drowsiness. In a baby: bright red, cold skin and very low energy. Call the emergency services. Meanwhile: get the person out of the cold and wind, replace wet clothes with dry ones, wrap them in blankets, starting with the chest, head and neck. If they are conscious, give a warm sweet drink, with no alcohol.
- **Frostbite** (nose, ears, cheeks, fingers, toes): white or grayish-yellow skin that feels firm or waxy, and numb. Put the area in lukewarm water, 38 °C (100 °F) at most, without rubbing, and see a doctor. Blue-black skin or blisters: call the emergency services.

## Never do this

- Rub or massage a person with hypothermia, or a frostbitten area.
- Warm with a direct heat source: hot water bottle, heating pad, radiator, stove.
- Walk on frostbitten feet, or break the blisters.
- Give alcohol, or give a drink to an unconscious person.
- Heat yourself with a barbecue, a charcoal brazier, a camping stove or the oven: deadly risk of carbon monoxide poisoning.

## Depending on where you are

- **Stranded in a car**: stay in the car. Run the engine and heater for about 10 minutes every hour, with a window slightly open, and check that the exhaust pipe is not blocked by snow. Wrap yourself in everything you have, move your arms and legs, stay awake. Tie a bright cloth to the antenna and raise the hood.
- **Outdoors**: get out of the wind. Hypothermia can set in well above freezing when you are wet from rain, sweat or a fall into water.
- **With a baby**: several thin layers rather than one thick one, a hat, and avoid carriers that squeeze the legs.
- **Driving in winter**: carry blankets, water, food, warm clothes and your medicines, and leave with a full tank.
