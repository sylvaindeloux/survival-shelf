# Protect yourself from the heat

> **In short**: drink water regularly without waiting to be thirsty, wet your skin several times a day, stay cool during the hottest hours and spend 2 to 3 hours a day in a cool place. A person with hot, red, dry skin who is confused or passes out has heatstroke: call the emergency services and cool them down at once.

## When to use it

- Heatwave, or a heat warning from your weather service.
- Power outage in summer, overheated home.
- Work or effort outdoors in hot weather.

## What you need

- Plenty of water (see [Work out how much water you need](../water/work-out-how-much-water-you-need.md)).
- A spray bottle, a washcloth, a basin.
- Light, loose clothing and a hat.
- A medical thermometer and a room thermometer.

## Steps

1. **Drink water regularly**, without waiting to feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol, very sugary drinks and drinks with caffeine.
2. **Wet your skin** several times a day: face, forearms, neck. Spray, wet cloth, cool shower or bath, foot bath.
3. **Stay cool during the hottest part of the day.** Keep strenuous activity for the early morning.
4. **Spend 2 to 3 hours a day in a cool place** if your home is not: library, cinema, air-conditioned shop.
5. **Keep your home cool** (see [Cool a home without air conditioning](cool-a-home-without-air-conditioning.md)).
6. **Dress lightly**: loose clothing made of natural fibers, a hat outdoors. Sleep under a light sheet.
7. **Eat enough**, in small meals: fruit, raw vegetables, cold soups.
8. **Check every day on people who are alone or vulnerable**: older people, the sick, infants.
9. **Keep your medicines cool**, below 25 °C (77 °F), and never change a treatment without medical advice: some medicines make dehydration worse. Ask your doctor or pharmacist.

### Recognize and react

- **Heat exhaustion**: dizziness, weakness, intense thirst, headache, cramps. Move to a cool place, drink, rest. See a doctor if cramps last more than an hour.
- **Heatstroke**: hot, red, dry skin, fever above 39 °C (102 °F), severe headache, nausea, incoherent speech, convulsions, loss of consciousness. **Call the emergency services.** Meanwhile:
  1. Move the person to a cool place and lay them down with their legs raised.
  2. Remove their clothing.
  3. Cool them: cold cloths or cold packs on the neck, armpits and groin, a fan, water at 25 to 30 °C (77 to 86 °F) sprayed on the skin.
  4. If they are unconscious, lay them on their side.

## Never do this

- Leave a child, an older person or an animal in a parked vehicle, even for a few minutes.
- Give aspirin or paracetamol (acetaminophen) for heatstroke.
- Wait to be thirsty before drinking, or drink alcohol to cool down.
- Stop a medicine on your own initiative.
- Exercise or do heavy work in the middle of the afternoon.

## Without equipment

- No fan and no air conditioning: a wet cloth on the neck and arms, a cool foot bath, and a few hours in a cool public place.
- No spray bottle: a wet washcloth does the job.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a city**: spot a cool place near your home in advance.
- **Older or isolated person**: ask your local authority whether it keeps a register of vulnerable people to contact during heatwaves, as French town halls do.
- **Baby or child under 4**: offer water often, between meals, and never leave them in a vehicle.
- **Outdoors or at work**: drink a glass of water every 15 to 20 minutes, look for shade, take breaks.
