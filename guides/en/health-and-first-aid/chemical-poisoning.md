# Chemical poisoning

> **In short**: do not make the person vomit, and give no water or milk. Call a poison center or the emergency services right away, without waiting for symptoms, with the product's packaging in front of you. Product on the skin or in the eye: rinse for a long time under running water. Fumes: get the person into fresh air.

## When to use it

- Someone has swallowed, breathed in or got on their skin or in their eyes a cleaning, DIY or garden product, a fuel, or too much medicine.
- Young children are the most exposed, with household products.
- Signs can take hours to appear: nausea, vomiting, drowsiness, blurred vision, confusion, trouble breathing, seizures.

## What you need

- A phone and the number of your poison center, written down in advance. Failing that, call your emergency number.
- The packaging or label of the product.
- Running water for rinsing.

## Steps

1. **Protect yourself**: wear gloves if you have some, do not breathe the fumes, open windows.
2. **Move the person away from the product**, and the product away from others.
3. **Identify the product** and estimate the amount: keep the packaging with you.
4. **Call a poison center or the emergency services** right away, even if the person feels fine. A call may avoid a trip to hospital.
5. **Act according to the case**, while waiting for instructions:
   - **swallowed**: do not make them vomit, give nothing to eat or drink;
   - **on the skin**: remove contaminated clothing, under the water if possible, and rinse with plenty of cool or lukewarm running water for at least 20 minutes. Brush a dry powder off first, before rinsing;
   - **in the eye**: rinse under running water for 15 minutes, keeping the water from running into the other eye. Remove contact lenses;
   - **breathed in**: get the person out of the room, into fresh air, and open windows. Sit them up if breathing is hard.
6. **Call the emergency services** if they lose consciousness, struggle to breathe, have a seizure, or if the burn is large.
7. **If they are unconscious**: see [Unconscious person who is breathing](unconscious-person-who-is-breathing.md) or [Cardiac arrest](cardiac-arrest.md).
8. **Give the packaging to the rescuers.** If the person has vomited, keep a sample for the hospital.

## Never do this

- Make them vomit: they could choke.
- Give water, milk or anything else to drink or eat.
- Wait for symptoms before calling, or treat it yourself.
- Try to neutralize one product with another, or put a cream on a chemical burn.
- Mix cleaning products: bleach with a descaler gives off a gas that is toxic for the eyes and lungs, and bleach must never be mixed with ammonia or any other cleaner.
- Pour a product into a food or drink container.

## Depending on where you are

- **At home**: store chemicals and medicines high up, out of sight and reach of children, not under the sink, in their original containers. Never use two cleaning products one after the other without rinsing.
- **Cleaning up after a flood**: wear gloves, open windows, and never mix bleach with another product.
- **No phone signal**: rinse with water for a long time, have the person rest in fresh air, and get to a place where you can call.
