# Get through a crisis with a medical treatment

> **In short**: keep a supply of your medicines, a copy of your prescriptions and an up-to-date list of your treatments, dry and ready to grab. Have a cooler ready for medicines that must stay cold. If your life depends on an electrical device, make yourself known before the outage. A medicine that got wet or hot, or sat in a fridge without power, is replaced as soon as possible.

## When to use it

- You take a treatment every day: diabetes, heart, epilepsy, asthma, thyroid, blood thinner, contraception and so on.
- You use an electrical medical device at home, or someone close to you depends on one.
- A power outage, a flood, an evacuation or a shortage could interrupt your treatment.

## What you need

- A supply of your usual medicines and your emergency medicines.
- An up-to-date list of your treatments, with doses, and a copy of your prescriptions, in a waterproof pouch.
- A cooler with ice or frozen gel packs, if one of your medicines must be kept cold.
- A spare pair of glasses if you wear them.
- The numbers of your doctor, your pharmacy and, if relevant, your medical equipment supplier.

## Steps

1. **List your treatments**: name, dose, time of day. Keep the list with a copy of your prescriptions, dry, in your go-bag.
2. **Always know how much you have left** of each medicine, and refill early, without waiting for the last box. Ask your doctor or pharmacist how much you can keep in advance.
3. **Store your medicines** in a watertight container, high up, above the level a flood could reach, and out of children's reach.
4. **Check your supply twice a year**: expiry dates, prescriptions up to date.
5. **Prepare for cold storage**: keep gel packs in the freezer and a cooler within reach.
6. **If you depend on an electrical device**, talk to your doctor and your supplier: how long the battery lasts, what backup exists, what to do if it fails, where to go if you need hospital care.
7. **During the crisis**, keep taking your treatment as usual. Do not stop or change anything on your own: ask a doctor or a pharmacist.
8. **If you evacuate**, take your medicines, your prescriptions, your cooler and your device with its charger.

### Power outage and refrigerated medicines

1. If the outage lasts, move the medicine to the cooler with ice or gel packs.
2. Read the leaflet again: it sometimes says how long the medicine can stay at room temperature. If not, ask your pharmacist or the manufacturer.
3. After a day or more without cold, the medicine should be replaced, unless the label says otherwise.
4. **If your life depends on it** (insulin, for example) and you have nothing else, keep using it until you can get a new supply, and replace it as soon as possible.

### Medicines exposed to water, heat or fire

- Throw away any medicine that touched flood water or contaminated water, even in a closed container, and any medicine that is wet or looks changed.
- Heat and fire can make a medicine less effective: replace it.
- Exception: if it is life-saving, cannot be replaced for now, and the pills are dry and look normal, you can use it until you find a new supply.
- For a medicine that must be mixed with water, use only bottled or purified water.

## Never do this

- Stop or change a treatment on your own because things are difficult.
- Wait for the last box before refilling.
- Use a medicine that has been in flood water, unless your life depends on it.

## Depending on where you are

- **Electricity-dependent patients**: in France, for example, people on a ventilator for more than 20 hours a day can register with their regional health agency. They are warned 5 days before a planned outage, but the power is still cut, so they need a device with its own backup power. Ask your doctor or your power company whether something similar exists where you live.
- **Heat wave**: keep your medicines out of the heat, below 25 °C (77 °F) if possible. Some medicines make dehydration worse: ask for advice, without stopping anything on your own.
- **Emergency shelter**: mention your treatment and your needs as soon as you arrive.
