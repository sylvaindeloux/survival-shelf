# Shock, stress and supporting others

> **In short**: after a violent event, shaking, reliving the scene, sleeping badly or feeling detached are normal reactions. To help someone: get them to safety, listen without pushing them to talk, meet simple needs, help them reach their loved ones. If daily life is still badly disrupted after 2 to 3 weeks, see a doctor. Thoughts of suicide: call a crisis line or your emergency number.

## When to use it

- You or someone close has just lived through or witnessed an accident, a disaster, an assault, a sudden death.
- **Normal reactions** in the hours and days that follow: fast heartbeat and breathing, tense muscles, feeling detached or numb, images and thoughts that keep coming back, nightmares.
- **Immediate help is needed** if the person has a serious injury, is so upset that they cannot care for themselves or their children, or may hurt themselves or others: call the emergency services.

## Steps

### Helping someone: look, listen, link

1. **Look.** Is the place safe? Who has an obvious urgent need: injury, cold, thirst? Who is in serious distress?
2. **Approach with respect.** Introduce yourself, ask what the person needs and what worries them.
3. **Listen.** Let them talk if they want to, without pressing them to tell their story, and help them feel calm.
4. **Meet simple needs**: a safe place, water, a blanket, something to eat.
5. **Give accurate information**, and simply say so when you do not know.
6. **Link.** Help them reach their loved ones, find the rescuers and support services, and not stay alone.
7. **Respect their choices** and their privacy. Tell them they can ask for help later if they do not want it now.

### Taking care of yourself in the following days

- Keep regular times for getting up and going to bed. Rest even if you cannot sleep.
- Breathe slowly, relax your muscles (see [Stay calm and decide under stress](../basics/stay-calm-and-decide-under-stress.md)).
- Avoid caffeine and nicotine, and switch off screens 30 minutes before bed.
- Limit alcohol and drugs: they mask emotions and make anxiety worse later.
- Keep doing things you enjoy, and talk to people you trust. You have the right not to give details.
- Limit images and news about the event.
- Put off major decisions: moving, quitting your job.

### When to get help

- Your daily life is still badly disrupted after 2 to 3 weeks: see your doctor.
- You have thoughts of suicide: call a crisis line or your emergency number, right away.
- Emergency: call the emergency services.

## Never do this

- Force someone to tell what they went through, or push your help on them.
- Make false promises or pass on information you have not checked.
- Judge their reactions, or share their story with others.
- Take sedatives or sleeping pills without medical advice: they can hinder recovery.
- Turn to "miracle" therapies.
