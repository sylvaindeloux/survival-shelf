# Drowning

> **In short**: do not jump in. Call for help, talk to the person, reach out with a pole or throw something that floats. Once they are out of the water, if they are not breathing: 5 breaths, then chest compressions. Anyone who has nearly drowned must be checked by a doctor, even if they seem fine.

## When to use it

- Someone is in trouble in the water: sea, river, lake, pool, flood.
- A person has just been pulled out of the water.
- You fall into the water yourself.

## What you need

- A phone.
- A long object: pole, branch.
- Something that floats: lifebuoy, ball, large empty bottle with its cap on.

## Steps

1. **Call for help**: the lifeguard if there is one, otherwise the emergency services. Give the exact location.
2. **Talk to the person.** Shout to them to stay calm and float on their back, arms and legs spread, like a starfish.
3. **Reach out with a long object**, lying down or crouching at the edge so that you are not pulled in. Let go if they are pulling you in.
4. **Or throw something that floats.** With a lifebuoy on a line, throw beyond the person, then pull it back toward them.
5. **Do not enter the water** if you are not trained in rescue: water hides dangers, and one rescue is easier than two.
6. **Once the person is out of the water**:
   - conscious: settle them comfortably, sitting or lying, cover them, reassure them, and call the emergency services;
   - not responding but breathing: roll them onto their side and watch their breathing (see [Unconscious person who is breathing](unconscious-person-who-is-breathing.md));
   - not responding and not breathing normally: have someone call and fetch a defibrillator. Start with 5 breaths, then alternate 30 compressions and 2 breaths (see [Cardiac arrest](cardiac-arrest.md)). If you cannot give breaths, push without stopping.
7. **If they vomit during CPR**, roll them onto their side, clear the mouth, then resume.
8. **Have them checked in every case**: lung complications can appear afterward.

### If you fall into the water

1. Do not thrash, and resist the urge to swim right away.
2. Lean back, stomach up, arms and legs spread, with gentle movements to stay afloat.
3. Get your breathing under control.
4. Only then, shout for help or swim to safety.

## Never do this

- Jump in without training or equipment.
- Press on the person's stomach to get water out: there is no need to drain water.
- Let them leave without medical advice because they "feel fine".
- Leave a child unattended near water, even in the bath: they must stay within arm's reach.

## Depending on where you are

- **Private pool**: a barrier or an alarm, and an adult watching at all times.
- **Sea, lake, river**: swim in supervised areas, never alone, never after drinking alcohol, never in bad weather.
