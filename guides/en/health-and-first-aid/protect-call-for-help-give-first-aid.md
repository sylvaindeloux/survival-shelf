# Protect, call for help, give first aid

> **In short**: in that order. Protect first: look for remaining danger and go closer only if it is safe for you. Then call your emergency number and say where you are and what is happening. Then give first aid with simple actions, and stay with the person until help arrives.

## When to use it

- You witness an accident, a collapse, a fall, an injury.
- You do not know where to start: follow the four steps, without rushing.

## Steps

1. **Protect.** Before you go closer, look around: traffic, fire, smoke, electricity, gas, risk of collapse.
   - Go closer only if it is safe for you.
   - Remove the danger if you can do it without risk, for example by switching off the power.
   - If you cannot, keep your distance, keep other people out of the area and call for help.
   - Move the person only if a real, immediate danger that cannot be removed threatens them (see [Move an injured person](move-an-injured-person.md)).
2. **Check the person.** Talk to them, ask simple questions, tap or gently shake their shoulders, ask them to squeeze your hand.
   - Do they respond?
   - Are they breathing normally? Watch their belly and chest for no more than 10 seconds.
   - Are they bleeding heavily?
3. **Call your emergency number**, or have someone call (112 in Europe, 911 in North America).
   - Give your phone number, what is happening, how many people are hurt and the most precise location you can.
   - Answer the questions, follow the instructions, hang up only when you are told to.
   - If someone else calls, check afterward that they did.
   - Send someone to meet the rescuers and guide them.
4. **Give first aid**, calmly, according to what you saw:
   - heavy bleeding: [Stop severe bleeding](stop-severe-bleeding.md);
   - cannot speak, cough or breathe: [Choking](choking.md);
   - does not respond but breathes: [Unconscious person who is breathing](unconscious-person-who-is-breathing.md);
   - does not respond and does not breathe, or breathes abnormally: [Cardiac arrest](cardiac-arrest.md);
   - burned: [Burns](burns.md);
   - in pain after a blow or a fall: [Broken bones and sprains](broken-bones-and-sprains.md).
5. **Stay with them.** Cover them, protect them from cold, heat and weather, talk to them: they may hear you even if they do not answer. If they get worse, call again.

### They are conscious but feel unwell

- Have them rest: lying down, or sitting if breathing is hard, or in the position they find best. Loosen their clothes.
- Ask their age, how long it has lasted, whether it has happened before, whether they have an illness or take a treatment.
- Call for medical advice and pass on the answers.
- If they ask for it, or if the emergency service tells you to, help them take their usual treatment for this problem, or some sugar.
- Chest pain, drooping face, an arm that will not lift, trouble speaking: see [Recognize a stroke or a heart attack](recognize-a-stroke-or-a-heart-attack.md).

## Never do this

- Go closer while the danger is still there: you would become one more victim.
- Move an injured person who is not in immediate danger.
- Hang up before you are told to, or assume that someone else has already called.
- Leave the person alone if you can avoid it.

## Depending on where you are

- **On the road**: switch on your hazard lights and, if you carry a warning triangle, set it up well before the scene (visible from 100 m or 330 ft in the French guidance), without putting yourself in danger.
- **Attack or violence**: get away, or hide if you cannot. Call, obey the police, and give first aid only when it is safe.
- **Blood**: avoid touching it. Wear gloves, or put your hand in a plastic bag. Wash your hands afterward.
- **Epidemic**: wear a mask if you have one, and wash your hands afterward.
