# Electric shock

> **In short**: do not touch the person until the power is off. Switch off at the breaker or unplug the appliance, then call the emergency services. If they are not breathing, start chest compressions. Even if they get up and say they are fine, they must see a doctor: the injuries do not always show.

## When to use it

- A person has had a shock: faulty appliance, bare wire, socket, wet installation after a flood, a power line on the ground.
- **Possible signs**: burns where the current entered and left the body, trouble breathing, irregular pulse, confusion, muscle pain, loss of consciousness, cardiac arrest.
- Wet skin and long contact make the accident worse.

## Steps

1. **Look, do not touch.** Is the person still in contact with the source of the current? Is there water on the floor?
2. **Switch off the power**: switch, breaker, or unplug the appliance, without touching the person.
3. **If you cannot switch it off**, separate the person from the source without touching them directly: stand on something dry and insulating (plastic mat, wooden box) and use a dry wooden object, such as a broom handle. Make sure nobody else gets a shock.
4. **Call the emergency services.**
5. **Check that they respond and breathe.**
   - Not breathing, or breathing abnormally: see [Cardiac arrest](cardiac-arrest.md).
   - Unconscious but breathing: see [Unconscious person who is breathing](unconscious-person-who-is-breathing.md).
6. **Cool any burns** under cool running water and cover them with sterile gauze or cling film (see [Burns](burns.md)).
7. **Stay with them** until help arrives.

After any electric shock, even a mild one with no symptoms, medical advice is needed: the heart must be checked, and internal injuries can go unnoticed.

## Never do this

- Touch the person while they are in contact with the current.
- Use a metal or damp object to move them.
- Go near a power line that has fallen: stay at least 6 m (20 ft) away and do not try to move it.
- Walk into a flooded room where the power is still on.
- Plug in an appliance that has been in water.

## Depending on where you are

- **Fallen power line, accident with a pole or pylon**: keep your distance, keep others away and call the emergency services.
- **After a flood**: have the installation checked by a professional before you switch it back on.
