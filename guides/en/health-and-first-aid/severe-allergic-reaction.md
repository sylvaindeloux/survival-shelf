# Severe allergic reaction

> **In short**: within minutes of a food, a medicine or an insect sting, the person's face or throat swells, they struggle to breathe, their voice changes or they feel faint. If they have an adrenaline (epinephrine) auto-injector, use it at once, in the thigh. Call the emergency services. They stay lying down or half-sitting, without standing or walking.

## When to use it

- The reaction starts within minutes, sometimes up to an hour after contact.
- **Common triggers**: foods (peanuts, tree nuts, egg, milk, fish, shellfish, sesame), medicines, wasp, bee or hornet stings, latex.
- **Danger signs**:
  - swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat;
  - hoarse or fading voice, tight throat, trouble swallowing;
  - difficult, fast or wheezy breathing;
  - dizziness, feeling faint, confusion, loss of consciousness;
  - cold skin that is pale, gray or bluish;
  - a raised, itchy rash, stomach pain, nausea, vomiting;
  - in a child: limp, floppy, not responding as usual.

## What you need

- The person's adrenaline auto-injector, if they have one. People at risk are advised to carry two.
- A phone.

## Steps

1. **Use the adrenaline auto-injector** at once if there is one, even if the signs seem mild. Follow the instructions printed on it:
   - remove the safety cap;
   - place the tip against the outer thigh, at a right angle;
   - press firmly until it clicks and hold for 3 to 10 seconds, depending on the model;
   - massage the injection site.
2. **Call the emergency services** and say "anaphylaxis".
3. **Position the person**:
   - lying on their back, legs raised;
   - half-sitting if breathing is hard;
   - on the left side if pregnant.
4. **Remove the stinger** if it is a sting and it is still in the skin, by scraping it off sideways.
5. **Loosen their clothing**, reassure them, and watch their breathing and consciousness.
6. **If there is no improvement after 5 minutes**, use the second auto-injector if there is one.
7. **If they lose consciousness**: see [Unconscious person who is breathing](unconscious-person-who-is-breathing.md) or [Cardiac arrest](cardiac-arrest.md).

Even if they feel better after the injection, still call the emergency services and keep them lying down.

## Never do this

- Wait to see whether it passes.
- Let them stand up or walk, even if they feel better.
- Give them anything to eat or drink.
- Hold back from using the auto-injector for fear of doing it wrong.

## Without equipment

- No auto-injector: call at once, position the person as above and stay with them. Be ready to start chest compressions if they stop breathing.

## Depending on where you are

- **If you have an allergy yourself**: carry two auto-injectors, at room temperature, not in the fridge and not in direct sun, and check their expiry date. Show the people around you where they are and how to use them.
- **In your go-bag**: pack your auto-injectors and your prescription (see [Get through a crisis with a medical treatment](get-through-a-crisis-with-a-medical-treatment.md)).
