# Burns

> **In short**: cool the burn at once under cool running water, for at least 10 minutes, 20 if possible. Meanwhile, remove clothing and jewelry that are not stuck to the skin. Do not burst blisters, and use no ice and no grease. Then cover loosely. Large or deep burn, on the face or hands, or in a young child: call the emergency services.

## When to use it

- Burn from a flame, a hot liquid or object, steam, the sun.
- **Minor burn**: redness in an adult, or a blister smaller than half the palm of their hand.
- **Serious burn**: call the emergency services as soon as you start cooling (see the list below).

## What you need

- Cool or lukewarm running water, between 15 and 25 °C (59 to 77 °F).
- Cling film (plastic wrap), a sterile non-stick dressing, or a clean cloth.

## Steps

1. **Stop the cause.** Clothes on fire: stop the person from running, have them drop to the ground, cover their face with their hands and roll, or smother the flames with a blanket or a non-synthetic garment.
2. **Cool at once** under cool running water, at low pressure, for at least 10 minutes, ideally 20. Let the water run from above the burn, not straight onto it.
3. **While the water runs**, remove clothing and jewelry near the burn, except anything stuck to the skin: cut around it.
4. **Assess the burn.** If it is serious, call the emergency services and keep cooling as instructed.
5. **Cover it** loosely: cling film laid flat over the burn rather than wrapped around the limb, a sterile dressing or a clean cloth. Nothing else on the skin.
6. **Settle the person**: lying down, or sitting if breathing is hard. Cover the rest of the body so that they do not get cold.
7. **Minor burn**: cool until the pain eases, protect it with a sterile dressing, and watch it over the following days. Fever, heat, spreading redness, swelling, lasting pain, pus: see a doctor.

### Call the emergency services if

- blisters cover more than half the palm of the victim's hand;
- the skin is whitish or blackened, sometimes with no pain;
- the burn is on the face, neck, hands, genitals, a joint, or near a natural opening of the body;
- it is a child under 5, a baby, an older person;
- it comes from a chemical, electricity or an explosion;
- the person breathed in smoke or has trouble breathing, or was in a fire in an enclosed space.

Also get medical advice for any blister, and check that the tetanus vaccination is up to date.

### Special cases

- **Child**: children get cold fast. Water between 15 and 20 °C (59 to 68 °F), no more than 10 minutes, the rest of the body covered, and medical advice quickly.
- **Very large burn**: do not cool for long, the body temperature drops. Stop if the person feels cold.
- **Chemical**: rinse with plenty of water for a long time (see [Chemical poisoning](chemical-poisoning.md)).
- **Electricity**: do not touch the person before the power is off (see [Electric shock](electric-shock.md)).
- **Sunburn**: get into the shade, cool with lukewarm water, drink, stay out of the sun. Large blisters, fever, chills or dizziness: see a doctor.

## Never do this

- Apply ice or iced water.
- Put on butter, oil, toothpaste, honey, alcohol, or any cream without medical advice.
- Burst the blisters.
- Pull off clothing stuck to the skin.
- Put cotton wool or a sticky dressing on the burn, or bandage it tightly.
- Give food or drink to a person with a serious burn.

## Without equipment

- **No running water**: gently pour cool bottled water, or lay on a clean wet cloth and rewet it often.
- **No cling film or dressing**: a clean dry cloth, laid on loosely.

For children and large burns, this guide keeps the most cautious advice: cool for a shorter time to avoid hypothermia.
