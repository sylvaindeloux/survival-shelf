# Bites and stings

> **In short**: wash any bite for a long time with soap and water and show it to a doctor, above all a cat bite. Wasp or bee sting: scrape the stinger off, apply cold. Snake bite: call the emergency services, keep the limb still, do not suck, cut or tie anything around it. Swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing, feeling faint: call the emergency services at once.

## When to use it

- Bite from a dog, a cat or another animal.
- Sting from a wasp, a bee or a hornet, tick bite.
- Snake bite: get emergency help for any bite.
- Jellyfish, sea urchin or weever fish at the seaside.

## What you need

- Soap and water, an antiseptic, gauze pads.
- A tick removal tool, or fine-tipped tweezers.
- Cold: an ice pack wrapped in a cloth, or a cloth soaked in cold water.

## Steps

### Dog or cat bite

1. **Wash the wound** with soap and water as soon as possible, remove any dirt, rinse very thoroughly.
2. **Dry it**, then apply an antiseptic and cover with a clean dressing.
3. **If it bleeds**, press with a clean cloth and raise the limb.
4. **See a doctor** if the bite is deep, is on a hand, a foot or near a joint, comes from a cat (the risk of infection is very high), if your tetanus vaccination is not up to date or you do not know (within 48 hours), if you have diabetes or a weakened immune system, or if the wound becomes red, swollen, painful or oozes.
5. **Call the emergency services** if the bite is on the face, neck or genitals, if the wound is torn or reaches the bone, bleeds heavily, or if you have lost feeling or movement in the area.
6. **Rabies**: tell the doctor which animal bit you, when and how it happened. A bite from a stray or wild animal needs medical advice quickly: the doctor decides on treatment.

### Wasp, bee or hornet sting

1. **Remove the stinger** if it is still in the skin, by brushing or scraping it sideways with a fingernail or the edge of a bank card, without pinching it.
2. **Wash** with soap and water.
3. **Apply cold** for up to 20 minutes and raise the area. Do not scratch.
4. **Call for medical help** if the sting is in the mouth or throat or near the eyes, if there are several stings, if the person has had a serious reaction before, or if they show signs of allergy (see [Severe allergic reaction](severe-allergic-reaction.md)).

### Tick bite

1. **Remove the tick as soon as possible.** With a tick removal tool: slide the hook under the tick, as close to the skin as possible, and turn gently until it lets go. With fine-tipped tweezers: grip the tick as close to the skin as possible and pull upward slowly, without squeezing it.
2. **Clean the skin** with antiseptic or soap and water, wash your hands, and look for other ticks all over the body: scalp, ears, neck, armpits, elbows, groin, knees.
3. **Watch the area for a month.** A red patch that spreads in a ring, fever, aches or great tiredness: see a doctor and say that you were bitten by a tick.
4. **See a doctor without waiting for signs** for a pregnant woman, a child under 8, a person with a weakened immune system, or if the tick stayed attached for more than 36 hours.

### Snake bite

1. **Call the emergency services**, for any bite.
2. **Lay the person down and reassure them.** They should not walk.
3. **Keep the bitten limb as still as possible.**
4. **Remove rings, watch and bracelets** and loosen clothing: the area will swell.
5. **If possible, wash** with soap and water.
6. **Remember what the snake looked like**, or take a photo from a distance, without going near it.

### At the seaside

- **Jellyfish**: do not rub. Rinse with seawater, cover with sand, let it dry, then scrape it off with a stiff card. Rinse again and disinfect.
- **Sea urchin**: remove the spines with tweezers and disinfect.
- **Weever fish**: put the limb in the hottest water you can bear without burning yourself, disinfect, and see a doctor quickly.

## Never do this

- **Snake**: suck or cut the wound, use a venom extractor, tie anything tightly around the limb, take aspirin or ibuprofen, try to catch or kill the snake.
- **Tick**: put alcohol, oil, ether or nail polish on it, burn it, crush it, pull sharply or squeeze its body.
- **Stinger**: pinch it with tweezers, which can inject more venom.
- **Bite**: wait with a cat bite or a wound that turns red.
- **Jellyfish**: touch a stranded jellyfish.

## Depending on where you are

- **Far from help, after a snake bite**: the person does not walk. Carry them, or bring help to them.
- **In woods and tall grass**: long, light-colored clothing, pants tucked into socks, closed shoes, and stay in the middle of paths. Do not lift stones or put your hands into tree stumps.
- **Local species**: jellyfish, snakes and insects differ a lot from one region to another. Follow local advice when it exists.

The seaside advice comes from the French Red Cross and was written for French coasts.
