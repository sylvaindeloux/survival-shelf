# Heatstroke

> **In short**: in hot weather or after exertion, a person who is burning hot, with hot dry skin, a headache, confused speech or loss of consciousness has heatstroke: call the emergency services. Meanwhile, move them somewhere cool, undress them, wet their skin and fan them, and put cold packs on the neck, armpits and groin.

## When to use it

- Heat wave, overheated home, car in the sun, effort in hot weather.
- **Heat exhaustion**: great tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea, heavy sweating, pale clammy skin, cramps, intense thirst. The person needs cooling down and should feel better within 30 minutes.
- **Heatstroke, a life-threatening emergency**: very high temperature (above 39 °C or 102 °F), hot, red, dry skin, violent headache, fast breathing and heartbeat, confused speech, seizures, loss of consciousness. Or still unwell after 30 minutes of cooling.

## What you need

- A cool, airy place, shade.
- Water, wet cloths, a spray bottle, a fan or something to fan with.
- Cold packs or ice packs, wrapped in a cloth.
- A thermometer if you have one.

## Steps

1. **Move the person somewhere cool**: into the shade, the coolest room, an airy place.
2. **Lay them down** and remove or loosen their clothing.
3. **Cool their skin**: spray or sponge them with cool water, lay on wet cloths, and fan them or use an electric fan.
4. **Put cold packs wrapped in a cloth** on the neck, under the armpits and on the groin.
5. **If they are fully conscious and can swallow**, have them drink cool water, a little at a time. A sports drink or a rehydration drink is fine too.
6. **Take their temperature** if you can, to tell the rescuers.
7. **Stay with them.** They should feel better within 30 minutes.
8. **Call the emergency services** if they show any sign of heatstroke, or if they are no better after 30 minutes. Keep cooling them while you wait.
9. **If they lose consciousness**, roll them onto their side and watch their breathing (see [Unconscious person who is breathing](unconscious-person-who-is-breathing.md)).

## Never do this

- Give aspirin or paracetamol (acetaminophen) to bring the temperature down.
- Give a drink to a person who is not fully conscious.
- Give alcohol.
- Leave the person alone, or wait for it to pass when there are signs of heatstroke.
- Leave a child or an animal in a parked car, even for a few minutes.

## Depending on where you are

- **Babies, young children, older people**: they are the most vulnerable to heat. Do not wait to call.
- **Outdoors, far from help**: get them into the shade, wet their clothes and skin with the water you have, fan them.
- **Cramps after effort in the heat**: rest in a cool place, and drink. If they last more than an hour, get medical advice.
