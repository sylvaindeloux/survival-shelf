# Wounds and infections

> **In short**: wash your hands, rinse the wound for a long time with clean water, cover it with a clean dressing and change it regularly. Check it every day: spreading redness or swelling, growing pain, pus or fever mean you need a doctor without delay. Deep or large wound, bite, or object stuck in it: call the emergency services.

## When to use it

- Cut, graze, scrape, small wound from a dirty object.
- In a crisis, a small wound that is badly cared for can get infected: treat it right away, even if it looks minor.
- Blood flows steadily or spurts: see [Stop severe bleeding](stop-severe-bleeding.md) first.

## What you need

- Clean water (tap or bottled) and soap.
- Sterile gauze pads, adhesive bandages, tape.
- An antiseptic, useful above all when you have no clean water.
- Disposable gloves if you are treating someone else.

## Steps

1. **Wash your hands** with soap and water. Without water: alcohol-based hand sanitizer. Wear gloves if you have some.
2. **If it bleeds**, press firmly with gauze or a clean cloth for 5 to 10 minutes, and raise the limb.
3. **Rinse the wound** thoroughly with clean running water, bottled or tap, to wash out soil and dirt.
4. **Clean the skin around it** with soap and water, wiping away from the wound.
5. **Disinfect** with an antiseptic if the wound is deep or large, or if you could not rinse it with clean water: from the center outward, with a gauze pad.
6. **Pat dry**, then cover with an adhesive bandage or a sterile pad held with tape.
7. **Change the dressing** every day for 2 to 3 days, then every 2 days if the wound is clean, and whenever it gets wet or dirty.
8. **Look at the wound every 24 hours** and check your tetanus vaccination: only the vaccine protects you.

### See a doctor without delay if

- the wound becomes red, hot, swollen or more and more painful, or oozes yellowish or greenish fluid;
- you have a fever above 38.5 °C (101.3 °F);
- the wound is still dirty after cleaning, or has soil in it;
- it was made by a dirty pointed object, or by a bite;
- it is longer than about 5 cm (2 in);
- your tetanus vaccination is not up to date, or you do not know;
- you have diabetes or a weakened immune system: the same day;
- it has not started to heal after 2 weeks.

### Call the emergency services if

- the bleeding does not stop, or the blood spurts;
- the wound is deep, wide, torn or crushed, or an object is stuck in it;
- it is on the chest, abdomen, eye, face or neck;
- you have lost feeling or cannot move the injured part normally;
- with an infected wound, the person becomes confused, short of breath, has a fast heartbeat, shivering, extreme pain or clammy skin.

While waiting for help: sitting for a chest wound, lying with knees bent for an abdominal wound, lying with eyes closed and head still for an eye wound, lying down in other cases.

## Never do this

- Remove an object stuck in the wound.
- Close a dirty wound, a bite or a puncture wound yourself: germs would be trapped inside.
- Keep a wet or dirty dressing on.
- Let a wound touch flood water or doubtful water.
- Wait when there are signs of infection.

## Without equipment

- **No tap water**: bottled water, or water you have made safe (see [Make water safe to drink](../water/make-water-safe-to-drink.md)).
- **No dressing**: a clean dry cloth, changed every day.

## Depending on where you are

- **Flood**: keep your wound out of the water, cover it with a waterproof bandage, and wash with clean water and soap after any contact.
- **Far from help**: any bite, deep puncture or wound from a dirty object must be shown to a health worker as soon as possible, even if it looks clean.
