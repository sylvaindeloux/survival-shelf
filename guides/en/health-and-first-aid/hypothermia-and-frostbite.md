# Hypothermia and frostbite

> **In short**: someone who has been cold and becomes confused, clumsy, drowsy or slurs their words has hypothermia: call the emergency services. Get them into shelter, replace wet clothes, wrap them in blankets, head included. No hot bath, no hot water bottle, no rubbing, no alcohol. Frostbite is rewarmed in lukewarm water, never by rubbing.

## When to use it

- After exposure to cold, wind or rain, a fall into water, or in an unheated home. Hypothermia can set in well above freezing when you are wet.
- **Hypothermia** (body temperature below 35 °C or 95 °F): intense shivering, pale cold skin, bluish lips, tiredness, fumbling hands, slow or slurred speech, slow breathing, confusion, drowsiness.
- **In a baby**: cold skin, sometimes bright red, floppy, unusually quiet and sleepy, may refuse to feed.
- **Frostbite** (fingers, toes, nose, ears, cheeks): cold skin that is white or grayish-yellow, firm or waxy, numb, with tingling.

## What you need

- Shelter, dry clothes, blankets, a sleeping bag, an emergency blanket, a hat.
- A warm sweet drink, with no alcohol, and something sugary to eat.
- For frostbite: a basin of lukewarm water, 38 °C (100 °F) at most.

## Steps

### Hypothermia

1. **Call the emergency services.**
2. **Get the person out of the cold and wind**, indoors if possible.
3. **Remove wet clothing** and replace it with dry clothing.
4. **Wrap them** in blankets, a sleeping bag or dry towels, starting with the chest, head and neck. Cover the head.
5. **If they are fully awake**, give a warm sweet drink, with no alcohol, and something sugary to eat, such as chocolate.
6. **Talk to keep them awake** and stay with them until help arrives.
7. **If they stop responding**, check their breathing: see [Unconscious person who is breathing](unconscious-person-who-is-breathing.md) or [Cardiac arrest](cardiac-arrest.md).

### Frostbite

1. **Get into shelter** and wrap up: warm the whole body first.
2. **Remove gloves, rings and boots** from the frozen area.
3. **Rewarm gently**: hands in the armpits, or the frozen part in lukewarm water, 38 °C (100 °F) at most.
4. **Dry without rubbing**, cover with a light dressing, raise the limb.
5. **See a doctor.** Blue-black, hard or numb skin, or blisters: call the emergency services.

## Never do this

- Rewarm with a direct heat source: hot bath, hot water bottle, heating pad or electric blanket, radiator, fire.
- Rub or massage the person, their arms, their legs or a frostbitten area.
- Give alcohol.
- Give a drink to a person who is not fully awake.
- Walk on frostbitten feet, or break the blisters.

## Without equipment

- Huddle together, out of the wind and insulated from the ground.
- Wrap the person in anything dry you have: clothing, blankets, newspapers.
