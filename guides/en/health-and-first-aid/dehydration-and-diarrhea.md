# Dehydration and diarrhea

> **In short**: with diarrhea, vomiting or great heat, give drinks often, in small sips, more than usual. For a baby or a young child, give oral rehydration solution, never plain water alone. A person who is very drowsy, confused or hardly passing urine, or a baby who is floppy and hard to wake: call the emergency services.

## When to use it

- Diarrhea, vomiting, fever, great heat, hard effort, or simply not drinking enough.
- **Most at risk**: babies and young children, older people, people with a chronic illness. Older people feel little thirst: think of it when they lose their appetite or are unusually tired or drowsy.
- **Early signs**: thirst, dry lips, unusual tiredness, dark, strong-smelling urine and less of it, dizziness, headache.
- **Serious signs**: intense thirst, dry mouth and tongue, sunken eyes, cold pale skin that stays pinched when you pinch it, almost no urine, disorientation, drowsiness, unusual behavior.

## What you need

- Safe drinking water.
- Sachets of oral rehydration solution (ORS), sold in pharmacies: keep some in advance if you have a young child.
- Failing that, sugar and salt.

## Steps

1. **Give drinks right away**, often and in small sips, more than usual. With vomiting, very small amounts, very close together, then more.
2. **Choose the right drink.**
   - Baby and young child: oral rehydration solution, prepared as the sachet says (usually one sachet in 1 liter of safe water). Several times an hour at first, then as much as they want. Keep breastfeeding or giving the usual bottles, and offer the solution between feeds.
   - Adult and older child: water, sweetened water, salted vegetable broth, or rehydration solution.
3. **Have the person rest**, somewhere cool. In hot weather, undress them and cool them with damp cloths and a fan.
4. **Let them eat** as soon as they can, their usual food.
5. **Watch the urine**: it should become pale and regular again.
6. **Protect the others**: diarrhea spreads through hands. Wash your hands with soap and water after the toilet or a diaper change and before cooking, wash soiled laundry separately on a hot wash, clean toilets, taps and door handles every day. The sick person does not cook for others.

### If you have no sachet

In 1 liter (about 1 quart) of safe drinking water, dissolve 6 teaspoons of sugar and half a teaspoon of salt. Stick to the amounts, and use a commercial sachet as soon as you can.

### Get medical help quickly if

- it is a baby under 1 year, or a child who has stopped drinking or feeding;
- the person cannot keep any fluid down;
- there is blood in the stools;
- diarrhea lasts more than 7 days, or vomiting more than 2 days;
- a child under 5 shows signs of dehydration.

### Call the emergency services if

- the person is confused, very drowsy, hard to wake, or collapses;
- their skin is cold, pale, gray or blotchy, their breathing or heartbeat is fast;
- a baby is floppy, moans, sleeps a lot, breathes fast, has dark-ringed eyes, a sunken soft spot on the head, few or no tears, far fewer wet diapers, or keeps vomiting despite the rehydration solution;
- they vomit blood or a green fluid, or have a sudden, severe stomach ache or headache.

## Never do this

- Rehydrate a baby or a young child with plain water alone.
- Give them sodas or fruit juice: they make diarrhea worse.
- Dilute formula more than usual.
- Give anti-diarrhea medicine to a child under 12, or aspirin to anyone under 16, without medical advice.
- Drink alcohol to rehydrate.
- Prepare the solution with doubtful water.

## Depending on where you are

- **Tap water cut off or doubtful**: use only bottled water or water you have made safe (see [Make water safe to drink](../water/make-water-safe-to-drink.md)).
- **Several people ill at the same time** where hygiene is hard to keep up: see [Avoid disease when hygiene breaks down](../hygiene/avoid-disease-when-hygiene-breaks-down.md).
