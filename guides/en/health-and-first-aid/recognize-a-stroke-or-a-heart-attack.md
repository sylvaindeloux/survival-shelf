# Recognize a stroke or a heart attack

> **In short**: a drooping face, an arm or a leg that will not respond, trouble speaking: it may be a stroke. A squeezing pain in the chest that does not go away, sometimes spreading to the arm or jaw: it may be a heart attack. In both cases, call the emergency services at once, even if the signs pass. Have the person rest, give nothing to eat or drink, and note the time the first signs appeared.

## When to use it

- The signs come on suddenly. Do not wait to see whether they pass.
- The person often says "it's nothing": do not believe it, call.

## Steps

### Recognize a stroke: FAST

- **F**ace: one side may droop, and they find it hard to smile.
- **A**rms: they cannot lift both arms and keep them up, or one arm is weak or numb.
- **S**peech: slurred words, or they seem confused and struggle to understand.
- **T**ime to call the emergency services, at once.

Other possible signs: weakness or numbness on one side of the body, blurred vision or loss of sight in one or both eyes, a sudden severe headache, dizziness or loss of balance, an unexplained fall.

If the signs disappear within minutes, it is still an emergency: the risk of a stroke is high in the following 24 hours.

### Recognize a heart attack

- Intense pain in the chest, crushing or squeezing, that does not go away.
- It may spread to the jaw, neck, shoulder, left arm or both arms, or the stomach. It can feel like burning indigestion.
- Often with: pale or grayish skin, sweating, shortness of breath, nausea or vomiting, anxiety.
- In women, older people and people with diabetes, the signs can be misleading: feeling faint, sudden breathlessness, unexplained tiredness, an odd sensation in the left arm.

### What to do

1. **Call the emergency services** at once. Describe the signs and say when they started.
2. **Have the person rest.**
   - Stroke: lying down, with a pillow under the head if possible.
   - Chest pain: in the position they find best, lying, half-sitting, or sitting on the floor with knees bent and the back supported. Loosen their clothes.
3. **Note the time** the first signs appeared.
4. **Gather their prescriptions** and recent test results for the rescuers.
5. **Answer the operator's questions**: since when, has it happened before, have they been in hospital or do they take a treatment for it. If they carry a treatment for chest pain, such as a spray prescribed for angina, the operator will say whether to use it.
6. **Stay with them** and keep checking that they respond and breathe.
7. **If they collapse**, stop responding and stop breathing or breathe abnormally: start chest compressions (see [Cardiac arrest](cardiac-arrest.md)).

## Never do this

- Wait for it to pass, or wait until the next day to see a doctor.
- Give anything to eat or drink.
- Give a medicine on your own initiative. With signs of a stroke, no medicine at all, not even their usual treatment.
- Let them drive themselves to hospital.
- Leave them alone.

## Depending on where you are

- **No phone or no signal**: send someone to get help and stay with the person. If you are alone, settle them at rest before going for the nearest help.
