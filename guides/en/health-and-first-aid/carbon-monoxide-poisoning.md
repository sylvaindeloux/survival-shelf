# Carbon monoxide poisoning

> **In short**: headache, nausea, dizziness and tiredness in several people in the same home, with a fuel-burning appliance running: think carbon monoxide. Open doors and windows, switch off the appliances, get everyone outside, then call the emergency services from outdoors. Do not go back in until you are told it is safe.

## When to use it

- Carbon monoxide is a gas you cannot see or smell, produced by anything that burns: boiler, furnace, stove, fireplace, water heater, space heater, camping stove, barbecue, generator, engine.
- **Signs**: headache, tiredness, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, weakness, blurred vision, confusion, chest pain, shortness of breath, collapse. They can feel like the flu.
- **Warning clues**: several people ill at the same time, signs that get worse in one room and better outside, a pet behaving strangely, an alarm sounding.
- At a high level, coma comes within minutes. A person who is asleep or drunk can die without feeling anything.

## Steps

1. **Open doors and windows wide.**
2. **Switch off** heating and cooking appliances, if you can do it quickly.
3. **Get everyone out** into fresh air, pets included.
4. **Call the emergency services from outside.** Say that you suspect carbon monoxide poisoning.
5. **If someone is unconscious**, bring them out into fresh air if you can do it without endangering yourself, then check their breathing: see [Unconscious person who is breathing](unconscious-person-who-is-breathing.md) or [Cardiac arrest](cardiac-arrest.md).
6. **Do not go back in, and do not switch anything back on,** until the rescuers or a professional say it is safe.

Even if everyone feels better once outside, get medical advice.

## Never do this

- Stay inside to look for the source.
- Trust your nose: this gas has no smell.
- Go back into the home because you feel better.
- Heat yourself with a camping stove, a barbecue, charcoal or the oven.
- Run a generator or an engine in the home, the garage, the basement or an enclosed porch.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a garage**: never leave a car engine running, even with the door open.
- **In a tent or a shelter**: never use a stove or a barbecue inside.
