# Stop severe bleeding

> **In short**: press hard, directly on the wound, right away, with a clean cloth or your bare hand. Lay the person down, have someone call the emergency services, and do not let go until help arrives. A tourniquet is only for an arm or a leg, when pressure does not work or cannot be applied.

## When to use it

- Blood spurts or flows steadily from a wound and does not stop by itself.
- The amount already lost is large: about half a soda can, less for a child.

## What you need

- A clean cloth: gauze, a towel, a piece of clothing.
- Disposable gloves, or a plastic bag to protect your hand.
- A wide bandage for a pressure dressing.
- For an improvised tourniquet: a non-elastic strip of cloth 3 to 5 cm (1 to 2 in) wide and at least 1.5 m (5 ft) long, and a strong stick 10 to 20 cm (4 to 8 in) long.

## Steps

1. **Protect your hand** if you can, without losing time: gloves, plastic bag, cloth. You can also ask the person to press on the wound themselves.
2. **Uncover the wound** by moving or removing clothing.
3. **Press hard, directly on the wound**, with your fingers or palm, on a clean cloth that covers it completely. With no cloth, press with your bare hand.
4. **Lay the person down.**
5. **Have someone call the emergency services.** Alone, call on speakerphone without letting go.
6. **Keep pressing until help arrives.** If blood soaks through, press harder and add a second cloth on top of the first, without removing it.
7. **Cover, reassure and watch the person.** Heavy sweating, feeling cold, very pale skin, fast breathing, loss of consciousness: call again.
8. **Wash your hands** when it is over. If their blood touched a wound of yours, or your face, get medical advice without delay.

### If you must free your hands: the pressure dressing

Only if pressure has stopped the bleeding, and never on the neck, head, chest or abdomen.

1. Quickly replace your hand with a clean cloth that covers the whole wound.
2. Wrap a bandage wide enough to cover the cloth, at least two turns, tight enough to press.
3. Check the skin beyond the bandage: if it turns cold, pale or numb, loosen slightly.
4. If it still bleeds, add a second pad on top. If that is not enough, go back to pressing with your hand.

### The tourniquet, as a last resort

On an arm or a leg only, when pressure does not work or is impossible: several victims, a wound you cannot reach, an object stuck in the wound.

1. Place it 5 to 7 cm (2 to 3 in) above the wound, between the wound and the heart, never on a joint.
2. A manufactured tourniquet is best: follow its instructions.
3. Improvised tourniquet: wrap the strip twice around the limb, tie a knot, lay the stick on the knot, tie two knots over it, then twist the stick until the bleeding stops and secure it.
4. Keep it tight despite the pain, and leave it visible.
5. Never loosen or remove it: only a medical professional decides that.

### Special cases

- **Object stuck in the wound**: never remove it. Press on either side.
- **Nosebleed**: sit the person down, head leaning forward. Have them blow their nose hard, then pinch both nostrils for 10 minutes without letting go. Get medical advice if it does not stop, if it comes back, after a blow or a fall, or if they take blood-thinning medicine.
- **Vomiting or coughing up blood**: let them take the position they find best, on their side if unconscious, and call.

## Never do this

- Let go to check whether it is still bleeding.
- Remove an object stuck in the wound.
- Loosen or remove a tourniquet.
- Make a tourniquet with a thin or elastic cord.
- Lay down a person with a nosebleed, or tilt their head back.
- Give them something to drink.

## Without equipment

- Press with your bare hand: stopping the bleeding comes first.
- Folded clothing makes a good pad, a scarf or a wide cloth belt a bandage.
