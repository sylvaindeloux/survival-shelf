# Choking

> **In short**: the person cannot speak, cough or breathe. Give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades. If that fails, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts, then start again. If they are still coughing, do not hit them: encourage them to cough. If they pass out, call the emergency services and start chest compressions.

## When to use it

- Most often during a meal.
- **Complete choking**: hands on the throat, cannot speak, cry, cough or make a sound, or only a high-pitched squeak. Mouth open, panicked, skin turning red, then pale or bluish.
- **Partial choking**: they can speak, cry or cough, sometimes with noisy breathing. In that case, no back blows.

## Steps

1. **Ask: "Are you choking?"**
   - They speak or cough: leave them in the position they find best, encourage them to cough, get medical advice and stay with them. If the cough weakens and they tire, go to step 2.
   - They cannot speak or cough: act at once.
2. **Give 1 to 5 back blows.**
   - Stand to the side, slightly behind. Support their chest with one hand and lean them forward.
   - Strike firmly between the shoulder blades with the heel of your open hand, each blow separate from the last.
   - Stop as soon as the object comes out, or they cough or breathe.
3. **If that fails, give 1 to 5 abdominal thrusts.**
   - Stand behind them, your arms under theirs, around the upper abdomen. Lean them forward.
   - Place a closed fist, back of the hand up, just above the navel and below the breastbone. Cover it with your other hand, without pressing your forearms on their ribs.
   - Pull sharply inward and upward. Release between thrusts.
4. **Repeat**: 5 back blows, 5 thrusts, until the object comes out.
5. **If they pass out**: lower them to the ground, have someone call, and start CPR with 30 compressions (see [Cardiac arrest](cardiac-arrest.md)). After each set of compressions, look in the mouth and remove the object only if you can see it and reach it.
6. **Afterward**: let them take the position they find best, reassure them, and get medical advice even if the object came out. Complications can appear in the following days.

### Depending on the person

- **Very large adult or late pregnancy**, when you cannot get your arms around the abdomen: give chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts. Standing behind, forearms under the armpits, place your fist in the middle of the breastbone, not on its lower tip, cover it with your other hand and pull sharply backward.
- **Child**: kneel behind them for the thrusts. If the child is small, sit down and lay them face down across your thigh for the back blows.
- **Baby under 1 year**:
  - lay the baby face down along your forearm, head lower than the chest. Hold the head with your fingers on either side of the mouth, without pressing on the throat;
  - give 1 to 5 back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand;
  - if that fails, turn the baby face up on your other forearm, head still lower. With the tips of two fingers in the middle of the chest, one finger's width above the lower end of the breastbone, give 1 to 5 deep thrusts;
  - never give abdominal thrusts to a baby.
- **Wheelchair user**: give abdominal thrusts as usual, kneeling behind the chair if needed. If you cannot, give chest thrusts.
- **Alone and choking**: call your emergency number even if you cannot speak, and leave the line open. Give yourself abdominal thrusts with your hands, or lean hard over a firm object such as the back of a chair.

## Never do this

- Give back blows or thrusts to a person who can still speak or cough.
- Sweep your fingers blindly inside the mouth.
- Hang a child upside down by the feet.
- Leave it at that once the object is out: medical advice is always needed.
