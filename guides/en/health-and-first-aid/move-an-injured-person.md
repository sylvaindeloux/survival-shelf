# Move an injured person

> **In short**: the rule is not to move an injured person: you could make the injuries worse. The only exception is a real, immediate danger that cannot be removed, such as fire, rising water or a risk of collapse. In that case, drag them along the ground in line with their body, by the safest and shortest way, without putting yourself in danger.

## When to use it

- **Never for comfort**: an injured person who is not in danger stays where they are until help arrives.
- **Only if all of this is true**:
  - the danger is real and immediate, and you cannot remove it;
  - the person cannot get away from it alone;
  - you can see them, reach them easily, and nothing is in the way;
  - you can do it without risk to yourself.
- Otherwise: keep your distance, call for help, and keep others away.

## Steps

1. **Pick the safest and quickest way** to a place out of danger, before you take hold of the person.
2. **Do not lift them: drag them along the ground**, in line with the long axis of the body, keeping head, neck and trunk aligned.
3. **Choose your grip** according to their injuries:
   - **by the ankles**, if the legs do not look injured: crouch low, grip both ankles, lean back and use your body weight;
   - **by the arms**, if the legs are injured: bring their arms above their head, grip the elbows, holding them against the head, or both wrists, and lean back;
   - **by the clothing**: grip their clothing pulled up under the armpits and pull. A coat works better than a T-shirt, which may tear.
4. **Stop as soon as they are out of danger**, no farther.
5. **Call the emergency services.**
6. **Check them and give first aid** (see [Protect, call for help, give first aid](protect-call-for-help-give-first-aid.md)).
7. **Do not move them again.** Cover them, talk to them, watch them.

## Never do this

- Move an injured person to make them "more comfortable", to warm them up or to bring them closer to the road.
- Lift them, bend them or twist their back and neck.
- Enter a danger zone if you may not come out.
- Pull a person out of a crashed car when they are in no immediate danger.
- Give them alcohol.

## Depending on where you are

- **Person trapped in a car, no immediate danger**: leave them in their seat, seat belt on, support their head, and open their airway where they are.
- **Far from help**: with a phone signal, call and follow the advice. Evacuating the person yourself is rarely wise. Shelter them from cold or heat, insulate them from the ground, cover them. If help is more than 2 hours away, help them change position to relieve pressure points, and give frequent small sips of cool fluid if they are fully conscious and can swallow.
- **Several helpers, no urgency**: carrying someone on a blanket takes at least 6 people. It is not a technique for immediate danger.
