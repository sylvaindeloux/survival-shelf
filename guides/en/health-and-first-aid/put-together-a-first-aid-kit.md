# Put together a first aid kit

> **In short**: enough to protect your hands, stop bleeding, clean and cover a wound and keep someone warm, plus emergency numbers and your household's medicines. Store it dry, out of children's reach but easy to grab, and check it regularly.

## When to use it

- Before the crisis: one kit at home, one in the car, a small one in the go-bag.
- Adapt it to your household: number of people, ages, health problems.

## What you need

The quantities are those the American Red Cross gives for a family of four.

### Information

- Emergency phone numbers, including your poison center, your doctor and your relatives.
- First aid instructions, on paper.

### To protect yourself

- 2 pairs of non-latex disposable gloves.
- 1 breathing barrier with a one-way valve, for rescue breaths.
- Masks, hand sanitizer and soap.

### For wounds and bleeding

- 25 adhesive bandages in assorted sizes.
- 10 sterile gauze pads in two sizes.
- 2 large absorbent compress dressings.
- 1 roll of adhesive cloth tape.
- 2 roller bandages of different widths.
- 2 triangular bandages.
- 5 antiseptic wipes.
- An emergency pressure dressing.
- Saline solution for rinsing.

### Other items

- 1 emergency blanket.
- 1 instant cold compress.
- Tweezers, scissors, safety pins.
- A non-mercury, non-glass thermometer.
- Burn gel (hydrogel).
- A tick removal tool if you go into woods or tall grass.
- Sugar sachets.

### Medicines

- Each person's usual treatments and emergency medicines, with a copy of the prescriptions.
- The American Red Cross list also includes aspirin, antibiotic ointment and hydrocortisone ointment. For these and other over-the-counter medicines, ask your pharmacist what suits your household.

## Steps

1. **Choose a container** that is light, sturdy and easy to carry.
2. **Gather the contents** of the list, starting with gloves, gauze pads, bandages and the emergency blanket.
3. **Add the phone numbers** and the first aid instructions, on paper.
4. **Store the kit** in a dry place, away from heat and humidity, out of children's reach but easy to get to: a high cupboard, for example.
5. **Tell everyone in the household where it is.**
6. **Check it regularly**, at least twice a year: expiry dates, general condition.
7. **Replace at once** anything you have used.
8. **Get trained**: equipment only helps if you know how to use it.

## Never do this

- Leave the kit within reach of young children.
- Keep expired products.
- Store medicines without their box and leaflet.
- Leave it in a car in full sun with medicines inside.

## Without equipment

- A clean cloth replaces a gauze pad, a plastic bag replaces a glove, a scarf replaces a bandage.
- Clean water and soap are enough to clean a small wound.

## Depending on where you are

- **Car**: scissors, sterile gauze pads, tape, bandages, adhesive bandages, a pressure dressing, antiseptic, saline, sugar sachets, an emergency blanket.
- **Hiking**: add water or purification tablets, food, an emergency blanket, a flashlight, sunscreen, insect repellent and a tick removal tool.
- **Go-bag**: a smaller version, with your medicines and prescriptions (see [Pack a go-bag](../basics/pack-a-go-bag.md)).
