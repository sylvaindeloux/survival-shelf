# Keep food without a fridge

> **In short**: without cold, a fresh product or a cooked dish does not keep more than 2 hours at room temperature. So live on what keeps without a fridge: canned food, dry goods, whole fruit and vegetables, hard cheeses. Store it somewhere cool, dry and dark, in closed containers, and open or cook only what will be eaten at the meal.

## When to use it

- A power outage that lasts, a broken fridge, a home without electricity.
- Evacuation or temporary housing.
- To organize a supply that will hold without electricity.

## What you need

- Boxes and jars that close well.
- A cool, dry and dark place: a cupboard, a pantry, a dry basement.
- A cooler and ice, if you can get some.
- A marker to date what you open.

## Steps

1. **Separate what needs cold from the rest.** Meat, fish, deli meats, fresh milk, cream, cooked dishes and leftovers do not last more than 2 hours out of the cold. Eat them first, or put them in a cooler with ice, at 4 °C (40 °F) or below.
2. **Rely on what keeps at room temperature**:
   - unopened cans, jars and cartons;
   - pasta, rice, flour, sugar, salt, dried beans and lentils, cereal, crackers and dry cookies;
   - whole fruit and vegetables, such as apples and carrots;
   - eggs, if they were already sold unrefrigerated;
   - hard cheeses, butter, bread, jam.
3. **Store it cool, dry and dark**, away from any heat source, in covered containers.
4. **Close it again after each use.** Move opened sugar, dried fruit and nuts into screw-top jars or airtight boxes. Slip opened packs of cookies or crackers into a bag, then into a box.
5. **Open small.** An opened can, carton of milk or jar no longer keeps without cold: choose sizes that are finished in one meal.
6. **Cook just what you need**, and eat it right away.
7. **Rotate the supply**: oldest products at the front, new ones at the back. Write down the date of purchase or of opening.
8. **Inspect before eating**: packaging, smell, appearance (see [Tell whether food is still safe to eat](tell-whether-food-is-still-safe-to-eat.md)).
9. **Take the trash out** every day in a closed bag (see [Handle household trash](../hygiene/handle-household-trash.md)).

## Never do this

- Leave a cooked dish or a fresh product more than 2 hours at room temperature, then eat it.
- Keep food outdoors or in the snow, even in winter: the sun warms it and animals contaminate it.
- Start home canning to save food: boiling is not enough to sterilize, and a failed preserve can cause botulism.
- Keep an opened can in its can, without cold, for the next day.

## Without equipment

- No cooler: eat the fresh food in the hours after the outage starts, then move on to the supplies in the cupboard.
- No airtight boxes: close the packs with a clip or a rubber band and store them up high.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a flood-prone area**: store the supply up high, in waterproof containers.
- **In hot weather**: choose the coolest room in the home.
- **On the move**: an insulated bag extends the cold for the journey, not beyond.
