# Ration food

> **In short**: water is not rationed, food can be. A healthy adult who moves little can last a long time on half of what they usually eat. Do not cut the rations of children or pregnant women. Take stock, eat what spoils first, keep at least one real meal a day and save your strength.

## When to use it

- Stores are closed, empty or out of reach, and you do not know for how long.
- Your supplies do not cover the announced length of the crisis.
- Before a crisis, to know how many days you can last.

## What you need

- Paper and a pen for the inventory.
- Enough water (see [Work out how much water you need](../water/work-out-how-much-water-you-need.md)).

## Steps

1. **Take stock** of everything edible in the home: fridge, freezer, cupboards, basement, car. Write it down.
2. **Sort by urgency**: what spoils in hours or days, what keeps for weeks, what keeps for months.
3. **Eat what spoils first**: fresh food, then frozen food, then the supplies in the cupboard.
4. **Count the days.** Divide what is left into rations per day and per person, over the announced length, with a margin. As a guide, UN agencies plan about 2,100 kilocalories per person per day for a whole population with light activity: labels give the calories of each product.
5. **Cut the rations of healthy adults**, down to half of their usual ration if you have to, and only if they move little.
6. **Do not cut** the rations of children and pregnant women. A pregnant or breastfeeding woman needs more than usual. Ask a doctor about a sick person.
7. **Keep at least one balanced meal a day**, eaten together, at a set time.
8. **Save your strength**: rest, limit effort, keep warm. Cold raises your needs: about 100 kilocalories more per day for every 5 °C (9 °F) the temperature drops below 20 °C (68 °F).
9. **Keep drinking** normally.
10. **Count again every day** and restock as soon as you can, buying only what you need.

## Never do this

- Ration water to make the supply last.
- Skip the meals of a child or a pregnant woman.
- Eat doubtful food because you are hungry (see [Tell whether food is still safe to eat](tell-whether-food-is-still-safe-to-eat.md)).
- Count on wild plants or mushrooms you do not know.

## Depending on where you are

- **If water is short too**: avoid very salty, fatty or protein-rich food, which makes you thirsty.
- **In winter, without heating**: do not cut rations too far, the body burns more to stay warm.
- **With neighbors**: sharing one hot meal saves fuel and makes sure nobody is forgotten.

## Sources

- [World Food Programme and UNHCR, "Guidelines for Estimating Food and Nutritional Needs in Emergencies" (PDF)](https://www.unhcr.org/uk/sites/uk/files/legacy-pdf/3b9cbef7a.pdf)
- [Ready.gov (United States), "Food"](https://www.ready.gov/food)

The rule "half the usual ration, except for children and pregnant women" comes from FEMA and the American Red Cross. The 2,100 kilocalories are the figure the World Food Programme and UNHCR use to plan rations for a whole population, children included, at 20 °C (68 °F) and with light activity: it is not a survival minimum, and an active adult needs more. The same source counts about 285 kilocalories more per day for a pregnant woman and 500 for a breastfeeding woman. Common sense, with no official source: the written inventory, the shared meal and the advice to ask a doctor about a sick person.
