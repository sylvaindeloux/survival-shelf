# Cook without electricity or mains gas

> **In short**: eat what needs no cooking first. To heat a meal indoors, you are left with a stove running on bottled gas, a fireplace or wood stove, and a fondue burner. A camping stove, a barbecue and a brazier are for outdoors only: indoors, their carbon monoxide kills.

## When to use it

- A power or gas outage that lasts: the stovetop, the oven and the microwave no longer work.
- Before a crisis, to choose a backup way of cooking and the fuel that goes with it.

## What you need

- A backup way of cooking and its fuel: a gas bottle for the kitchen stove, dry wood, a fondue burner, a camping stove with its canisters.
- Matches or a lighter.
- A saucepan with its lid and a manual can opener.
- A battery carbon monoxide alarm, and something to put out a fire (see [Put out a small fire](../fire-and-power/put-out-a-small-fire.md)).

## Steps

1. **Start by eating cold.** Store-bought canned food can be eaten as it is, without reheating (see [Eat without cooking](eat-without-cooking.md)). Keep the fuel for what really has to be cooked or boiled.
2. **Choose the safest means you have**:
   - **Kitchen stove on bottled gas**: it works without power. If the ignition is electric, light the burner with a match. It is for cooking, never for heating the room.
   - **Fireplace or wood stove** in good condition, with a serviced flue: put the saucepan on the stove or near the embers.
   - **Fondue burner or chafing dish**: enough to reheat a can or a soup. Stand it on a stable surface that cannot burn.
   - **Camping stove, barbecue, brazier, gas griddle**: outdoors only, away from windows and doors.
3. **Reheat a can safely**: remove the label, wash the can and open it before you heat it.
4. **Save fuel**: cut food into small pieces, put the lid on, cook one dish for everyone.
5. **Wash your hands** with soap and water before cooking, and cook ground meat and sausages all the way through.
6. **Prepare only what will be eaten right away.** Without a fridge, leftovers do not keep more than 2 hours at room temperature.
7. **Watch the flame** until the end, keep anything that can burn away from it, and put it out completely before you leave the room or go to sleep.

## Never do this

- Use a camping stove, a barbecue or a brazier in the home, the garage, the basement or a conservatory, even with a window open.
- Burn charcoal indoors.
- Heat your home with the stove or the oven.
- Heat a closed can.
- Leave a flame unattended.
- Ignore a headache, nausea or dizziness while something is burning: air the room, get out and call the emergency services.

## Without equipment

- No safe way to cook: eat cold. It is safe with store-bought canned food.
- Keep dry food that needs cooking (rice, pasta, dried beans and lentils) for the day you can cook again.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment**: without a fireplace or a yard, you are left with the stove on bottled gas and the fondue burner. Otherwise, eat cold.
- **In a house**: set up the camping stove or the barbecue outdoors, away from windows, never in the garage.
- **Outdoors**: see [Light a fire](../fire-and-power/light-a-fire.md).
