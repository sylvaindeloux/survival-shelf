# Save the food in your fridge and freezer

> **In short**: keep the doors closed. A closed fridge stays cold for about 4 hours, a full freezer for about 48 hours, 24 hours if it is half full. Eat fresh food first. When the power is back, throw out fresh food that spent more than 4 hours without cold, and never taste food to check it.

## When to use it

- A power outage that lasts more than a few hours, or an appliance that has broken down.
- Before a storm or a planned outage, to get the cold ready.

## What you need

- A thermometer in the fridge and another in the freezer.
- A cooler, ice packs or bottles of water kept in the freezer.
- Paper and a pen to write down when the power went out.

## Steps

### Before the outage

1. **Set the fridge to 4 °C (40 °F) or below** and the freezer to -18 °C (0 °F).
2. **Freeze bottles of water** and ice packs: they will hold the cold longer.
3. **Group the food** in the freezer: packed close together, it stays cold longer.
4. **Freeze what you will not eat right away**: leftovers, milk, fresh meat.
5. **Stick a list of what the freezer holds** on its door, so you open it less often.

### During the outage

1. **Write down the time** and stop opening the doors unless you have to.
2. **Eat in this order**: fresh food from the fridge, then food from the freezer, then the supplies in the cupboard.
3. **After 4 hours without power**, move the perishable food from the fridge (meat, fish, milk, eggs, leftovers) to a cooler with ice, at 4 °C (40 °F) or below.
4. **If the outage is going to last**, ask a relative or friend who has power to keep your food, or add block ice.
5. **Keep medicines cold** in the cooler (see [Get through a crisis with a medical treatment](../health-and-first-aid/get-through-a-crisis-with-a-medical-treatment.md)).

### When the power is back

1. **Look at the thermometers** first. Without a thermometer, check each package.
2. **Freezer**:
   - still hard, with ice crystals: you can leave it frozen;
   - thawed but kept at 4 °C (40 °F) or below: cook it right away and eat it soon, do not refreeze it raw;
   - thawed and above 4 °C (40 °F) for more than 2 hours: throw out meat, fish, seafood, dairy, eggs, prepared dishes and ice cream.
3. **Fridge, after more than 4 hours without power**, throw out:
   - meat, poultry, fish, deli meats, raw or cooked;
   - milk, cream, yogurt, soft and fresh cheeses;
   - eggs and egg dishes, custards and cream pastries;
   - leftovers, cooked dishes, soups, meat sauces, cooked pasta, rice and vegetables;
   - cut fruit and vegetables, prepared salads;
   - opened baby formula, mayonnaise.
4. **You can keep**: hard cheeses, butter, whole fruit and vegetables, jam, mustard, ketchup, pickles, bread.
5. **When in doubt, throw it out.** Unsafe food does not always smell or look wrong.

## Never do this

- Taste food to find out whether it is still good.
- Open the doors "just to look".
- Put food outdoors or in the snow, even in winter: the sun can thaw it and animals can contaminate it.
- Refreeze raw food that has thawed completely.
- Touch dry ice with bare hands or let it touch the food.

## Without equipment

- No thermometer: go by the time you wrote down and by the state of each package (hard, ice crystals).
- No cooler and no ice: eat the fresh food within the first 4 hours.

## Depending on where you are

- **Freezer compartment of a fridge**: it stays cold for far less time than a separate freezer.
- **Flood-prone area**: throw out any food the floodwater touched (see [Tell whether food is still safe to eat](tell-whether-food-is-still-safe-to-eat.md)).
- **Large freezer**: for a long outage, dry ice can keep it cold. The US Department of Agriculture counts about 23 kg (50 lb) to keep a full 500-liter (18 cubic foot) freezer cold for 2 days.
- **In France**: the food safety agency and the Ministry of Agriculture say never to refreeze a thawed product. This guide follows that rule.

The times and the lists come from the US, Canadian and Quebec authorities. Where they differ, this guide keeps the most cautious figure: 4 hours for the fridge (Quebec says 4 to 6 hours; FEMA says to throw out refrigerated food that stayed above 4 °C (40 °F) for more than 4 hours, this guide counts from the start of the outage), 48 hours for a full freezer (an older US brochure said 3 days), and yogurt and mayonnaise are thrown out (Quebec keeps unopened yogurt and store-bought mayonnaise). The US authorities accept refreezing food that stayed at 4 °C (40 °F) or below; the French authorities say never to refreeze a thawed product: this guide follows the stricter rule and asks you to cook it.
