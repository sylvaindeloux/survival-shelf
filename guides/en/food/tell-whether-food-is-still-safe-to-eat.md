# Tell whether food is still safe to eat

> **In short**: "use by" is a safety limit: respect it. "Best before" is a quality date: after it, the food can still be eaten. Throw out any can that is swollen, rusty or leaking, any jar that does not "pop" when you open it, and anything with an abnormal smell, color or texture. Never taste food to check it.

## When to use it

- You are sorting your supplies, or you find a product past its date.
- After a power outage, a flood or several days without a fridge.
- You hesitate over a damaged can or a dish that was left out.

## Steps

### Read the date

1. **"Use by"**: fresh products such as meat, fish, deli meats and some dairy. Do not eat them after the date.
2. **"Best before"**: pasta, rice, sugar, salt, flour, canned food. After the date they are no risk to your health, but they may have lost some taste or nutritional value.
3. **Once opened**, the date on the package no longer counts: follow the time given by the maker, often 2 or 3 days in the fridge.

### Look at the packaging

1. **Can**: throw it out if it is swollen, dented out of shape, rusty, pierced or leaking, or if it spurts or foams when you open it.
2. **Jar**: you should hear air rushing in when you open it. No "pop": throw it out.
3. **Swollen pouch or tray**: throw it out.

### Look at the food

1. **Abnormal smell, color or texture, mold**: throw it out, even if the packaging looks normal.
2. **Do not taste it.** Unsafe food does not always smell or look suspicious: what counts is what happened to it, not only how it looks.

### Trace what happened to it

1. **A fresh product or a cooked dish** left more than 2 hours at room temperature: throw it out.
2. **Homemade leftovers**: 3 days in the fridge at most. A dish with raw eggs (homemade mayonnaise, chocolate mousse): 24 hours.
3. **A thawed product**: eat it within 3 days, without refreezing it.
4. **After a power outage**: see [Save the food in your fridge and freezer](save-the-food-in-your-fridge-and-freezer.md).

### After a flood

1. **Throw out everything the water touched** that was not in a waterproof package: cardboard boxes, pouches, bottles and jars with a screw cap or a crimped cap, home-canned food.
2. **Undamaged store-bought cans can be saved**: remove the label, wash the can in hot soapy water, rinse it, put it in boiling water for 2 minutes, let it dry, then write the contents and the date on it with a marker. Eat them soon.
3. **In the vegetable garden**, throw out fruit, vegetables and herbs that grew above the ground and were covered by the water.

## Never do this

- Taste "just a little" to find out.
- Eat the contents of a swollen can, even if they look fine.
- Go by smell or appearance alone.
- Give doubtful food to a vulnerable person: a child under 5, a pregnant woman, an elderly or sick person. For them, follow the dates strictly.
- Give honey to a baby under one year old.

## Depending on where you are

- **Home-canned food and homemade preserves**: they are the most common cause of botulism, a serious food poisoning. Boiling is not enough to sterilize them. At the slightest doubt, throw them out.
- **After a meal**: blurred or double vision, a dry mouth, difficulty swallowing or speaking, sometimes after vomiting or diarrhea, can be signs of botulism. They most often appear 12 to 48 hours later. Call the emergency services.
- **Date labels**: the two kinds of date described here are those of the European Union. In France, they read "à consommer jusqu'au" (use by) and "à consommer de préférence avant le" (best before). Elsewhere, check what your food safety agency says.
