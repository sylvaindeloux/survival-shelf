# Sea and coast

> **In short**: before going into the water or out to sea, check the weather, the tides and the wind, and tell someone on shore where you are going. Swim in a supervised area. Caught in a current, do not fight it: float, wave, swim parallel to the beach. On a boat, wear a life jacket and never leave the craft, even capsized. At sea, call for rescue on VHF channel 16 or on the sea rescue number.

## When to use it

- Before a swim, a walk at low tide, a trip by boat, kayak or paddleboard.
- You are carried off by a current, cut off from the coast by the tide or in trouble on the water.
- You see someone in trouble in the water.

In France, between June 1 and September 30, 2025, 1,418 drownings were recorded, 409 of them fatal.

## What you need

- A life jacket or buoyancy aid for each person on board: it is mandatory in France.
- A VHF radio on a boat, and a charged phone.
- A waterproof light per person, lasting at least 6 hours.
- Warm clothes, water, something to eat, a first-aid kit.
- The tide times and the day's weather bulletin.

## Steps

### Before you go

1. **Check the local forecast**: weather, wind strength and direction, tide times, currents.
2. **Find out about the place**: currents, rocks, dangerous areas.
3. **Tell someone on shore** where you are going and your departure and return times, then let them know of any change and when you are back.
4. **On a boat**, check the equipment, the fuel and the engine before casting off.

### Swimming

1. **Swim in a supervised area** and look at the flag. In France:
   - green: supervised swimming, no particular danger;
   - yellow: supervised swimming, limited danger;
   - red: swimming forbidden;
   - purple: pollution or dangerous species.
2. **Go into the water gradually.**
3. **Do not go far out**: avoid swimming more than 300 m (330 yd) from the shore. The current and the tide can stop you from getting back.
4. **Watch children at all times.**
5. **No alcohol** before swimming.
6. **Beware of rip channels and of areas where waves break.**

### Caught in a current

1. **Do not fight it and do not panic.** The current carries you away from the shore, it does not pull you under.
2. **Do not swim straight for the beach.** Swim parallel to the shoreline to get out of the current, then come back at an angle, with the waves.
3. **If you cannot make it**, float, wave your arms and call out.

### On foot at low tide

1. **Check the tide times before you set out** and keep the time in mind.
2. **Watch which way the tide is moving**: the sea can come in surprisingly fast and cut off your way back, especially around a rocky point or on the way to another cove.
3. **Cut off by the tide**: climb as high as you can, call the emergency services and wait for rescue rather than swimming across.

### On a boat, kayak or paddleboard

1. **Wear your life jacket.**
2. **In trouble, never leave the craft** and do not try to swim to the coast. Attract attention.
3. **Capsized boat**: hold on to it. A boat is far easier to spot than a person in the sea. Leave it only if it sinks.
4. **Raise the alarm** on VHF channel 16, or on the sea rescue number.

### Someone is in trouble

1. **Do not go into the water**: many of those who drown went in to rescue someone else.
2. **Alert the lifeguards**, or call the emergency services.
3. **Throw them something that floats** and keep your eyes on them (see [Drowning](../health-and-first-aid/drowning.md)).

## Never do this

- Swim under a red flag, or outside supervised areas when the sea is rough.
- Swim against a current until you are exhausted.
- Leave a floating boat to swim for the coast.
- Set out without checking the tide.
- Stay in or on the water when a thunderstorm is coming.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: 196 reaches the sea rescue coordination center (CROSS) from a phone. From a boat, VHF channel 16 lets rescuers locate you faster. On land: 15, 18 or 112.
- **Cold water**: see [Fallen into cold water](../outdoors/fallen-into-cold-water.md).
- **An earthquake felt by the sea, or the sea suddenly drawing back**: see [Tsunami](../disasters/tsunami.md).
- **Stings at sea**:
  - jellyfish: get out of the water, rinse with seawater and remove the tentacles carefully;
  - sea urchin: wear water shoes, remove the spines and disinfect;
  - venomous fish (ray, scorpionfish, stonefish): soak the area in hot water, without scalding yourself, disinfect and get medical advice;
  - feeling unwell after a sting: call the emergency services.
