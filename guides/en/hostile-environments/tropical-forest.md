# Tropical forest

> **In short**: in a tropical forest, you can get lost a few steps from the trail: do not leave it, go with a local guide and tell your host where you are going. Do not grab the vegetation, check your hammock in the evening and your shoes in the morning, and protect yourself from mosquitoes. Snake bite: keep the limb still and get to medical help, without cutting, sucking or applying a tourniquet.

## When to use it

- Before a hike or a stay in equatorial or tropical forest: French Guiana, the Amazon, Central Africa, Southeast Asia.
- Once there, before each outing away from the village or the camp.

## What you need

- Closed, suitable shoes, long covering clothes.
- A skin repellent and an insecticide-treated mosquito net.
- A medical kit, with your malaria treatment if a doctor prescribed one.
- Water and a way to treat it.
- A light, a whistle, a phone, knowing that coverage is limited.

## Steps

### Before you leave

1. **See a doctor before the trip**: malaria, vaccines, medical kit.
2. **Go with a local guide** as soon as you leave maintained trails.
3. **Tell your host** which route you are taking that day.
4. **Ask about the state of the trails**: many forest paths are not maintained, marked or cleared.

### On the move

1. **Do not stray from the trail.**
2. **When a fallen tree blocks the trail**, wait until one member of the group has found the path again on the other side before following.
3. **Do not hold on to the vegetation** and do not pull on vines or aerial roots: you may disturb stinging insects or bring down a dead branch.
4. **Do not touch vegetation or objects on the ground with bare hands.**
5. **Keep your distance from venomous species**: watch them from afar and move on.
6. **Do not pet any animal**, wild or stray.
7. **Swim with caution**: bathing spots are not supervised.

### At camp

1. **Check your hammock** before lying down.
2. **Look inside your shoes and shake out your clothes** in the morning, before putting them on.
3. **Protect yourself from mosquitoes**: long clothes, repellent on uncovered skin, a treated net to sleep under. Those that carry malaria bite mostly at night, from dusk.
4. **Inspect your skin** every evening: ticks and chiggers are the most common nuisance.
5. **Always treat water** before drinking it (see [Make water safe to drink](../water/make-water-safe-to-drink.md)).

### If something goes wrong

1. **Lost**: stop at once, do not hunt for the trail at random, and signal (see [Lost in the wild](../outdoors/lost-in-the-wild.md)).
2. **Snake bite**: keep the limb still, take off rings and bracelets, clean the wound and get to medical help urgently. Do not cut, do not suck, do not apply a tourniquet or ice.
3. **Animal bite or scratch**: wash thoroughly with soapy water and see a doctor as soon as possible.
4. **Fever**, during the stay or in the weeks after you return: see a doctor quickly and say where you traveled. Take no aspirin or anti-inflammatory drug until dengue has been ruled out.

## Never do this

- Leave the trail, even by a few meters, without keeping the rest of the group within earshot.
- Put your hand down without looking, or sit down without checking.
- Walk barefoot or in sandals.
- Cut, suck or tourniquet a snake bite.
- Wait for a fever to pass on its own.

## Depending on where you are

- **In French Guiana**: the Amazonian Park asks visitors to tell their host and points out that mobile coverage is limited. The emergency numbers are 15 and 112.
- **French nationals abroad**: register with "Fil d'Ariane", the free service of the French foreign ministry that sends security alerts. Other countries run similar services for their citizens.

The walking rules come from the Guiana Amazonian Park guides for visitors to Saül and Papaïchton: they are written for French Guiana and this guide extends them to other tropical forests. The health advice comes from the French foreign ministry and the French High Council for Public Health. Keeping the group within earshot, checking before you sit down, stopping as soon as you are lost and telling the doctor where you traveled are common sense.
