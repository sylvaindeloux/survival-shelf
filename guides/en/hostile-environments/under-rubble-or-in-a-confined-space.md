# Under rubble or in a confined space

> **In short**: trapped under rubble, move as little as possible, cover your nose and mouth with a cloth, light no flame and signal by tapping on a pipe or a wall, or with a whistle. Shout only as a last resort, because of the dust. If someone collapses in a well, a tank, a pit or a cellar, do not go down: call the emergency services.

## When to use it

- A building has collapsed after an earthquake, an explosion or a landslide, and you are trapped inside.
- You can hear someone under rubble.
- Someone has collapsed in a well, a pit, a tank, a silo, a cellar or a crawl space.

## What you need

- A small metal whistle, which everyone carries on them in earthquake-prone regions.
- By the bed: a flashlight, sturdy shoes, a dust mask.
- A charged phone.

## Steps

### Trapped under rubble

1. **Stay calm** and check whether you are injured.
2. **Move as little as possible**, so that you do not kick up dust or shift what is on top of you.
3. **Cover your nose and mouth** with a handkerchief, a piece of clothing or whatever you have at hand.
4. **Do not light a match or a lighter**: there may be a gas leak.
5. **Signal**:
   - tap on a pipe or a wall, three knocks in a row, and repeat every few minutes;
   - blow a whistle, if you have one;
   - shine a flashlight;
   - send a text or call, if the phone works.
6. **Shout only as a last resort**: when you shout, you breathe in a dangerous amount of dust.
7. **Expect aftershocks** after an earthquake: protect your head and neck.

### Someone is under the rubble

1. **Call the emergency services** and say where you saw or heard the person.
2. **Do not try to free them by moving the rubble**: you could make things worse.
3. **Do not go into a damaged building.**
4. **Talk to them** from a safe place, tell them help is coming, and guide the rescuers when they arrive.

### Confined space: well, pit, tank, silo, cellar

1. **Understand the danger.** In a closed, poorly ventilated space, the air can lack oxygen, be toxic or explode. Manure and slurry give off methane, hydrogen sulfide and carbon dioxide.
2. **Do not go in** if you are neither equipped nor trained, even for a moment.
3. **Do not trust a candle**: a flame that stays lit does not prove the air is safe to breathe.
4. **Someone has collapsed inside: do not go down.** The risk of a second accident is at its highest: would-be rescuers die this way next to the first victim.
5. **Call the emergency services** and say: "accident in a confined space, with asphyxiation".
6. **Keep others out** and, if you can do it from outside, open whatever lets air in.

## Never do this

- Light a flame under rubble or in a damaged building.
- Shout nonstop in the dust.
- Move rubble with your bare hands to reach someone.
- Go down into a well, a pit or a tank to rescue an unconscious person.
- Go into an abandoned mine or tunnel: the air inside may be unbreathable.

## Without equipment

- **No whistle**: knock with a stone, a shoe or a hard object on metal or concrete.
- **No cloth**: breathe through your sleeve or the collar of your clothing.
