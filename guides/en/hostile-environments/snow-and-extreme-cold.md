# Snow and extreme cold

> **In short**: in extreme cold, it is the wind that freezes skin: cover everything, stay dry and limit your time outdoors. When the wind chill drops below -27 (about -17 °F), bare skin can freeze in 30 minutes, and in 2 minutes in the worst conditions. Caught by bad weather, stop, get out of the wind, insulate yourself from the snow and wait.

## When to use it

- Before going out in extreme cold, in snow or in an icy wind.
- You are caught outdoors by a snowstorm, fog or nightfall.
- Someone's fingers, nose or cheeks are white and numb.

## What you need

- Several layers of clothing, including a windproof and waterproof outer layer.
- A hat, mittens, a scarf or a face mask.
- Dry spare clothes.
- A survival blanket, a light, a whistle, a charged phone.
- A hot drink and something to eat.

## Steps

### Before you go out

1. **Check the forecast**, wind included. Put the outing off if cold or a storm is on the way.
2. **Dress in loose layers**: next to the skin, wool or synthetics rather than cotton; over that, a warm layer; on the outside, a waterproof windbreaker.
3. **Cover all your skin**: hat, scarf or mask over nose and mouth, mittens, which are warmer than gloves.
4. **Tell someone** where you are going and when you will be back, and do not go alone.

### Outdoors

1. **Limit your time outside** and get out of the wind whenever you stop.
2. **Stay dry.** Take off a layer before any effort so that you do not sweat, and change wet clothing at once.
3. **Keep active**, without tiring yourself: move your arms, legs and fingers.
4. **Eat and drink**, warm and sweet if possible. No alcohol.
5. **Do not eat snow**: it cools you down. Melt it, and boil the water for 1 minute before drinking it.
6. **Watch each other**:
   - white or grayish-yellow skin, hard and numb, on the nose, ears, cheeks, fingers or toes: this is frostbite;
   - shivering that will not stop, clumsiness, slurred speech, confusion, drowsiness: this is hypothermia, call the emergency services.

### Caught by bad weather

1. **Stop** before you are exhausted or lost.
2. **Get out of the wind**: a building, a car, a rock, or a shelter dug in the snow, which insulates well from the cold.
3. **Insulate yourself from the snow** with your pack, branches, a rope.
4. **Huddle together** and cover yourselves with everything you have.
5. **Do not light a stove in a closed shelter or a tent**: carbon monoxide kills. Any flame needs ventilation.
6. **Signal** (see [Call for help without a phone signal](../communication-and-navigation/call-for-help-without-a-phone-signal.md)).

### Frostbite or hypothermia

1. **Get the person into shelter** and replace their wet clothes.
2. **Frostbite**: rewarm the area in lukewarm water, 38 °C (100 °F) at most, without rubbing. Do not walk on a frostbitten foot.
3. **Hypothermia**: wrap the person in blankets, head included. No hot bath, no hot water bottle, no rubbing, no alcohol.
4. **Follow the guide** [Hypothermia and frostbite](../health-and-first-aid/hypothermia-and-frostbite.md).

## Never do this

- Rub frostbite, with your hand or with snow.
- Hold frostbite close to a fire, a radiator or a stove.
- Keep wet clothes on.
- Drink alcohol to warm up.
- Keep moving in a storm when you cannot see where you are going.
- Venture onto a frozen lake without knowing how thick the ice is.

## Without equipment

- **No mittens**: tuck your hands under your armpits.
- **No blanket**: newspaper or dry leaves between two layers of clothing.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: follow the "grand froid" and "neige-verglas" weather warnings (see [Extreme cold, snow and ice](../disasters/extreme-cold-snow-and-ice.md)).
- **In the mountains, off the marked runs**: see [Avalanche](../disasters/avalanche.md).
- **In a car**: stay in the vehicle (see [Stuck in snow or cold](../in-the-car/stuck-in-snow-or-cold.md)).
- **On ice**: see [Fallen into cold water](../outdoors/fallen-into-cold-water.md).

For hypothermia, this guide keeps the most cautious advice, that of the French Ministry of Health and the NHS: no direct heat source. Tucking your hands under your armpits comes from the NHS; the rest of the "Without equipment" section takes up CDC advice written for someone stranded in a car.
