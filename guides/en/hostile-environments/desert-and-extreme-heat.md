# Desert and extreme heat

> **In short**: in the desert, water and shade keep you alive. Carry at least 4 liters (1 gallon) of water per person per day, far more if it is very hot, and tell someone where you are going. Do not walk in the heat of the day. If you break down, stay near the vehicle, in the shade. Headache, dizziness, nausea: stop, get into the shade and drink in small sips.

## When to use it

- Before crossing a desert or very remote region, by car or on foot.
- In extreme heat, far from any water and any shelter.
- Broken down or lost in a place with no shade.

## What you need

- **Water**: at least 4 liters (1 gallon) per person per day. For remote travel, South Australia Police count 6 liters (1.6 gal) in mild weather and 10 liters (2.6 gal) in extreme heat, with a reserve for 3 to 4 more days.
- Food for several days.
- A wide-brimmed hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, loose light-colored clothes.
- Something to make shade: an umbrella, a tarp.
- A charged phone, and a satellite phone or a distress beacon: coverage is scarce or absent.
- A paper map, a light, a first-aid kit, blankets.

## Steps

### Before you leave

1. **Leave your route in writing** with someone, with the times you expect to pass each point and the time you will check in.
2. **Prepare the vehicle**: full tank, tires checked and a spare wheel, two if possible, coolant, fuel stops planned.
3. **Carry the water in several containers**, so that you do not lose it all if one leaks.
4. **Check the weather**: heat, but also thunderstorms.

### Out there

1. **Do not walk in the heat of the day**, especially on valley floors.
2. **Drink regularly**, without waiting to be thirsty, and eat proper meals, to replace the salt you lose in sweat.
3. **Stay in the shade** whenever you stop, head and skin covered.
4. **Watch where you put your hands and feet**: snakes and scorpions. Shake out your shoes and clothes before putting them on.
5. **Leave canyons and dry riverbeds as soon as it rains**: a flash flood can arrive there without warning. Climb to higher ground.
6. **Do not go into an abandoned mine or tunnel**: the air inside may be unbreathable.
7. **Drive slowly**: in Death Valley, the leading cause of death is a single vehicle rolling over.

### Broken down or lost

1. **Stay near the vehicle**: it is far easier to find than a person on foot. Raise the hood.
2. **Get into the shade.** If the inside of the car gets too hot, everyone gets out into the shade of the vehicle.
3. **Drink**, and make everyone drink.
4. **Call the emergency services** or trigger your beacon, then signal (see [Broken down in a remote place or in extreme heat](../in-the-car/broken-down-in-a-remote-place-or-in-extreme-heat.md)).

### If someone is unwell

1. **Nausea, vomiting, tiredness, headache, cramps, pale and clammy skin**: this is heat exhaustion. Stop all effort, get the person into the shade, have them drink small sips every few minutes, wet their skin and fan them.
2. **They are no better after 30 minutes, become confused, have hot dry skin or lose consciousness**: this is heatstroke. Call the emergency services and keep cooling them (see [Heatstroke](../health-and-first-aid/heatstroke.md)).

## Never do this

- Leave the vehicle to set off on foot in the full heat.
- Leave a child or an animal in a stopped vehicle.
- Walk in a canyon during a thunderstorm.
- Drink alcohol.
- Leave without telling anyone where you are going.

## Without equipment

- **No shelter**: get into the shade of the vehicle or of a rock and cover your head with a piece of clothing.
- **Not enough water**: stop moving in the heat of the day and stay in the shade. Do not drink urine or seawater.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: during a heat wave, the same signs mean calling 15 or 112 (see [Heat wave](../disasters/heat-wave.md)).
- **In the United States**: the National Park Service counts in gallons, "at least one gallon a day", which is 3.8 liters.
- **At night**: depending on the season and the altitude, it can get cold. Carry a blanket.

This guide relies on the US National Park Service (Death Valley), South Australia Police and the Arizona Department of Transportation. The amounts of water differ: at least 3.8 liters a day for the first, 6 to 10 liters for the second. This guide gives the minimum rounded to 4 liters and quotes the higher benchmark. Splitting the water between several containers, the "Without equipment" section and the remark about cold nights are common sense, with no official source.
