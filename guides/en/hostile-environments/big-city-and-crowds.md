# Big city and crowds

> **In short**: in a city, watch out above all for crowds, public transport and enclosed or underground places. Spot the exits when you arrive, stay at the edge of the crowd and move away as soon as it tightens. Caught inside, stay on your feet, hands in front of your chest, and go with the flow toward the edge. Never go down onto the tracks. After a disaster, do not rush home: wait in a safe place.

## When to use it

- You are going to a large gathering: a concert, a game, a festival, fireworks.
- You take the subway or a train, or walk through a crowded station or underground mall.
- A disaster or a major breakdown stops public transport while you are far from home.

For a demonstration that turns violent, see [Riots and unrest](../disasters/riots-and-unrest.md). For an attack, see [Terrorist attack](../disasters/terrorist-attack.md).

## What you need

- Comfortable shoes, with the laces tied.
- An identity document on you.
- A small bag that closes, rather than a backpack.
- Little cash, and your means of payment kept in several places.
- A charged phone, and several ways to reach your family.
- At work: walking shoes, and the route home on foot worked out in advance.

## Steps

### Before entering a crowded place

1. **Spot the exits** and the first-aid station as soon as you arrive.
2. **Agree on a meeting place** in case you get separated.
3. **Dress to move**: no long, flowing clothes or jewelry that can get caught. Bright clothes help your companions find you.
4. **Keep your wallet and phone** in a closed inside pocket, never in a back pocket or an outside pocket of your bag.

### In the crowd

1. **Stay at the edge**, or where the crowd is least dense.
2. **Walk at the same speed as the others**, on level, dry ground, avoiding anything you could trip over.
3. **Keep space around children.** They, shorter people and people with breathing problems are the most at risk.
4. **Look behind you now and then**: could you still get out if you had to?
5. **Watch the crowd before you enter it**: if it moves, then stops, again and again, things may go wrong.
6. **Move away as soon as it tightens.** At five or six people per square meter (about one person for every 2 square feet), a crowd starts to become a problem, and that is hard to judge by eye.
7. **Beware of staged jostling**: pickpockets use it to distract you.

### Caught in a crowd crush

1. **Stay on your feet**: feet planted firmly, do everything you can not to fall.
2. **Raise your hands in front of your chest, like a boxer**, so that it is not squeezed.
3. **Do not fight the push and do not push**: go with the flow, calmly.
4. **Do not bend down**, even to pick up your phone: stopping can make people fall over one another.
5. **As soon as the pressure eases, work your way diagonally to the edge.**
6. **If you fall**, curl into a ball to protect yourself and get up as soon as you can.

### On the subway, on a train or in a station

1. **Stand well back from the platform edge** and do not run to catch a train.
2. **Never go down onto the tracks**, even to pick something up: tell a member of staff. The footing is unstable, high-voltage cables may run beside the rails and a train may come on the next track.
3. **Someone is on the tracks**: do not go down. Raise the alarm at once with the call point on the platform, or the emergency stop button if there is one.
4. **Serious emergency on board**: pull the alarm.
5. **Fire or smoke in your car**: move to a car away from the fire and tell the driver. Do not leave the train on your own. When it reaches a station, get out at once, following instructions.
6. **Train stopped between two stations**: stay on board. The emergency lights come on: wait for the driver's instructions.
7. **Fire or smoke in a station**: follow the staff or the evacuation signs and get out without waiting.

### In an underground mall, a hall or a stadium

1. **Do not rush to the exit** or the staircase where everyone is piling up.
2. **Look for another emergency exit, walking along the walls.**
3. **Power cut**: do not move without good reason, and wait for the emergency lights to come on.
4. **Listen to the announcements** and follow the staff's instructions.
5. **After a tremor or a power cut, take the stairs**, not the elevator (see [Stuck in an elevator](../at-home/stuck-in-an-elevator.md)).

### Stranded far from home

1. **Do not rush home.** Stay in a safe place: your workplace, your school, wherever you are.
2. **Do not hurry to the station**: after a disaster, service is suspended for safety checks.
3. **Leave the streets clear.** If everyone walks home at the same time, the crowd blocks the emergency services during the first 72 hours, when they save the most lives, and you are exposed to the aftershocks of an earthquake.
4. **Let your family know you are safe** (see [Reach your family when the network is overloaded](../communication-and-navigation/reach-your-family-when-the-network-is-overloaded.md)).
5. **When the authorities open temporary shelters**, go there calmly.
6. **Walk home once the way is safe**, in good shoes, along the route you worked out (see [Evacuate on foot](../outdoors/evacuate-on-foot.md)).

### Theft or assault

1. **Someone snatches your bag: do not resist**, you could get hurt.
2. **Someone attacks you: shout to scare the attacker off** and take refuge in the nearest shop, which will call the police.
3. **Alert the people around you and call the police.** Give the place, the number of thieves, their description and which way they went.
4. **File a report** as soon as possible, with a detailed list of what was stolen.

## Never do this

- Go down onto the tracks.
- Fight the movement of a crowd, push, or bend down in the middle of it.
- Rush to the exit where everyone is piling up.
- Leave a train stopped between two stations on your own.
- Hurry to the station or onto the road right after a disaster.
- Resist someone who snatches your bag.

## Without equipment

- **Nothing to protect your head** when objects are falling: use your bag.
- **No phone**: ask a member of the station staff or a shopkeeper.
- **Separated from your companions**: go to the agreed meeting place and wait there.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: the police answer on 17, or on 112. On a train or in a station, call 3117, free, day and night, or text 31177 if you cannot speak.
- **In France, with nowhere to sleep**: 115, free and open day and night, directs homeless people to a shelter. The town hall can also help you.
- **When traveling**: keep a photocopy of your papers where you are staying. Lost papers: contact your consulate. Stolen papers: report it to the police.
- **Earthquake downtown**: protect your head, move away from buildings, as glass and signs fall from their fronts, and make for an open space such as a park (see [Earthquake](../disasters/earthquake.md)).
- **High winds**: see [Storm and high winds](../disasters/storm-and-high-winds.md).

No official French page read says what to do in a crowd crush: the actions come from the US CDC. The warning signs come from a researcher at York University (Canada) quoted by Scientific American, a magazine and not an authority. In the same article, another researcher doubts that the position of the hands really protects you and points out that deep inside a very dense crowd there is almost nothing left you can do: that is why this guide insists on moving away early. The advice for the subway, underground places and getting home comes mostly from Japanese sources written for earthquakes: this guide extends it to other situations, and the equipment (emergency stop button on platforms, emergency exits every 60 m (200 ft) in underground malls) varies from country to country. The advice on theft and assault comes from a leaflet published by the French prefecture of Charente and from Paris police advice written for visitors to Paris. In France, 115 is meant for homeless people. Asking a member of staff or a shopkeeper when you have no phone and waiting at the meeting place are common sense, with no official source.
