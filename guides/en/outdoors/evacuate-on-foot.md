# Evacuate on foot

> **In short**: leave early, in daylight if you can, with a pack you can carry for hours, good shoes, water, your documents and a light. Say where you are going, follow the route given by the authorities and stay together. On a road with no sidewalk, walk facing the traffic, and make yourself visible at night.

## When to use it

- The authorities ask you to evacuate and you have no vehicle, or the roads are cut or jammed.
- The danger is too close to wait: rising water, fire, tsunami.
- Your car has broken down or is stuck on the way.

## What you need

- One backpack per person (see [Pack a go-bag](../basics/pack-a-go-bag.md)).
- Water: 1 liter weighs 1 kg (2.2 lb), take what you can carry.
- Sturdy closed shoes, long pants, long sleeves, a cap, a rain jacket.
- Everyone's documents, medicines, cash in small bills.
- A light, a battery radio, a phone and a power bank.
- Light-colored clothes or reflective armbands.

## Steps

### Before you leave

1. **Follow the instructions of the authorities**: route, reception center, time.
2. **Lighten the pack.** Keep water, documents, medicines, food that needs no cooking and something to keep you warm.
3. **Close up your home**: doors and windows, water, gas and electricity if you are asked to, and take your keys.
4. **Leave a note** with the time you left and where you are going, and tell a relative.
5. **Check whether a neighbor needs help** to leave.

### On the way

1. **Stay together**, at the pace of the slowest, and count everyone at each stop.
2. **Follow the recommended route**, with no shortcuts. Go around flooded roads, damaged bridges and fallen power lines.
3. **On the road**:
   - use the sidewalk or the shoulder when there is one;
   - outside towns, alone or in single file, keep to the edge of the road that faces oncoming vehicles;
   - in an organized group, walk on the side of the traffic flow, two abreast, leaving the rest of the road clear;
   - a long column splits into sections of 20 m (65 ft) at most, 50 m (165 ft) apart.
4. **At night**, wear light-colored or reflective clothes. A group shows a white or yellow light at the front and a red light at the rear.
5. **Take regular breaks**: drink, eat a little, check feet, children and older people.
6. **Listen to the radio** to follow instructions (see [Get information without the internet](../communication-and-navigation/get-information-without-the-internet.md)).
7. **If night catches you**, stop in a safe place rather than carrying on in the dark (see [Spend an unplanned night outdoors](spend-an-unplanned-night-outdoors.md)).

### On arrival

1. **Report in** at the reception center and tell your relatives.
2. **Do not go back** before the authorities allow it.

## Never do this

- Walk on a motorway or freeway: pedestrians are banned there.
- Walk through moving water or along a flooded road.
- Take a shortcut off the indicated route.
- Leave someone behind or split from the group.
- Turn back to fetch an object.

## Without equipment

- **No backpack**: a pillowcase or a shopping bag slung across your body leaves your hands free.
- **No reflective armband**: a white piece of clothing, or a lit flashlight held on the road side.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: the rules above are those of the French highway code. Vehicles drive on the right, so a lone walker keeps to the left edge and an organized group to the right.
- **French nationals abroad**: only the embassy decides on a regrouping or an evacuation. Follow its instructions and those of your local warden. On an evacuation flight, luggage is often limited to 10 kg (22 lb).
- **Conflict zone**: see [Conflict zone](../hostile-environments/conflict-zone.md).
- **Tsunami**: leave on foot, at once, for high ground (see [Tsunami](../disasters/tsunami.md)).
