# Lost in the wild

> **In short**: as soon as nothing looks familiar, stop: sit down, drink, think, observe, then decide. Retrace your steps only if you are sure of the way. If not, or if night is falling, or if you are injured or exhausted, stay where you are: call the emergency services, make yourself visible, blow your whistle, and protect yourself from the cold before dark.

## When to use it

- You have lost the trail or the markers, and you no longer recognize the landscape.
- Fog or darkness catches you far from where you started.
- Someone in the group is missing.

## What you need

- A phone, a whistle, a light.
- A map and a compass.
- Water, a warm layer, a survival blanket.

## Steps

### At the first doubt

1. **Stop.** Panic is your worst enemy: sit down, drink, eat a little, breathe.
2. **Think**: which way did you come? What is the last place you recognized, and how long ago? Do not move without a specific reason.
3. **Observe**: map, compass, trail markers, signs, the shape of the land, the sound of a road or of water.
4. **Decide.** List your options:
   - you are sure of the way: retrace your steps to the last known point;
   - you are not sure, night is falling, you are injured or exhausted: stay put.

### If you stay put

1. **Dial the emergency number**, even if your phone shows no signal. Give your position, or describe what you can see around you. After that, do not move from the spot until the rescuers have called you back.
2. **Save the battery** (see [Make your phone battery last](../communication-and-navigation/make-your-phone-battery-last.md)).
3. **Move into the open**, to a clear spot close by, and wear or spread out the brightest things you have.
4. **Signal**: six whistle blasts or six flashes of light in a row, a one-minute pause, then again. Keep going even if someone answers, until they reach you.
5. **Get ready for the night while it is still light**: shelter, insulation from the ground, dry clothes (see [Spend an unplanned night outdoors](spend-an-unplanned-night-outdoors.md)).
6. **Rest before you are exhausted**, and drink.

### If you decide to move

1. **Only if** you know where you are and where you are going, or if you are certain that nobody will come looking for you.
2. **Leave a message** in plain sight at the place you are leaving: your destination, your route, the time, your condition.
3. **As a last resort**, follow a small valley or a stream downhill: the going is often hard, but it may lead to a path or a road.

### If someone in the group is missing

1. **Stop** and gather everyone.
2. **Go back to the last place** where the person was seen and call out. Check the meeting point you agreed on.
3. **Call the emergency services** and give that last place and the time.

### What to teach children

1. **As soon as you feel lost, stop and stay by a tree**: you will be found sooner if you do not move.
2. **Stay warm and dry.**
3. **Make yourself big and visible**, and answer when someone calls you. Blow your whistle if you have one.
4. **Nobody will be angry with you.**

## Never do this

- Keep walking in the hope of recognizing something.
- Cut across ground you do not know to save time.
- Walk at night, in fog, injured or exhausted.
- Split up to look for the way.

## Without equipment

- **No phone and no whistle**: stand in the open and wave both arms above your head as soon as you see or hear someone.
- **To be seen from the air**: make large letters on the ground ("SOS") with stones, branches or clothes, in a color that stands out.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: dial 112. If you cannot speak or hear, text 114.
- **In the United States**: the distress signal is a series of three whistle blasts, not six.
- **In a tropical forest**: see [Tropical forest](../hostile-environments/tropical-forest.md).
- **In extreme cold**: see [Snow and extreme cold](../hostile-environments/snow-and-extreme-cold.md).
