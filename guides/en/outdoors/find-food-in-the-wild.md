# Find food in the wild

> **In short**: food comes last. You can last about 3 weeks without eating, but only 3 days without water and 3 hours without shelter in severe cold. Eat a plant, a berry or a mushroom only if you know it perfectly: at the slightest doubt, do not eat it. Save your strength for shelter, water and signals, and ration what you brought.

## When to use it

- You are stuck outdoors longer than planned and your supplies are running low.
- You wonder whether you can eat what you find around you.

This guide does not teach you to identify plants and mushrooms: without local knowledge, the risk of a fatal mistake is too high.

## Steps

### Everything else first

1. **Take care of shelter, water and signals** before looking for food: they are what keeps you alive during the first days.
2. **Take stock** of what you have and spread it over several days (see [Ration food](../food/ration-food.md)).
3. **Drink.** Always treat water found outdoors (see [Find water outdoors](../water/find-water-outdoors.md)).

### If you forage anyway

1. **Pick only what you know perfectly.** Do not improvise.
2. **At the slightest doubt, do not eat it.**
3. **Do not trust an identification app** on your phone.
4. **Do not pick by the armful**: you risk mixing a toxic plant in with the others.
5. **Take a photo of what you picked** before preparing it: in case of poisoning, the photo helps identify what you ate.
6. **Stop eating at once** if the taste is bitter or unpleasant.
7. **Wash thoroughly, or better, cook** berries, plants and mushrooms that grow close to the ground: they can carry the eggs of a parasite, the fox tapeworm. Freezing does not kill them.
8. **Mushrooms**: only those you know perfectly, always cooked for at least 20 minutes, and never for a young child.
9. **Wash your hands** after touching the soil.

### If someone feels unwell after eating

1. **Call a poison center or a doctor at once**, without waiting for the signs to get worse.
2. **Call the emergency services** if the person has trouble breathing, loses consciousness or no longer responds.
3. **Keep the photo and the leftovers** of what was eaten, and note the time of the meal.

## Never do this

- Eat a plant, a berry or a mushroom that "looks like" an edible species.
- Taste to check.
- Give foraged mushrooms to a young child.
- Eat wild mushrooms raw.
- Wear yourself out looking for food when you have neither shelter nor water.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: a pharmacist or a mycology society can check foraged mushrooms. In six months, from July to December 2024, 1,363 people called a poison center after eating some, and three died. Autumn crocus, mistaken for wild garlic, caused two deaths between 2020 and 2022.
- **Abroad**: species change from one region to another. A plant that looks like one you know at home may be toxic.

The "3 hours, 3 days, 3 weeks" benchmark comes from US Air Force survival instructors, for freezing temperatures. The foraging rules of the French food safety agency are written for walkers, not for a survival situation: this guide applies them as they are. Hunting, fishing and trapping are not covered, for lack of an official source read. Keeping the leftovers, noting the time of the meal and the warning about species abroad are common sense.
