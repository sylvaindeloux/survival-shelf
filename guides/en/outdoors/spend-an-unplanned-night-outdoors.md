# Spend an unplanned night outdoors

> **In short**: decide early. As soon as you realize you will not be back before dark, stop and settle in while it is still light. Look for a shelter that already exists, insulate yourself from the ground, put on everything dry you have, and huddle together. In severe cold, you last about 3 hours without shelter.

## When to use it

- Night is falling and you are still far away: lost, late, injured, or stopped by bad weather.
- You are waiting for rescue and it will not arrive before morning.

## What you need

- A survival blanket, a tarp or a bivy bag.
- Warm, dry clothes, a hat, gloves.
- A light, a whistle, water, something to eat.

## Steps

1. **Stop while it is still light.** Walking at night on unknown ground is more dangerous than waiting for morning.
2. **Let people know if you have a signal**: the person waiting for you, and the emergency services if you are lost, if someone is injured or exhausted, or if it is cold.
3. **Choose the spot**: flat, out of the wind, away from dead trees, riverbeds and avalanche paths.
4. **First look for a shelter that already exists**: a hut, a cabin, a building, a car, a rock that breaks the wind. Otherwise, set up a sloping roof with a tarp or branches (see [Build an emergency shelter](../cold-and-heat/build-an-emergency-shelter.md)).
5. **Insulate yourself from the ground**, which draws your heat away: a thick layer of branches, leaves or dry moss, your backpack, a rope.
6. **Stay dry.** Replace wet clothing at once, put on all your layers, cover your head, neck and hands, then wrap yourself in the survival blanket.
7. **Huddle together.**
8. **Eat and drink**, warm and sweet if possible. No alcohol: it makes you lose heat.
9. **Move** your arms, legs and fingers from time to time, without working up a sweat.
10. **Light a fire only if it is safe**: it warms you and shows where you are (see [Light a fire](../fire-and-power/light-a-fire.md)).
11. **Stay easy to find**: light and whistle within reach, bright clothing in plain sight.
12. **Watch each other.** Shivering that will not stop, clumsy movements, slurred speech, confusion or drowsiness are signs of hypothermia: call the emergency services (see [Hypothermia and frostbite](../health-and-first-aid/hypothermia-and-frostbite.md)).
13. **Wait for daylight before setting off again.** If you are lost, stay put (see [Lost in the wild](lost-in-the-wild.md)).

## Never do this

- Lie directly on the ground or on snow.
- Keep wet clothes on.
- Drink alcohol to warm up.
- Light a fire or a stove in a closed shelter, a tent or a car: risk of carbon monoxide poisoning.
- Light a fire in a forest in dry weather or strong wind.
- Shelter under a rocky overhang or a lone tree during a thunderstorm.

## Without equipment

- **No blanket and no sleeping bag**: stuff dry leaves or newspaper between your layers of clothing.
- **No tarp**: sit against a rock or a fallen trunk that breaks the wind, and cover yourself with branches.

## Depending on where you are

- **Near your car**: stay inside it (see [Sleep in your car](../in-the-car/sleep-in-your-car.md)).
- **In snow**: see [Snow and extreme cold](../hostile-environments/snow-and-extreme-cold.md).
- **In a thunderstorm**: see [Caught in a thunderstorm](caught-in-a-thunderstorm.md).

The choice of the spot and the insulation from the ground come from a US armed forces survival manual, and the 3-hour benchmark comes from US Air Force survival instructors, for freezing temperatures. The CDC advice on cold (huddling, moving, wrapping yourself in newspaper) is written for someone stranded in a car: this guide extends it to a night outdoors. Calling the emergency services in the cases listed and waiting for daylight before setting off again are common sense.
