# Plan an outing

> **In short**: choose a route that suits the least fit person in the group, check the weather, and tell someone where you are going and when you will be back. Carry what you need to drink, eat, keep warm, see in the dark, find your way and call for help. Once out there, be ready to turn back: phones do not work everywhere and rescue can take hours.

## When to use it

- Before a hike, a walk in the woods, a day in the mountains or by the water, even a short one.
- Before going on foot into a place you do not know, or far from a road.

## What you need

- **Water and food**, with a reserve: enough to eat for one extra day, without cooking.
- **Clothing** against the sun (sunglasses, cap, sunscreen), the wind (windbreaker), the cold (sweater, long pants, hat, gloves) and the rain, even if the weather is fine when you leave. Bright colors make you easier to spot.
- **A charged phone**, with the emergency numbers saved, and a power bank.
- **A detailed paper map and a compass**, even if you have a GPS or a hiking app.
- **A light**, ideally a headlamp, with spare batteries.
- **A whistle and a knife.**
- **A first-aid kit** with pressure dressings, bandages, tape and a survival blanket.
- **Something to light a fire** (waterproof matches or a lighter) and **a light emergency shelter**: a tarp, a bivy bag or a second survival blanket.

## Steps

### Before you leave

1. **Choose the route for the weakest member of the group**, not the strongest: experience, fitness, age. Ask professionals who know the area for advice.
2. **Check the weather forecast**, the day before and again in the morning. Plan an alternative route, and give up if the conditions look bad.
3. **Set a turnaround time**: after that time you head back, even if the goal is close.
4. **Tell someone where you are going**: the route, the starting point, the time you expect to be back, who is with you, your car and where it is parked. Agree on the time after which that person raises the alarm.
5. **Do not go alone** if you can avoid it.
6. **Charge your phone** and save the emergency numbers.
7. **Check your insurance**: not every policy covers search and evacuation costs, especially abroad.
8. **Mountain hut**: book your place ahead, and cancel if your plans change.

### During the outing

1. **Stay together**, at the pace of the slowest. Agree on a meeting point in case someone strays.
2. **Drink and eat regularly**, without waiting to be thirsty.
3. **Watch the children**: they spend their energy without counting, and get dehydrated and exhausted quickly.
4. **Stay on marked trails** and on the route you announced. If you change it, let someone know.
5. **Watch the sky and the time.** Be ready to give up, turn back or shorten the outing.
6. **In the forest**: do not smoke, do not light a barbecue and respect access bans.
7. **Near livestock**: keep your distance (see [Meet a wild animal](meet-a-wild-animal.md)).

### When you are back

1. **Tell the person** who had your route, right away.

## Never do this

- Rely on your phone alone: there is no signal in many places, especially in the mountains.
- Leave without telling anyone where you are going.
- Press on when the weather turns, when it is getting late or when someone is tiring.
- Leave someone alone at the back, or go on ahead.

## Without equipment

- No map: take a photo of the board at the trailhead and write down the names of the places you pass.
- No whistle and no survival blanket: shorten the outing and stay on a busy route.
