# Caught in a thunderstorm

> **In short**: outdoors, no place is safe during a thunderstorm. At the first thunderclap, get to a solid building or a closed car. If there is none: leave ridges and summits, move away from lone trees, water and metal, spread out, and stay low with your feet together, never lying down. Wait 30 minutes after the last thunderclap.

## When to use it

- The sky darkens or thunder rumbles during a hike, a night in a tent, a swim, a boat trip.
- Thunderstorms are forecast and you are planning an outing.

## Before

1. **Check the forecast** and put off or shorten the outing if thunderstorms are expected.
2. **Spot the safe shelters** along your route: a solid building, a car.
3. **Turn back as soon as the sky darkens.** If you can hear thunder, lightning is already close enough to strike you.
4. **When camping**, pitch the tent in a low spot, away from tall lone trees.

## During

### A shelter is within reach

1. **Go there without delay**: a solid building with electricity or plumbing, or a car with a metal roof and the windows closed.
2. **Do not count on** an open hut, a canopy or a tent, small or large: they do not protect you from lightning.

### No shelter

1. **Get off high ground at once**: hills, ridges, summits.
2. **Move away from lone trees** and from anything that stands tall. Do not shelter under a rocky overhang or at the foot of a cliff.
3. **Avoid wide open spaces**, where you are the highest point.
4. **Get out of the water** and away from ponds, lakes and rivers.
5. **Stay away from anything that conducts electricity**: fences, power lines, pylons, cables.
6. **Spread out**: if lightning strikes someone, the others can give first aid.
7. **Stay as low as you can, feet together.** Never lie flat on the ground.

These actions lower the risk a little, without removing it: the only real protection is a shelter.

## After

1. **Wait 30 minutes** after the last thunderclap before moving on.
2. **Someone has been struck**: call the emergency services. If they do not respond and are not breathing, start chest compressions (see [Cardiac arrest](../health-and-first-aid/cardiac-arrest.md)).
3. **Beware of mountain streams**: after a storm, the water can rise suddenly.
4. **Change your clothes** if you are soaked: the cold sets in fast.

## Never do this

- Shelter under a lone tree.
- Shelter under a rocky overhang or at the foot of a cliff.
- Stay in the water or on a boat.
- Lie down on the ground.
- Think your tent protects you.
- Move on because the rain has stopped while thunder is still rumbling.

## Depending on where you are

- **In the mountains**: come down from ridges and summits at the first signs.
- **On the water or at the beach**: get to land and to shelter at once.
- **In a car**: see [Thunderstorm, high winds, wildfire or earthquake while driving](../in-the-car/thunderstorm-high-winds-wildfire-or-earthquake-while-driving.md).
- **In dry weather**: lightning can start a fire (see [Wildfire](../disasters/wildfire.md)).

The Météo-France page asks people outdoors to avoid open spaces, high ground and trees, without describing a position to adopt: "low, feet together, group spread out" and the 30-minute rule come from the US National Weather Service, which presents them as a last resort. Changing your clothes after the storm is common sense.
