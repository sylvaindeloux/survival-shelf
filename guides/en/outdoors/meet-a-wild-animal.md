# Meet a wild animal

> **In short**: most wild animals avoid you. Make a little noise so that you do not surprise them, keep your distance, never feed them and keep your dog close. Facing an animal: stay calm, talk, back away slowly without running and always leave it a way out. Wash any bite at length with soapy water and see a doctor quickly.

## When to use it

- Hiking, camping or in the countryside, in a region where bears, wolves, wild boar or snakes live.
- In mountain pastures, near a flock guarded by livestock guardian dogs.
- When traveling, around stray animals.

## Steps

### To avoid a bad encounter

1. **Make a little noise** as you walk: an animal that hears you coming may move away.
2. **Keep your distance.** Do not approach an animal, even from far away, even for a photo.
3. **Do not follow tracks**, and never get between a mother and her young.
4. **Do not feed any animal**, and take all your trash away with you.
5. **Keep your dog close**, on a leash where it is required: a roaming dog can provoke an animal and bring it back to you.
6. **Do not pet a wild or stray animal**, even a calm one.

### Livestock guardian dog

1. **Go widely around the flock.** Signs usually announce that dogs are present.
2. **On a bike, get off** before you come near the flock.
3. **Stay calm and passive** when the dog approaches: it comes to sniff and identify you, then goes back to its flock. It may also try to intimidate you.
4. **If it intimidates you, turn back slowly.**
5. **Do not try** to feed, pet or photograph it, or the ewes, or the lambs.

### Bear

1. **Closer than 50 m (55 yd), help it identify you**: show yourself calmly, move, talk.
2. **Move away little by little**, keeping clear of the path it might take to flee.
3. **Do not run.**
4. **A bear standing on its hind legs is not being aggressive**: it is curious and trying to identify you.

### Wolf

1. **Wolves avoid people**: in France, the wolf moved away or fled in nearly 8 out of 10 encounters studied between 1993 and 2020. Out of about 4,000 recorded behaviors, 10 were perceived as aggressive, and nobody was injured.
2. **Apply the general rules**: distance, calm, no food, dog kept close.

### Wild boar

1. **Do not approach it** and do not feed it.
2. **Make noise to scare it off**, without putting yourself in danger.

### Snake

1. **Wear closed shoes** and covering clothes.
2. **Watch where you put your hands and feet**, and do not reach into vegetation with bare hands.
3. **Bite**: call the emergency services, keep the limb still, take off rings and bracelets. Do not cut, do not suck, do not apply a tourniquet or ice (see [Bites and stings](../health-and-first-aid/bites-and-stings.md)).

### If you are bitten or scratched

1. **Wash the wound thoroughly** with soapy water.
2. **See a doctor as soon as possible**, even if you are vaccinated against rabies. It is spread through saliva (a bite, a scratch, a licked wound) and can no longer be cured once the first signs appear.

## Never do this

- Run.
- Go near young animals, even if they seem alone.
- Feed an animal or leave food and trash behind you.
- Let your dog loose near a flock or in bear country.
- Pet a stray animal when traveling.

## Depending on where you are

- **In the Pyrenees**: about forty brown bears lived there in 2019. Aggressive behavior is very rare and is most often limited to a bluff charge.
- **In North America**: do not run, do not climb a tree, keep your pack on. The National Park Service separates two cases if the bear attacks:
  - brown bear or grizzly: play dead, flat on your stomach, hands clasped behind your neck, legs spread, and fight back only if the attack continues;
  - black bear: do not play dead, get to a safe place, otherwise fight back.
- **In the tropics**: see [Tropical forest](../hostile-environments/tropical-forest.md).
