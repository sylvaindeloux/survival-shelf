# Injured far from help

> **In short**: stay calm and first protect everyone from a second accident. Call the emergency services with your exact position, then look after the casualty: stop any bleeding, insulate them from the ground, cover them, talk to them. Do not move them unless there is immediate danger. With no signal, make yourself seen and heard and, if there are several of you, send someone for help.

## When to use it

- A fall, sudden illness, injury or exhaustion on a hike, in the mountains or in the forest, far from a road.
- Rescue will take a long time to arrive, or the phone has no signal.

## What you need

- A phone, a whistle, a light.
- A first-aid kit and a survival blanket.
- Warm clothes, water.
- Paper and a pencil.

## Steps

### Protect

1. **Stop and look** before you go closer: falling rocks, a slope, a drop, water, a thunderstorm.
2. **Get the group out of the way** of the danger, the wind and the cold.
3. **Move the casualty** only if an immediate danger threatens them (see [Move an injured person](../health-and-first-aid/move-an-injured-person.md)).

### Raise the alarm

1. **Dial the emergency number**, even if your phone shows no signal.
2. **Say**:
   - your name and phone number;
   - the exact place: mountain range, trail, altitude, coordinates given by the phone, what can be seen around;
   - what happened and at what time;
   - the number of casualties, their condition and what has already been done;
   - the number of people in the group and your equipment;
   - the weather on the spot: wind, visibility.
3. **Do not hang up first.** After that, stay reachable and do not move from the spot.

### Give first aid

1. **Deal first with what kills**: heavy bleeding, a person who no longer responds or no longer breathes (see [Protect, call for help, give first aid](../health-and-first-aid/protect-call-for-help-give-first-aid.md)).
2. **Insulate the casualty from the ground** with packs, clothes or a rope, and cover them, head included, so that they do not get cold.
3. **Give them nothing to drink or eat** after a fall or a blow.
4. **Support an injured limb** in the position it is in, without straightening it (see [Broken bones and sprains](../health-and-first-aid/broken-bones-and-sprains.md)).
5. **Talk to them, reassure them**, and watch their consciousness and breathing until help arrives.

### With no signal

1. **Dial the emergency number anyway**: the call can go through any operator's network. Move to a high or open spot if you can do it safely.
2. **Signal**: six whistle blasts or six flashes of light in a row, a one-minute pause, then again.
3. **If there are several of you**, someone stays with the casualty and someone goes for help, in daylight, by a known path, with a note giving: the place, the time, the casualty's condition, the number of people.
4. **If you are alone with the casualty**, stay close to them as long as there is a chance of being seen or heard.

### When a helicopter arrives

1. **Raise both arms in a V**: it means "yes, I need help". One arm up and one arm down means "no".
2. **Put away or hold down anything that could blow away.**
3. **Do not approach** until the crew signals you to, and never from the rear.

## Never do this

- Rush to the casualty without checking for danger.
- Straighten a deformed limb, or move a seriously injured person when there is no immediate danger.
- Leave the casualty directly on the ground with nothing over them.
- Go for help at night or across ground you do not know.
- Move after you have given your position to the rescuers.

## Without equipment

- **No survival blanket**: slip anything that insulates under the casualty (packs, branches, clothes) and huddle against them.
- **No whistle**: shout in series, or strike two stones together, always at the same rhythm.

## Depending on where you are

- **In France**: 112 works from any mobile phone. 114 takes text messages from people who cannot speak or hear.
- **In the United Kingdom**: dial 999 or 112 and ask for the police, then for mountain rescue.
- **Abroad**: check before you leave that your insurance covers search and evacuation.
