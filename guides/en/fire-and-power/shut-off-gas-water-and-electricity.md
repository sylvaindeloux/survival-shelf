# Shut off gas, water and electricity

> **In short**: find the gas valve, the main water valve and the main breaker of your home today, and show them to the whole household. Smell of gas: no flame and no switch, get everyone out, and call the gas emergency number or the emergency services from outside. Never touch the electrical panel with wet hands or while standing in water.

## When to use it

- Smell of gas, hissing near a pipe or a gas appliance.
- Water leak, burst pipe, tap water announced as contaminated.
- Flood on its way: shut off gas, electricity and heating if you can do it without putting yourself in danger.
- Evacuation, if the authorities ask for it or if the home is damaged.
- An electrical appliance that smokes or burns.

## What you need

- A battery flashlight, kept near the entrance.
- The wrench or tool needed, if any, stored next to the valve. For gas, a non-sparking wrench.
- Labels to mark each shut-off.

## Steps

### Before: locate

1. **Find the three shut-offs**: the gas valve (at the meter, or on the cylinder), the main water valve (usually where the pipe enters the home, at the meter), the main breaker (on the electrical panel).
2. **Label them** and post the instructions next to them. Check that you can operate them.
3. **Show them to the whole household**, adults and children.

### Gas

1. **No flame, no spark.** Do not touch any switch, electrical appliance or phone inside.
2. **Get everyone out.** Open windows on your way if you can do it at once.
3. **Shut off the gas if you can reach the valve safely**: the valve at the meter usually closes with a quarter turn. With bottled gas, close the valve on the cylinder.
4. **Call from outside**: your gas company's emergency number or the emergency services.
5. **Wait outside** for the technician or the firefighters.
6. **Never turn the gas back on yourself**: only a qualified professional does that.

Shut off the gas only for a reason: a smell, a hissing sound, damage, or an instruction from the authorities. Once it is off, you will need a professional to restore it.

### Electricity

1. **Check that you are dry**: dry hands, feet on a dry floor.
2. **Switch off the individual breakers first, then the main breaker.** With a fuse box, remove the individual fuses, then the main one.
3. **To restore power**: switch the main breaker on first, then the circuits one at a time, and plug appliances back in one after the other. If a breaker trips again at once, do not insist: call an electrician.
4. **After a grid outage**: let the system stabilize, turn the heating back up first, reconnect the fridge and freezer, and wait 10 to 15 minutes before the rest.
5. **Blown fuse**: replace it with a new one of the same rating, never a higher one.

### Water

1. **Close the main water valve** of the home.
2. **If water is running near an outlet or an electrical appliance**, cut the power at the main breaker first, without walking through the water.
3. **Then drain the pipes** if you want to limit damage or collect the water (see [Find water at home](../water/find-water-at-home.md)).
4. **In freezing weather**, insulate exposed pipes and let the taps drip so that they do not freeze.

## Never do this

- Operate a switch, an electrical appliance or a phone in a home that smells of gas.
- Look for a gas leak with a flame.
- Touch the electrical panel with wet hands or while standing on a damp floor.
- Go down into a flooded basement or wade through water to cut the power.
- Go near a downed power line: stay away and report it.
- Turn the power or the gas back on for an empty water heater.

## Without equipment

- No light to find the valves: that is why you locate them beforehand. If you smell gas, do not light anything: get out and call.
- You cannot find or cannot close the gas valve: do not waste time, get out and call.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment**: valves and meters are sometimes in the hallway or in a utility closet. Ask the building manager or the landlord where they are.
- **In a house with a well**: the water cutoff is usually at the well pump.
- **In a flood**: shut off gas, electricity and heating before the water arrives, if you can do it safely, then go upstairs.
- **Before evacuating**: switch off the main breaker if you have time, and the rest if you are told to.
- **In France**: the gas emergency number is 0 800 47 33 33, free of charge.
