# Store fuel safely

> **In short**: store little, in the original container or an approved, tightly closed fuel can, in a cool, ventilated place, away from any flame and out of reach of children. Gasoline is never kept in the home or the basement. Propane cylinders stay outdoors. Check your local rules: allowed quantities differ from one country to the next.

## When to use it

- You keep fuel for a generator, a mower or the car.
- You have gas cylinders or cartridges for a stove, a barbecue or a heater.
- You are building a reserve of wood, denatured alcohol, kerosene or candles.

## What you need

- Approved fuel containers, preferably metal safety cans, with a lid that closes tightly.
- A cool, ventilated place outside the home: a shed or a garage.
- Labels to write the contents and the purchase date.
- A fire extinguisher within reach.

## Steps

1. **Store as little as possible.** Keep only what you really need.
2. **Apply the rules common to all fuels**:
   - away from any open flame, pilot light, stove, heater or electric motor;
   - in a cool, well-ventilated place;
   - in closed, labeled containers, standing upright and stable;
   - out of reach of children;
   - with no smoking nearby.
3. **Store gas cylinders by your local rules.** Two official examples:
   - **Quebec**: no propane cylinder may be stored inside a building, garage and shed included. Stand the cylinder on a solid base so that it cannot fall.
   - **France**: propane cylinders of more than 3 kg stay outside the home, placed so that leaking gas cannot enter it; a butane cylinder of less than 16 kg may be kept indoors; only one spare cylinder of more than 3 kg per room; the valve of any unconnected cylinder stays closed, even if you think it is empty; never near a heat source that can exceed 50 °C (122 °F).
4. **Keep gasoline and diesel out of the home**: never in the house, the apartment or the basement. An approved can, tightly closed, in a cool, ventilated place. Do not leave a can in the trunk of the car.
5. **Keep denatured alcohol, kerosene and liquid firelighters** in their original container, tightly closed, away from the kitchen and the heating.
6. **Store firewood dry and aired**: outdoors, split, stacked on pallets, under a covered, ventilated shelter; indoors, in a dry, ventilated room. Bring logs in 24 to 48 hours before burning them.
7. **Keep candles, matches and lighters** high up, out of reach of children.
8. **Throw oily or fuel-soaked rags** into a closed metal container.
9. **Write the purchase date** on every container and check now and then that nothing leaks.

## Never do this

- Keep gasoline in a drink bottle, a glass jar or any container not made for it.
- Store fuel in the home, the basement or near the furnace or water heater.
- Bring a propane cylinder indoors.
- Keep several spare gas cylinders in the same room.
- Pour gasoline or alcohol near a flame, or use them to light a fire.
- Make someone vomit after swallowing fuel: get medical help right away or call a poison center.

## Without equipment

- No approved fuel can: do not store fuel. Keep the car's tank at least half full instead.
- No place outside the home: settle for candles and firewood, and for the gas your local rules allow indoors.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment**: no gasoline, no propane. Check the building rules too.
- **In a house**: a shed separate from the house is the best place for fuels.
- **Depending on the country**: allowed quantities differ. In Quebec, for example, a garage or shed must not hold more than 30 liters (8 US gallons) of flammable liquids such as gasoline, and 50 liters in total with combustible liquids. Find out your local rule and what your insurance requires.
