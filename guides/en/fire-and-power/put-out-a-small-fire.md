# Put out a small fire

> **In short**: raise the alarm and have someone call the fire department before anything else. Fight the fire only if it is small, limited to one object, with no dangerous smoke, and with an exit behind you. Pan fire: turn off the heat and slide a lid on, never water. If the fire is not out within seconds, get out and close the door behind you.

## When to use it

- A fire has just started: pan, wastebasket, knocked-over candle, electrical appliance.
- Someone's clothes catch fire.
- To know in advance when to give up and get out.

## What you need

- A fire extinguisher that is easy to reach and that the whole household knows how to use. A multipurpose (A-B-C) model covers most home fires.
- A lid within reach whenever you cook.
- A smoke alarm on every floor, near the bedrooms.
- A blanket or a thick towel.

## Steps

1. **Raise the alarm.** Warn everyone in the home and have someone call the fire department. Never fight a fire alone before help has been called.
2. **Decide in two seconds.** When in doubt, do not try: get out. Try to put it out only if all of this is true:
   - the fire is small and limited to one object (a pan, a wastebasket);
   - the smoke does not make it hard to breathe;
   - you have a clear exit behind you;
   - you are physically able to do it. Children and older adults should not.
3. **Pan or deep fryer fire**:
   1. Turn off the burner.
   2. Slide a lid of the right size, a baking sheet or a damp cloth over the pan: with no air, the flames go out.
   3. Do not move the pan, and leave the lid on until everything is cool.
   4. Never throw water on burning oil or grease.
   5. Also turn off the oven and the range hood if the fire is close to them.
4. **Electrical appliance on fire**: cut the power at the main breaker first, then call the fire department.
5. **With an extinguisher** (remember PASS):
   1. Stand with your back to the exit, about 2.5 m (8 ft) from the fire.
   2. **P**ull the pin, with the nozzle pointing away from you.
   3. **A**im low, at the base of the fire.
   4. **S**queeze the lever slowly and evenly.
   5. **S**weep from side to side until the fire seems out.
   6. If the fire is not out after 5 seconds, put the extinguisher down and get out.
6. **Clothes on fire**: the person stops, drops to the ground, covers their face with their hands and rolls over and over until the flames are out. If they cannot, smother the flames with a blanket or a towel.
7. **If the fire resists, get out.** Get everyone out, close the door of the burning room and then the front door, without locking them, and take nothing with you. A closed door holds back fire and smoke.
8. **Even if the fire seems out**, let the firefighters come: it can smolder, in the range hood duct for example. Keep watching the spot.

## Never do this

- Throw water on burning oil or on a live electrical appliance.
- Move a pan or deep fryer that is on fire.
- Go through smoke: where there is smoke, you do not go.
- Take the elevator, jump from a window, or turn back.
- Go back inside to save an object.
- Let the fire get between you and the exit.

## Without equipment

- No extinguisher: a lid for a pan, a blanket for clothes on fire, and nothing else. If that is not enough, get out and close the door.
- No phone: warn the neighbors on your way out and have one of them call.

## Depending on where you are

- **In an apartment, the fire is in your home**: leave by the nearest exit, closing the doors behind you, and call once you are outside.
- **In an apartment, the fire is elsewhere** (smoke in the stairwell, floor below): stay in, close the door, wet it and seal the bottom with wet cloths, then show yourself at the window. If smoke comes in, stay low: the air is easier to breathe near the floor.
- **The fire is above your home**: leave by the nearest exit.
- **At night**: keep a phone by the bed and working smoke alarms.
