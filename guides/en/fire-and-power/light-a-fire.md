# Light a fire

> **In short**: first check that you are allowed to make a fire and that the weather permits: many areas ban open fires near forests or in dry, windy weather. Prepare everything before you light: tinder, kindling, logs, and a bucket of water. Keep the fire small, never leave it, and put it out with water until you can lay your hand on the ashes.

## When to use it

- To warm up, boil water, cook, or signal your position.
- In a fireplace or a wood stove when the heating is off.
- Only if the fire brings more than it risks: in dry, windy weather, give it up.

## What you need

- A lighter or matches, kept dry in a waterproof container.
- Tinder: paper, thin bark, dry grass, wood wool, a commercial firelighter with no petroleum product.
- Dry kindling, then dry split logs.
- A bucket of water and a shovel, ready before you light.

## Steps

### Outdoors

1. **Check the rules and the weather.** Fire rules are local: look for fire bans and restrictions from the land manager, the fire service or the local authority. In France, for example, lighting a fire within 200 m of woods and forests is forbidden unless you own the land, and prefects often ban all fires in strong wind or when fire risk is high.
2. **Choose the spot**: flat ground, out of the wind, open overhead for at least three times the height of the flames, at least 4.5 m (15 ft) from tents, bushes and low branches. Use an existing fire ring when there is one.
3. **Prepare the pit**: clear the ground over a 3 m (10 ft) circle, dig down about 30 cm (1 ft) and ring it with rocks. Never use riverbed rocks or porous or wet stones: they can explode.
4. **Gather three piles before lighting**: tinder, kindling (twigs, finely split wood), larger wood.
5. **Build the fire so that air gets through**: a small cone of twigs around the tinder (the "tepee"), or pieces crossed in a square (the "log cabin"). A fire needs heat, fuel and oxygen.
6. **Light the tinder**, then add kindling little by little, and finally the logs.
7. **Keep the fire small** and never leave it unattended. Keep children and pets at a distance.
8. **To get more warmth**, build the fire in front of a rock or a wall of logs that reflects the heat toward you.
9. **Put it out completely**:
   1. Drown it with plenty of water, until the hissing stops.
   2. Stir the ashes and embers with the shovel and scrape the pieces of wood.
   3. Drown it again.
   4. Hold the back of your hand near the ashes: if it is too hot to touch, it is too hot to leave.

### In a fireplace or a wood stove

1. **Check the appliance**: chimney swept within the year, air inlets open.
2. **Build the fire upside down**: split logs at the bottom, spaced apart, the smaller ones above, then kindling and the firelighter at the very top.
3. **Light it from the top.** The fire works its way down, with less smoke.
4. **Reduce the air intake** once the fire is burning well, without ever closing it completely, even at night.
5. **Burn only dry, untreated wood** (dried for at least 18 months): no painted or varnished wood, panels, plastic or magazines.

## Never do this

- Use gasoline or alcohol to light or revive a fire or a barbecue.
- Make a fire in strong wind, under trees or near dry grass.
- Leave a fire that is still smoking, or put it out with dirt or sand alone.
- Burn aerosol cans, batteries, glass, aluminum or trash.
- Make a fire or keep embers in a closed room, outside an appliance designed for it: deadly risk of carbon monoxide.

## Without equipment

- **No tinder**: shave thin curls from dry wood, collect thin bark, dry grass and the finest dead twigs.
- **No water to put it out**: do not make a fire.
- **No lighter and no matches**: friction methods take a lot of practice. Put a lighter and matches in your go-bag now.

## Depending on where you are

- **In a forest, in the fire season**: many authorities then ban all fires, even for landowners. You are liable for a fire that escapes.
- **In a campground or a park**: use the fire rings provided and follow the posted rules.
- **If the fire escapes**: move away and call the emergency services at once.
