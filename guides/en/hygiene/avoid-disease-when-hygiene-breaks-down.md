# Avoid disease when hygiene breaks down

> **In short**: after a disaster, diseases spread mostly through water, hands, food and human waste. Drink only safe water, wash your hands at the key moments, eat safe food, keep human waste and trash away, and clean up without putting yourself at risk. If someone has diarrhea or is vomiting, they have their own towel and cutlery and do not cook for others.

## When to use it

- Long water outage, tap water declared unsafe, flood, sewers out of service.
- Many people living in little space: emergency shelter, family gathered in one home.
- Someone in the household is ill: diarrhea, vomiting, fever.

## Steps

1. **Drink only safe water**: bottled, boiled or disinfected. Use it also to brush your teeth, prepare baby food, wash raw fruit and vegetables and make ice. After a flood, ask your local authority whether tap water is safe; well water is not.
2. **Wash your hands** with soap and water before cooking and eating, after the toilet, a diaper change, cleaning up or contact with flood water (see [Wash your hands without running water](wash-your-hands-without-running-water.md)).
3. **Eat safely**: throw away food that touched flood water, and food left in a fridge or freezer that stopped working. Wash dishes and worktops before cooking.
4. **Keep human waste away**: if the toilet no longer flushes, set up an emergency toilet and store the bags away from everything (see [Set up an emergency toilet](set-up-an-emergency-toilet.md)).
5. **Seal up the trash** and empty containers where water stands: they attract flies and rats and breed mosquitoes (see [Handle household trash](handle-household-trash.md)).
6. **Avoid contact with flood water**: it may hold sewage, chemicals and sharp objects. After contact, wash with clean water and soap.
7. **Protect your wounds**: waterproof bandage, no contact with doubtful water, and a doctor if they turn red, swollen or start oozing.
8. **Air** the home every day.

### Cleaning up after a flood

1. Wear gloves, a mask and boots. Open the windows.
2. Remove water and mud, then scrub floors, walls and objects with water and detergent.
3. Disinfect with household bleach: 1 cup (240 mL) per gallon (3.8 L) of water for surfaces, according to the CDC. Follow the label of your product.
4. Wash soiled clothes in hot water with detergent, separately from the rest of the laundry.
5. Disinfect toys before giving them back to children.
6. Wash yourself thoroughly with soap and water when you have finished.

### When someone is ill

- They rest and drink plenty (see [Dehydration and diarrhea](../health-and-first-aid/dehydration-and-diarrhea.md)).
- They have their own towel and cutlery, and do not prepare food for others.
- Everyone washes their hands often, with soap and water.
- Clean toilets, flush handles, taps, door handles and surfaces every day.
- Wash their soiled clothes and bedding separately, on a hot wash.
- They stay away from others until 2 days after the diarrhea or vomiting has stopped.
- If they cough or have a fever: they wear a mask and use paper tissues.

## Never do this

- Drink tap water after a flood without knowing whether it is safe.
- Mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaner, or use it in a closed room.
- Let children play in flood water or with toys that have been in it.
- Bathe or wash in contaminated water.
- Keep food that touched flood water "just in case".

## Depending on where you are

- **Group shelter**: set up one handwashing station next to the toilets and another where people eat.
- **With a baby**: disinfect the surface after each diaper change and wash your hands (see [Manage periods and diapers](manage-periods-and-diapers.md)).
- **Tetanus**: check that everyone's vaccination is up to date (see [Wounds and infections](../health-and-first-aid/wounds-and-infections.md)).
