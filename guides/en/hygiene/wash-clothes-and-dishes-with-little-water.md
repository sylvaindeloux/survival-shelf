# Wash clothes and dishes with little water

> **In short**: dishes in three stages, in basins: wash in hot soapy water, rinse in clean water, air dry. Sanitize them if they touched flood water. Laundry: wash anything soiled separately, in hot water with detergent, and dry everything completely.

## When to use it

- Water or power outage: no washing machine, no dishwasher.
- Tap water declared unsafe.
- After a flood: dishes, laundry and worktops may be contaminated.

## What you need

- Two or three basins, or buckets.
- Dish soap, laundry detergent or soap.
- A sponge or a brush, clean dish towels.
- Unscented household bleach for sanitizing if needed.
- A clothesline and clothespins.
- A way to heat water (see [Use a stove or a generator without poisoning yourself](../fire-and-power/use-a-stove-or-a-generator-without-poisoning-yourself.md)).

## Steps

### Dishes

1. **Scrape the plates** into the trash before washing: fewer scraps, less water needed.
2. **Wash** in a first basin of hot soapy water, from the least dirty to the dirtiest: glasses, cutlery, plates, pans.
3. **Rinse** in a second basin of clean water.
4. **Let everything air dry completely**, on a rack or a clean dish towel.
5. **Throw the dirty water away** far from the water reserve. Rinse water can be used to flush the toilet.

### When you need to sanitize

If the dishes touched flood water:

1. Wash them first in hot soapy water, then rinse them in clean, safe water.
2. Soak them, fully covered, for 1 minute in a bleach solution: 1 cup (240 mL) of unscented household bleach in 5 gallons (about 19 liters) of clean water.
3. Let them air dry.
4. Throw away wooden cutting boards, baby bottle nipples and pacifiers that touched flood water.

Do the same for worktops, with a cloth soaked in the solution.

### Laundry

1. **Sort**: set apart anything soiled by stools, vomit or flood water, and the clothes you wore for cleaning up.
2. **Soak** the laundry in a basin of water with detergent or soap.
3. **Scrub**, above all the dirty areas, then wring out.
4. **Rinse** in a basin of clean water, and wring out again.
5. **Soiled laundry**: wash it separately, in hot water with detergent, before wearing it again. Wash your hands afterward.
6. **Hang it up and dry it completely**, on a line, in the open air. Cloth worn against the skin that stays damp or is badly washed can cause rashes and infections.

### Which water to use

- **Tap water under a boil-water advisory**: it is usually fine for laundry, and for dishes washed with detergent then dried well. Follow local advice.
- **Do-not-use advisory**: bottled water only.

## Never do this

- Mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaner, or use it in a closed room.
- Use scented bleach or bleach with added cleaners on dishes.
- Wash soiled laundry with the rest.
- Put away laundry or dishes that are still damp.
- Wash your dishes or clothes directly in a stream or a lake.

## Without equipment

- **No basin**: a bucket, a large pot, a plugged sink.
- **No detergent**: soap.

## Depending on where you are

- **In the wild**: wash at least 60 m (200 ft) from streams and lakes, with very little biodegradable soap. Strain the dishwater to catch food scraps, pack them out, then scatter the water on the ground.
- **With a sick person**: they do not share their cutlery or towel, and their soiled laundry is washed separately, on a hot wash.
