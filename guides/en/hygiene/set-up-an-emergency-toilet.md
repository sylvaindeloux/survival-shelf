# Set up an emergency toilet

> **In short**: if the water is off but the sewers work, pour non-drinking water into the bowl to flush. If the sewers or the septic tank are out of service, stop flushing: line the bowl or a bucket with two trash bags, cover each use with cat litter or sawdust, then close the bags and store them away from everything. Wash your hands every time.

## When to use it

- Water outage: the tank no longer fills.
- Sewers damaged after an earthquake or a flood, flooded septic tank: the flush must not be used.
- Badly handled human waste spreads serious diseases. This is a priority, not a matter of comfort.

## What you need

- Two sturdy buckets of about 20 liters (5 gallons), with lids. A toilet seat on top makes a bucket more comfortable.
- Heavy-duty trash bags.
- A dry, absorbent material: cat litter, sawdust, wood chips, shredded paper, dry leaves, straw.
- Toilet paper, gloves.
- Something to wash hands with: soap and water, or hand sanitizer.
- An outdoor trash can with a tight lid, to store the bags.

## Steps

### The sewers work, but there is no tap water

1. Keep non-drinking water for this, in large containers: rainwater, used washing water.
2. Pour it into the bowl to flush.

### The sewers or the septic tank no longer work

1. **First look for a working toilet** elsewhere: neighbors, a shelter.
2. **Prepare the home toilet**: close the water valve, empty the bowl, then line it with a plastic bag inside a heavy-duty trash bag. Or do the same in a bucket.
3. **Pour a layer of absorbent material** into the bottom.
4. **After each use**, cover with dry material. Toilet paper goes in the bag.
5. **Fill the bag no more than halfway.** Close it, put it into a second bag, tie it.
6. **Store the bags** in a closed trash can, outdoors, away from food and water, apart from other trash, out of reach of children, pets, flies and rats.
7. **Wait for instructions** from your local authority or waste service before disposing of them.
8. **Wash your hands** after each use and each time you handle a bag.
9. **Disinfect the bucket** with bleach when you stop using it.

### Better: two buckets

Keeping urine apart from feces reduces volume and smell.

- **"Pee" bucket**: no bag. Empty it often, diluted with non-drinking water if you can, onto a lawn or bare ground, then rinse it.
- **"Poo" bucket**: with two bags and dry material, as above.

## Never do this

- Flush when the sewers are out of service or the septic tank is flooded.
- Leave human waste in the open, or dump it in a stream, a ditch or a gutter.
- Store the bags near food or the water reserve.
- Bury human waste in the vegetable garden.
- Forget to wash your hands.

## Without equipment

- **No cat litter or sawdust**: shredded paper or cardboard, dry leaves, dry grass.
- **No bucket**: the toilet bowl, emptied and lined with two bags.

## Depending on where you are

- **House with land, if nothing else is possible**: dig a hole 75 to 90 cm (2.5 to 3 ft) deep, at least 60 m (200 ft) from any water source and any home, outside the vegetable garden. Empty the waste in without the bag, cover with soil each time, and lay boards over the hole to keep animals and children out. Stop 30 cm (1 ft) below the surface, fill it in and pack the soil down.
- **In the wild, for one use**: dig a hole 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in) deep, at least 60 m (200 ft) from water, camp and trails, then fill it in. Pack out toilet paper and menstrual products.
- **Apartment**: store the bags in a trash can with a tight lid, outdoors if you have a balcony, away from the kitchen and the water reserve.
- **Septic tank after a flood**: do not use it while the ground around it is flooded or saturated, and do not have it pumped during that time. If you suspect damage, have it inspected by a professional.
