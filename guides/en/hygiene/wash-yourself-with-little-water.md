# Wash yourself with little water

> **In short**: with a washcloth, soap and a basin, a few liters are enough. Start with the face, finish with the feet and groin. Hands and teeth come before everything else: brush your teeth only with water that is safe to drink. Never wash in flood water or water fouled by sewage.

## When to use it

- Water outage, tap water declared unsafe, a reserve to save, life in an emergency shelter or outdoors.

## What you need

- Water: allow 2 to 6 liters (about 0.5 to 1.5 gallons) per person per day for basic hygiene.
- Soap, a washcloth or a clean cloth, one towel per person.
- A basin or a bucket.
- Wipes, useful when water is really short.
- A toothbrush, toothpaste and safe drinking water.

## Steps

1. **Wash your hands first** (see [Wash your hands without running water](wash-your-hands-without-running-water.md)).
2. **Prepare a basin of water**, warm if you can. In the sun, water warms up by itself in a dark container.
3. **Wash with the cloth, from the top down**, from the cleanest areas to the dirtiest: face, neck, arms, armpits, chest, back, legs, then feet and groin last.
4. **Rinse** with the cloth wrung out in a little clean water.
5. **Dry yourself well.** Everyone keeps their own washcloth and towel, and dries them between washes.
6. **Brush your teeth with safe drinking water**: bottled, boiled or disinfected.

### Which water to wash with

- **Tap water under a boil-water advisory**: you can usually shower or wash without boiling it, as long as you do not swallow it or get it in your eyes. For young children, a sponge bath is better. Follow local advice.
- **Do-not-use advisory**: bottled water for everything.
- **Rainwater or water found outdoors**: only if it is clear and free of any risk of chemical pollution, without swallowing it, and never on a wound.
- **Teeth, wounds, baby bottles**: always safe drinking water.

## Never do this

- Wash or bathe in flood water, or in a river or lake that may have received sewage or chemicals.
- Brush your teeth with doubtful water.
- Wet a wound with water that is not safe to drink.
- Share your washcloth or towel.

## Without equipment

- **Very little water**: wipes, or a damp cloth, in the same order, from the face to the feet.
- **No washcloth**: a clean folded cloth.

## Depending on where you are

- **Group shelter**: make a private corner with a tarp or a hung blanket, or set a room aside for washing.
- **In the wild**: wash at least 60 m (200 ft) from streams and lakes, with very little biodegradable soap, and scatter the dirty water on the ground.
- **After contact with flood water**: wash as soon as possible with clean water and soap.
