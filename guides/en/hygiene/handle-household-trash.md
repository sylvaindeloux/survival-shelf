# Handle household trash

> **In short**: when trash is no longer collected, it attracts flies, rats and mosquitoes. Make less of it, close it in strong bags and lidded cans, and store it outdoors, away from the home, water and food. Do not burn plastic, set hazardous items apart, and follow your local authority's instructions as soon as there are some.

## When to use it

- Collection interrupted for several days or weeks.
- After a flood, a storm or an earthquake.
- **The risks**: garbage attracts flies, rats, dogs and snakes, especially in hot weather. Rainwater pooling in trash breeds mosquitoes. Rain running over it can contaminate water. Piles of trash block drainage and can catch fire.

## What you need

- Heavy-duty trash bags.
- Trash cans or bins made of thick plastic or metal, with tight lids.
- Gloves.
- A shovel, if you have land.

## Steps

1. **Make less trash** and reuse what can be reused.
2. **Sort into four**:
   - food scraps and anything that rots;
   - clean, dry packaging, which can wait a long time;
   - soiled items: diapers, menstrual products, bags from the emergency toilet;
   - hazardous items: broken glass, needles, chemicals, medicines.
3. **Seal what rots and what is soiled** in strong bags, tied tightly, then in a can with a lid.
4. **Store the cans outdoors**, away from the home, food, the water reserve and any water source, out of reach of children and animals.
5. **Leave nothing lying around**: no bags on the ground, no open containers where rainwater can pool.
6. **Wash the cans** regularly with soap and water.
7. **Wash your hands** after handling trash.
8. **Follow your local authority's instructions**: drop-off points, when collection resumes.

### If you have land and it lasts

- Bury food scraps in a pit, far from any water source.
- Cover each layer of waste with about 10 cm (4 in) of soil or ash.
- Build a small mound around it to keep runoff water out.
- When the pit is full, fill it in with at least 50 cm (20 in) of soil.

### Keeping rats away

- Keep food, pet food included, in thick plastic, metal or glass containers with tight lids.
- Clean up spills right away, wash dishes without waiting, leave no pet bowl out overnight.
- Seal holes: a mouse gets through an opening the width of a pencil (6 mm or 1/4 in).
- **Rodent droppings or nest**: air the place for 30 minutes, wear gloves, do not sweep or vacuum. Wet with disinfectant or diluted bleach (1 part bleach to 9 parts water), let it soak for 5 minutes, pick up with paper towels, throw into a covered trash can. Wash your gloves before taking them off, then your hands.

## Never do this

- Burn plastics or chemicals: the smoke is toxic.
- Dump trash in a stream, a ditch or a gutter.
- Sweep or vacuum rodent droppings dry.
- Mix bleach with another product.
- Touch a dead animal with bare hands.

## Depending on where you are

- **During a flood**: close the trash cans and put them in a cupboard, so that they do not float around the home.
- **Dead animal**: wearing gloves, put it in plastic bags, move it away from the home and tell your local authority. A dead rodent goes in a sealed bag inside a second sealed bag, then into a covered trash can.
- **Rats and mice fleeing the water**: do not touch them.
- **Apartment**: keep what rots double-bagged in a closed bin, and take it to the drop-off point as soon as one is announced.
- **In the wild**: pack out all your trash. Do not burn it or bury it.
