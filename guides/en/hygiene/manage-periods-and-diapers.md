# Manage periods and diapers

> **In short**: keep a reserve of menstrual products and diapers in advance, with something to throw them away in and to wash your hands. Change regularly, wash your hands before and after, and flush nothing down the toilet. A reusable product must be washed with soap and dried completely before it is used again. For a baby: wipe from front to back, disinfect the surface, wash their hands and yours.

## When to use it

- Water outage, stores closed, evacuation, group shelter.
- Periods usually last 2 to 7 days: build the reserve before the crisis.

## What you need

- **Periods**: your usual products for at least one cycle (allow 12 to 22 disposable pads), or reusable products: washable pads (at least 5), a menstrual cup.
- **Baby**: diapers, wipes or clean squares of cloth, something to cover the changing surface, a disinfectant.
- **For everyone**: soap, hand sanitizer, opaque bags that close, a trash can with a lid, a basin kept for washing reusable products, a clothesline.

## Steps

### Periods

1. **Wash your hands** before and after each change, with soap and water. Failing that, with hand sanitizer.
2. **Change regularly**:
   - disposable pad: every 8 hours at the latest;
   - tampon: every 8 hours at the latest, with the lowest absorbency that works for you. Wearing one for too long raises the risk of toxic shock;
   - washable pad: about every 3 to 6 hours;
   - folded cloth: about every 2 to 4 hours;
   - menstrual cup: empty it every 6 to 12 hours.
3. **Throw disposable products** into a closed bag, then into a trash can with a lid. Never down the toilet: they block it.
4. **Wash reusable products** in a basin kept for that, with clean water and soap, then dry them completely, on a line, in a place where you have privacy. Damp or badly washed cloth can cause rashes and infections.
5. **Menstrual cup**: rinse it with clean water when you empty it, and boil it for 5 to 10 minutes at the end of each cycle. It needs far less water than washable products.
6. **Wash your intimate area once a day** if you can, with clean water.

### Changing a baby

1. **Prepare**: cover the surface with a disposable liner if possible, get out a clean diaper, wipes and a bag for dirty clothes. Always keep one hand on the child.
2. **Clean the child** from front to back with wipes or a clean damp cloth. Put soiled clothes in the bag.
3. **Throw away** the wipes inside the dirty diaper, close it and put it in a trash can with a lid.
4. **Put on the clean diaper.**
5. **Wash the child's hands** with soap and water. Otherwise, with hand sanitizer, keeping their hands away from their mouth and eyes until it is dry.
6. **Clean up**: throw away the liner, wipe off any visible soil, then wet the whole surface with disinfectant, following its instructions.
7. **Wash your hands** with soap and water. If you only have sanitizer, wash with soap as soon as possible.

## Never do this

- Flush pads, tampons, wipes or diapers down the toilet.
- Keep a tampon in for more than 8 hours.
- Reuse a washable product that is still damp.
- Burn disposable products or diapers: they give off toxic smoke.

## Without equipment

- **No products left**: a clean cloth, folded into several layers, changed every 2 to 4 hours, washed with soap and dried completely.
- **No disposable diapers left**: squares of clean cloth, washed separately in hot water with detergent, and dried completely.

## Depending on where you are

- **Group shelter**: ask for a closed place to change, at night too, and somewhere to dry your products out of sight.
- **In the wild**: pack out used products and diapers in a closed bag. Do not bury or burn them.
- **In your go-bag**: a few products, a bag that closes, hand sanitizer.
